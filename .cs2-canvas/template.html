Calendar setup A: two questions, one screen each

Calendar setup

Syncing a calendar lets this week's Action Items sit between a person's own events, after two quick questions about their day.

Image

Your Set your boundaries screen: the preferred time frame with the suggested range from the person's calendar. Crop it so the range and the hours are readable at this size.

Image

Your When do you focus best screen, with Mornings, Mid-day, Evenings, Late night and No preference all visible and one tile selected.

People's weeks look nothing alike, and a plan that ignores that feels like a school timetable, so setup asks two things a calendar can't say. First, when a person prefers to work, since a free hour at 6am isn't time most people would use. Mondai suggests a range from their calendar and they adjust it. It needs at least eight hours, so a busy day still has open time.

Second, when they focus best, because demanding work tends to go better at a person's peak hours. Mondai uses both answers to decide which gaps Action Items fill first. They are preferences, not limits, because the evidence on peak hours is real but modest and plenty of days don't go as planned.

Source: May, Hasher and Healey 2023

The schedule view

The schedule view

The same week can also be laid out by time, so a person sees where each Action Item fits in their day and can flip back to the list whenever they like.

Image

Aisha's week as a calendar, opening at 9AM, the start time she set in setup, so the top rows are already full. Her own events and two or three Action Items sit side by side, one block clearly longer than another. The List and Schedule switch is visible at the top right.

Image

The click, as a short animation. A block opens a popover with Go to action item, Mark as complete and Reschedule, then the reschedule menu opens. Last frame: a passed time dropped off the card, the item still open.

Each Action Item gets a time because naming a time works better than naming a day. In a flu shot study, a date alone barely moved turnout, while a date and a time did.

The time stays a suggestion. People underestimate their own work, so Saturday night is still the only deadline.

We kept the sync one way. Mondai reads the calendar to find gaps between events and never writes anything back, because a suggestion written into a person's calendar would look like a commitment they never made.

Sources: Milkman, Beshears, Choi, Laibson and Madrian 2011; Buehler, Griffin and Ross 1994

Rollover

Rollover

So what happens to the Action Items someone doesn't get to by Saturday night? They have to go somewhere.

Image

One Action Item card shown twice, side by side. Left: Saturday night at the bottom of this week. Right: Sunday morning at the top of next week with the soft magenta carried tag. Next week still shows four Action Items, so the leftover took a slot instead of adding one.

A missed step on a long goal makes people more likely to give up, so where leftovers go is a motivation question. Pushing it all forward makes next week heavier than the hours the person gave. Asking them to sort it hands them a pile of decisions while they are behind. Dropping it lets the Pathway slip quietly.

So leftovers move into next week on their own and count first against the same hours. Carried items are marked in soft magenta instead of a warning color, because showing a lapse lowers later engagement. Pace updates once a week. A run of hard weeks is a different problem, which the plan handles when it rechecks the hours a person gave.

Sources: Rai, Sharif, Chang, Milkman and Duckworth 2022; Silverman and Barasch 2023
