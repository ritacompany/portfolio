# Section 1, message plan

## Block 1

- Claim: The route was already in the product, but nothing told a person where to start or what came next.
- What the reader has to see to believe it: the route as people met it, with stages laid out and no first step, no next step and no sense of time.
- Does the text alone make them see it? No. The body says nothing marked the way through, but an absence is only believable when the reader looks at the screen and finds nothing there to act on. The quiz and calendar labels stay as text; naming them is enough to show the pieces sat apart.
- The one thing to show: the route screen as it stood before the framework, a real Mondai screen he picks. It has to show the whole route at once with no start marker, no "this week" and no next action.
- Second figure: none. The other half of the claim, that each piece was designed on its own, is carried by the labels and the body.
- Figure count: 1. The route screen proves "nothing said how to move through it".

## Block 2

- Claim: The framework had two jobs that pull against each other: hold a fixed order and bend around a real week.
- What the reader has to see to believe it: the open questions that fall out of doing both, each one a place where order and life collide.
- Does the text alone make them see it? Yes. The four numbered cards are the questions themselves, and the body names both jobs. A picture would only restate them. Text only; cards stay as text.
- Figure count: 0.

## Block 3

- Claim: Each nearby product solved one half of the problem and none solved both.
- What the reader has to see to believe it: which half each product kept, and what was taken from it and what was left.
- Does the text alone make them see it? Yes. Each card names the product, the half it solved and what was kept and left. Showing other companies' screens adds nothing the cards do not already state. Text only; cards stay as text.
- Figure count: 0.
