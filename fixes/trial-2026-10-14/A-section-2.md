# Section 2 message plan

## Block 1: The unit of time

- Claim: the week is the right unit of time for these people, over a per day count and a calendar led plan.
- What the reader has to see: the same goal planned three ways, with only the week leaving room inside and a hard edge at the end.
- Do headline and body make them see it? No. The body names the risk of irregular weeks and the need for pressure, but the per day and calendar led options exist only as card text, so the reader never sees what was given up.
- The one thing to show: a new figure, three identical frames side by side holding one goal's work. Frame 01 splits it into a count per day worked back from a goal date. Frame 02 pins it to fixed calendar blocks. Frame 03 holds it loose inside one week band with a marked edge. Cards 01 to 03 sit under their frames. Same scale in every frame, so the only difference is the unit.
- Figure count: 1. It proves the comparison the headline rests on. The body adds no part a second figure would need to carry.

## Block 2: Due Saturday night

- Claim: work is due at the end of the week and free inside it.
- What the reader has to see: a week with one fixed deadline on Saturday night and items floating inside, one of them sitting in a suggested slot.
- Do headline and body make them see it? No. "Pressure at the edge, freedom inside" is abstract until the reader sees the edge drawn and an item placed but not pinned.
- The one thing to show: a real Mondai week screen with a calendar connected, which he picks, labelled This week, Suggested slot and Due Saturday night directly on the screen.
- Figure count: 1. It proves both halves of the headline in one view: the Saturday night line is the edge, the suggested slot is the freedom. The no calendar list state and the missed slot point are body detail, not part of the claim, so no second figure. The research line stays a caption.

## Block 3: Locks

- Claim: the week line that sets deadlines also sets what is locked.
- What the reader has to see: the first proposal locking everything not yet available, against the shipped rule locking only what is past this week.
- Do headline and body make them see it? No. The body says the lock was cut back to the week but the reader cannot picture how much was freed.
- The one thing to show: a new figure, one row of Action Items running across the five Pathways with the week marked. The first proposal's locked region is drawn as a faint outline over everything after the first available item; the shipped locked region is solid and starts only at the week's edge. Only the difference is loud. Cards 01 to 03 annotate the three zones: past this week, inside this week, the order.
- Figure count: 1. It proves "the same line" by placing the lock boundary on the week edge the reader just saw in block 2.

## Block 4: One level up

- Claim: the product opens on the week with exactly one level above it, the current Pathway and whether they are ahead or behind.
- What the reader has to see: the opening screen with the week and the current Pathway status, and that the whole journey level is not on it.
- Do headline and body make them see it? No. The reader has not seen what the product opens on.
- The one thing to show: a real Mondai opening screen, which he picks, labelled This week and Current Pathway.
- Second figure: a screen cannot show what was left off, so "one level and no more" stays unproven. A new figure of three nested containers, no arrows: This week inside Current Pathway inside Whole journey. The whole journey container is dimmed and its pace and target struck out, labelled Whole journey.
- Figure count: 2. The screen proves what the product opens on. The nested levels prove the limit of one level and the cut journey pace.

## Block 5: For looking

- Claim: the whole journey has its own pages and nothing can be checked off there.
- What the reader has to see: that the journey pages are for looking only, and why that cost was accepted.
- Do headline and body make them see it? Yes. The headline states the rule plainly and the body carries the trade off and the reason. A screen could only show an absence of checkboxes, which proves nothing a reader doubts. Text only, and a pause after two figures.
- Figure count: 0.

## Block 6: Built for each person

- Claim: the AI builds each person's Action Items from their onboarding answers.
- What the reader has to see: onboarding answers going in and a personal set of Action Items coming out.
- Do headline and body make them see it? No. "Built from onboarding answers" is a claim about a transformation the reader has not seen.
- The one thing to show: a new figure, a two part instance. Left, a few onboarding answers from one person. Right, the week of Action Items made from them. One labelled link between them: AI builds. He supplies the real answers and items from a Mondai screen of his pick, or approves invented placeholder content marked as such.
- Figure count: 1. It proves the input to output step. The body's last line hands off to the next section and needs no figure.
