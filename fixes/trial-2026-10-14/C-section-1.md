## Storyboard (eyebrows, headlines and pictures only)

1. **What was already there.** "The route existed, but nothing said how to move through it."
   Picture: one annotated product view. The route sits in the middle, five Pathways in their fixed
   order, with the onboarding quiz at one edge and the planned calendar connection at the other, each
   labelled in place. Nothing is drawn between them: no start marker, no arrow, no "next". The single
   thing it shows is that every piece is present and no way through them is. The headline alone names
   the gap but the reader has never seen the route, so without the picture "the route" is an abstraction
   and "nothing said how" is taken on trust.

2. **Two jobs at once.** "It had to keep the order and still fit around a life."
   Picture: the same five Pathways in the same fixed order, now with the four numbered questions pinned
   to the points where a real week pushes on it (how much fits in one week, falling behind or getting
   ahead, no calendar, stepping away and coming back). The quiz and calendar labels from picture 1 are
   gone. The single thing it shows is where order and a life collide. The headline states the two jobs
   but not that they leave open questions, and the questions only read as consequences of the order when
   they sit on it.

3. **The closest products.** "The closest products each solved half of it."
   Picture: three equal panels side by side, one per product (CareerFoundry, Duolingo, Notion and
   Todoist), each a screen that shows the one behaviour named in its label, with what was kept and what
   was left written on the panel. The single thing it shows is that each product holds one half, structure
   or room to move, and none holds both. One picture cannot do it: the point is a parallel comparison of
   three separate products, and drawing them in one frame would invent a placement (a spectrum or a
   matrix) that no source supports. Three identical frames keep the comparison honest.

Strip check, top to bottom. The headlines read as a chain: the pieces existed, but the order had to fit
a life, and nothing outside solved both. Picture 1 to picture 2 changes one thing: the surrounding pieces
are swapped for the questions on the same route. Picture 2 to picture 3 changes subject from the route to
the products outside it, which is a new beat, so the frame changes with it. No picture repeats a point
already landed: the route appears twice, but the first shows it unconnected and the second shows it under
pressure. The career products named in block 3's body get no picture, because the headline already carries
"each solved half" and a fourth or fifth panel would repeat it.
