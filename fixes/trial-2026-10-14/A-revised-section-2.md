# Section 2 message plan

## Block 1, The unit of time

- Claim: The week became the unit of scheduling because the people this is for don't live on a calendar,
  and a week still gives them a little pressure.
- What the reader has to see to believe it: the three units that were on the table side by side, each with
  the reason it lost or won.
- Does the text already make them see it? Yes. The three cards set as plain text already line the options
  up with one verdict each, and the body gives the two forces (irregular weeks, need for pressure). Text
  only. The cards stay as text.
- Figure count: 0.

## Block 2, Due Saturday night

- Claim: The week puts pressure at its edge and leaves freedom inside it.
- What the reader has to see to believe it: one week where items sit loosely across the days, some with a
  suggested slot, and all of them share a single deadline on Saturday night.
- Does the text already make them see it? No. "This week", "Suggested slot" and "Due Saturday night" as
  plain text are three phrases; the claim is about where the pressure sits in time, and only a picture of
  the week shows a soft inside and a hard edge.
- The one thing to show: the week as a person sees it with a calendar connected, items carrying suggested
  slots and the Saturday night deadline marked. Source: a real Mondai screen he will pick.
- Second figure: none. The no calendar state ("the week is a list") and the missed slot rule are stated in
  the body and are details of the freedom half, which the first figure already shows.
- Figure count: 1. The week screen proves both halves of the claim at once: freedom inside (loose,
  optional slots) and pressure at the edge (one shared deadline).
- The research caption stays as a caption under the body.

## Block 3, Locks

- Claim: The line that ends the week is the same line that decides what gets locked.
- What the reader has to see to believe it: that the lock boundary and the week boundary are one line, and
  that it moved there from a stricter first proposal.
- Does the text already make them see it? No. The three cards read as a rule list; they don't show that
  the week edge and the lock edge coincide, and the "First proposal" label means nothing without
  something drawn to point at.
- The one thing to show: a new figure described in words. A strip of Action Items running left to right
  in order, with the end of this week drawn as one vertical line. Items left of it are open and movable,
  items right of it are locked. A fainter earlier line, labelled First proposal, sits at the first item
  not yet available, showing where locking began before it was cut back to the week line. The three cards
  sit on the figure beside the zones they describe.
- Second figure: none. Both named parts (same line, cut back from the first proposal) are in the one
  strip.
- Figure count: 1. The strip proves "the same line": the lock edge is drawn on the week edge.

## Block 4, One level up

- Claim: The product opens on the week, with only one level above it: the current Pathway and whether the
  person is ahead or behind.
- What the reader has to see to believe it: the opening screen, with this week in front and the current
  Pathway status above it, and no whole journey level.
- Does the text already make them see it? No. The hierarchy is a screen fact; labels as plain text can't
  show what is in front, what sits above it and what is absent.
- The one thing to show: the opening screen of the product, labelled This week and Current Pathway, with
  the Whole journey label pointing at where that level is not. Source: a real Mondai screen he will pick.
- Second figure: none. The cut journey level is an absence; the first figure shows it missing and the body
  says why it was cut.
- Figure count: 1. The opening screen proves "opens on the week, with one level above it".

## Block 5, For looking

- Claim: The whole journey lives on its own pages, and nothing can be checked off there.
- What the reader has to see to believe it: a Pathway page with its Action Items listed and no way to mark
  any of them done.
- Does the text already make them see it? No. "Nothing can be checked off" is believed by seeing a page of
  items with no checkboxes; stated alone it reads as a claim about intent.
- The one thing to show: a Pathway page as a person sees it. Source: a real Mondai screen he will pick.
- Second figure: none. The contrast with the week, where work gets done, was shown in block 4 and is
  stated in the body.
- Figure count: 1. The Pathway page proves "nothing can be checked off there".

## Block 6, Built for each person

- Claim: The AI builds each person's Action Items from their onboarding answers.
- What the reader has to see to believe it: one onboarding answer next to the Action Items it produced, so
  the link from answer to week is visible.
- Does the text already make them see it? No. "Built for each person" stated alone is a promise; seeing an
  answer turn into specific items makes it a fact.
- The one thing to show: one frame pairing an onboarding answer with the Action Items it produced for that
  person, both taken from real Mondai screens he will pick.
- Second figure: none. The pairing proves the whole claim. The open question in the body (how much time
  someone has) is a bridge to the next section and needs no figure here.
- Figure count: 1. The pairing proves "from their onboarding answers".
