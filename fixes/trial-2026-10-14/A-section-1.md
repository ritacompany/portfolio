Section: 1, message plan (pass 1)

## Block 1

Claim: The Growth Journey was already built as a route of five Pathways in a fixed order, but none of the
pieces around it said what a person sees first or what comes next.

What the reader has to see to believe it: the route and the pieces beside it as they really were, sitting
next to each other with nothing leading from one to the next.

Do the headline and body already make them see it? No. The body tells them the pieces existed and were
designed on their own, but a stranger has never seen any of them. "Nothing said how to move through it" is
an absence, and an absence only lands when the reader can look at the pieces and find no way in.

The one thing to show: the three pieces in one figure, from real Mondai screens he picks. The Growth
Journey with its five Pathways in order, the onboarding quiz and the planned calendar connection, set side
by side at the same size. No arrows, no numbering, no start marker between them. Each carries its label
from the copy on the figure itself ("The route. A Growth Journey of five Pathways, stages that run in a
fixed order." / "Onboarding quiz." / "Calendar connection, planned."). If the calendar connection has no
design to pick, its place is an empty labelled outline, which says "planned" by itself.

A second figure? No. The one figure carries both parts of the claim: the route screen proves the route
existed, and the missing marks between the pieces prove nothing said how to move through them.

Figure count: 1. The figure proves the route existed and that nothing connected it to the pieces around it.

## Block 2

Claim: The framework had to hold the Pathways' fixed order and bend to an uneven life at the same time.

What the reader has to see to believe it: that holding both at once leaves real questions with no answer.

Do the headline and body already make them see it? Yes. The four numbered questions are the evidence.
Each is a concrete case where the fixed order and a real life collide (how much fits in a week, falling
behind or getting ahead, not planning in a calendar, stepping away and coming back). Read in a list they
prove the tension better than a drawing of two forces would, because the questions are the tension in its
real form. Text only.

Figure count: 0.

## Block 3

Claim: Every close product held either the structure or the room to move, never both.

What the reader has to see to believe it: for each product, which half it held and which it dropped.

Do the headline and body already make them see it? Yes. The three labels are written in parallel: each
names a product, what it does, what it gets wrong and what was kept or left from it. The body adds the
career products missing from the other side. The reader can check "each solved half" against every entry
without a picture. A drawn figure here would only repeat the labels, and product screens from other
companies would prove what those products look like, not which half they solved. Text only.

Figure count: 0.

Note for pass 2: blocks 2 and 3 are both text only and sit back to back, so they should not share the same
text placement.
