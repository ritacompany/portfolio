# Visual forms for qualitative and conceptual explanation

Scope: how expert explainers draw ideas, systems and mechanisms rather than data, so a form can be chosen per beat for a case study that must work as stills first. Labels used below: findings are sourced; inferences are this researcher's reasoning and are marked as such.

## Dan Roam: the six ways of seeing, the six frameworks and the SQVID

### Takeaway
Roam pairs six questions with six picture types: who or what to portrait, how much to chart, where to map, when to timeline, how to flowchart, why to multi variable plot. The SQVID then tunes any one picture along five slider pairs. For conceptual beats the map (relative position, not geography), the timeline and the flowchart do most of the work.

### Cited findings
- The six questions in Roam's "See" step are who or what, how much, where, when, how and why; they pair with portrait, chart, map, timeline, flowchart and multi variable plot. Roam calls these six the backbone for any problem solving picture. [Readingraphics summary](https://readingraphics.com/book-summary-the-back-of-the-napkin/); [Mediate.com review](https://mediate.com/the-back-of-the-napkin-new-approaches-to-visualizing-and-communicating-ideas/)
- The six questions sit inside a four step process: look, see, imagine, show. [Readingraphics summary](https://readingraphics.com/book-summary-the-back-of-the-napkin/)
- SQVID is five paired questions for choosing the style of a picture once you know what it must show: Simple vs elaborate; Quality vs quantity; Vision vs execution; Individual vs comparison; Delta (change) vs status quo. Roam's teaching example draws one apple at each pole. [Ethos3 review](https://ethos3.com/183/); [Welcome to the Jungle](https://www.welcometothejungle.com/en-GB/articles/back-of-the-napkin-can-drawing-help-your-business); [Mind mapping software blog, Roam interview](https://mindmappingsoftwareblog.com/dan-roam-unfolding-the-napkin/)
- Vision vs execution separates "where we are going" pictures from "how we do it" pictures; individual vs comparison asks whether to show one thing alone or beside alternatives. [Welcome to the Jungle](https://www.welcometothejungle.com/en-GB/articles/back-of-the-napkin-can-drawing-help-your-business)
- Delta vs status quo is "as is" vs "what it could become". [Ethos3 review](https://ethos3.com/183/)

### Inferences
- Applying Roam's grid to the beats (inference): "a hierarchy with no way through it" is a where question, drawn as a map of nested regions with no path; "a week holds a slice of a route" is when plus where, a route line with one window highlighted; "capacity sets how much fits" is how much, but qualitative, so a container with a fill level rather than a chart; "two dates, one fixed and one moving" is when, a single timeline with two different markers; "falling behind without failing" is a delta picture, the same timeline drawn twice; "structure versus flexibility" is an individual vs comparison picture, two panels side by side.
- On the SQVID, most beats in a case study read as stills belong at the simple, quality and execution poles. The opening framing beat is the one place for the vision pole.

### Gaps
- Roam's own site (napkinacademy) and the expanded edition were not fetched; the mapping comes from secondary summaries that agree with each other. Page numbers are unverified. The secondary sources differ slightly on SQVID wording (quality vs quantity is sometimes "qualitative vs quantitative").

## Tufte and McCloud on mechanism, cause and sequence in stills

### Takeaway
Tufte's Visual Explanations is explicitly about "pictures of verbs": mechanism, motion, process, cause and effect. Its tools for stills are parallelism (repeat the frame, change one thing) and the smallest effective difference (make the change the only loud mark). McCloud adds that the reader supplies what happens between two frames, so a sequence of stills explains change by what it leaves out. Tversky's research backs this: step by step stills match how people segment processes, and animation has not proved better than good statics.

### Cited findings
- Visual Explanations (Graphics Press, 1997) is catalogued as about pictures of verbs: mechanism and motion, process and dynamics, causes and effects, explanation and narrative. [Mediamatic catalogue](https://www.mediamatic.net/en/page/12619/visual-explanations)
- Its causal core is two cases: Snow's 1854 cholera map as good causal display and the 1986 Challenger O-ring charts as bad, where the engineers had the evidence but did not display cause (temperature) against effect (damage). [Online Ethics Center, Robison et al.](https://onlineethics.org/print/pdf/node/38206); [Tufte in Slate, 1997](https://edwardtufte.com/tufte/slate_0597)
- Counterpoint: Robison, Boisjoly, Hoeker and Young dispute Tufte's charge against the Thiokol engineers. [Online Ethics Center](https://onlineethics.org/print/pdf/node/38206)
- The "smallest effective difference" chapter argues subtle but clear distinctions can be far more powerful than heavy contrast. [In Trust review](https://www.intrust.org/in-trust-magazine/autumn-1999/words-in-pictures)
- The chapter "Parallelism: repetition and change, comparison and surprise" treats repeated structure with one varied element as an explanatory unit. [In Trust review](https://www.intrust.org/in-trust-magazine/autumn-1999/words-in-pictures)
- McCloud: closure happens in the gutter between panels; nothing is drawn there, but the reader infers what happened. His six transitions are moment to moment, action to action, subject to subject, scene to scene, aspect to aspect and non sequitur. [Hooded Utilitarian, Gavaler](https://hoodedutilitarian.com/?p=67985)
- Critique: McCloud's categories are often ambiguous in practice, and none covers causal closure (an action explained by something drawn in an earlier panel). The proposed alternative sorts closure into spatial, temporal and causal. [Hooded Utilitarian, Gavaler](https://hoodedutilitarian.com/?p=67985)
- Tversky: a frame says what is inside has a different status from what is outside; comics use frames to divide events in time or views in space. [Tversky 2011, Visualizing Thought](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/faculty/bt2158/faculty-profile/files/2011_Tversky_VisualizingThoughts.pdf)
- Tversky: the mind segments continuous processes into steps, so step by step presentation is more congruent than continuous presentation; animations are often too fast and complex (violating the Apprehension Principle) and "show, but do not explain"; across many contents they have not proved superior to static graphics. [Visualizing Thought](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/faculty/bt2158/faculty-profile/files/2011_Tversky_VisualizingThoughts.pdf)
- Congruence Principle: the content and format of the graphic should correspond to the content and format of the concept. Apprehension Principle: the graphic must be accurately perceived and appropriately conceived. Where animation looked better, comparisons were often unfair because the animation carried more information or interactivity. [Tversky, Morrison and Betrancourt 2002](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs448b/papers/Tversky_AnimationFacilitate_IJHCS02.pdf)
- Mayer's animation principle: people do not necessarily learn better from an animation than from static diagrams. [Ohio State multimedia learning page](https://u.osu.edu/multimedialearning/?p=11); [Mayer, research based principles (UNH PDF)](https://www.unh.edu/teaching-learning-resource-hub/sites/default/files/media/2023-06/itow-research-based-principles-for-designing-multimedia-instruction-mayer.pdf)

### Inferences
- Stills first is supported by evidence, not just a production constraint. Motion later should animate the transition between two approved stills, never carry meaning the stills lack.
- The useful McCloud transitions for a framework case study are action to action (same subject, one state change) and aspect to aspect (same moment, a different part of the system). Both need the frame and subject held constant, which is Tufte's parallelism.
- Causal beats need the cause drawn in the earlier frame. "Capacity sets how much fits": frame one shows the capacity, frame two shows what fits, nothing else changes. Without the cause in frame one, the reader cannot perform causal closure.

### Gaps
- Tufte's Beautiful Evidence (2006), especially the "fundamental principles of analytical design" (show causality, mechanism, explanation), was not fetched. McCloud's book text was not consulted directly; the six transitions come from a secondary source.

## Explorable explanations and visual essays, and how they work without interaction

### Takeaway
The explorable tradition (Victor, Case, Distill) treats understanding as movement between a concrete instance and an abstraction, guided by the author. Without interaction the same move survives as a pair or small multiple of stills: one concrete case, then the overview of many cases. Distill's survey finds readers prefer step based layouts to plain static articles and finds animation's advantage over a good still contested.

### Cited findings
- Victor: insight comes from moving between levels of abstraction. Stepping up shows all values of a parameter at once (a car's whole trajectory instead of one moment); stepping down picks one concrete state out of the abstraction. [Up and Down the Ladder of Abstraction](https://worrydream.com/LadderOfAbstraction/)
- Victor likens the all at once view to a helicopter view where every street is visible, at the cost that the concrete car disappears; every point on the abstraction corresponds to one concrete state. [Ladder of Abstraction](https://worrydream.com/LadderOfAbstraction/)
- Victor uses small multiples of variants, notes they are hard to compare and fixes it by transforming all variants onto a common baseline. [Ladder of Abstraction](https://worrydream.com/LadderOfAbstraction/)
- Victor wants text as "an environment to think in"; explorable examples should be guided by the author rather than left as a sandbox; multiple representations of one system deepen understanding. [Explorable Explanations](https://worrydream.com/ExplorableExplanations/)
- Nicky Case's five tricks: show what made you care; show, then tell (PEA: pictures, examples, analogy, e.g. a fractal picture before defining self similarity); link ideas with "therefore" and "but" rather than "and then"; cut 10% of a draft; test early for learning, not enjoyment. [How To Explain Things Real Good, transcript](https://ncase.me/StanfordTalk/transcript.html)
- Case's FAQ: examples first; worked example then a similar problem; share a hard won truth "the story way" through a compressed life rather than preaching. [ncase.me FAQ](https://ncase.me/faq/)
- Hohman et al. (Distill 2020) analyze 60+ interactive articles. Animation is effective for state transitions, uncertainty, causality and narrative, but whether it beats a well designed static graphic is debated. [Communicating with Interactive Articles](https://distill.pub/2020/communicating-with-interactive-articles/)
- McKenna et al. found readers preferred step or scroll layouts to static ones, with no engagement difference between the two; Zhi et al. found better comprehension with slideshow layouts. Both desktop only. [Hohman et al.](https://distill.pub/2020/communicating-with-interactive-articles/)
- Person shaped glyphs did not add empathy over abstract symbols; photographs of people did help interest and memory. [Hohman et al.](https://distill.pub/2020/communicating-with-interactive-articles/)
- Interactive articles are often built around multiple representations of one idea (a circle as algebra and as geometry). Segmenting: complex lessons in small parts aid learning. Multimedia principle: words and pictures together beat either alone. Redundancy: text and graphic should carry complementary, not repeated, information. [Hohman et al.](https://distill.pub/2020/communicating-with-interactive-articles/)
- Hohman et al. cite Shneiderman: overview first, zoom and filter, then details on demand. They also warn that not everything needs interactivity; it can distract or go unused. [Hohman et al.](https://distill.pub/2020/communicating-with-interactive-articles/)
- Maggie Appleton, after Lakoff and Johnson: metaphors highlight qualities source and target share and hide the rest; abstract thought rests on bodily spatial notions (ups, downs, ins, outs, betweens). [Metaphors web](https://maggieappleton.com/metaphors-web)
- Appleton's illustrated notes at egghead map abstract programming ideas onto familiar physical experiences, and her metaphors come from research into the topic and into the words learners use about it. [egghead case study](https://egghead.io/case-studies/maggie-appleton); [egghead podcast](https://egghead.io/podcasts/turning-technical-concepts-into-approachable-illustrated-metaphors-with-maggie-appleton)
- Appleton cites Lakoff and Johnson, Tversky and Tufte's Visual Explanations as her grounding. [egghead podcast](https://egghead.io/podcasts/turning-technical-concepts-into-approachable-illustrated-metaphors-with-maggie-appleton)

### Inferences
- The still equivalent of Victor's ladder is a two frame pair: the concrete instance (one person's one week) beside the abstraction (the whole route with that week marked). This is the natural form for "a week holds a slice of a route".
- Case's "therefore and but" gives the sequencing rule across beats: each beat's visual should answer the previous one with a but (a problem) or a therefore (a consequence), never an "and then".
- The Distill person glyph finding argues against decorating a running persona with a figure icon. A specific named situation does the work; a stick figure does not.
- Pick metaphors whose hidden side does not hurt. A "container" for capacity hides that capacity can change week to week; if that matters, the container needs a second state.

### Gaps
- Amelia Wattenberger and Bartosz Ciechanowski: no primary statement of their method was found in this pass (search returned unrelated people for Ciechanowski). Their form choices are not documented here and should not be asserted.
- Appleton's dedicated visual metaphor essay URL returned 404; her method is sourced from egghead profiles and interviews only.

## Diagram genres for concepts: when each works and fails

### Takeaway
Diagrams win when position on the page does the reasoning for the reader (Larkin and Simon). The marks carry built in meanings: boxes contain, lines connect, arrows assert cause or sequence, up means more, left to right means time (Tversky). A genre works when its built in meanings match the idea and fails when they fight it.

### Cited findings
- Larkin and Simon (1987): two representations can hold the same information yet differ in how easily it is used. Diagrams are indexed by location in a plane; they put the information an inference needs at one place, put cues to the next step adjacent and make explicit what text leaves implicit. [Larkin and Simon PDF](https://mechanism.ucsd.edu/bill/teaching/F12/cs200/Readings/larkin.whyadiagramissometimesworth.1987.pdf); [Jim Davies summary](https://www.jimdavies.org/summaries/larkin1987.html)
- Tversky: visual communication uses place on the page (proximity, center vs periphery, vertical, horizontal) and marks (dots, lines, arrows, boxes, blobs, likenesses, symbols) to express individual, category, order, relation, correspondence, continuum and hierarchy. [Visualizing Thought](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/faculty/bt2158/faculty-profile/files/2011_Tversky_VisualizingThoughts.pdf)
- Tversky: "lines connect, bars contain". Bars and boxes separate their contents from everything else; lines relate things on a shared dimension. Glyphs help when their natural meaning matches the intent and interfere when it conflicts. [Visualizing Thought](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/faculty/bt2158/faculty-profile/files/2011_Tversky_VisualizingThoughts.pdf)
- Tversky: arrows suggest asymmetric temporal or causal relations; diagrams with arrows drew step by step functional descriptions, without arrows structural descriptions. Arrows are ambiguous (label, sequence, cause, motion, force), with proposed counts of meanings from about seven to dozens. [Visualizing Thought](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/faculty/bt2158/faculty-profile/files/2011_Tversky_VisualizingThoughts.pdf)
- Tversky: children and adults put more and better at the top, never the reverse; time maps to the horizontal across cultures and ages; complete orderings use a straight horizontal or vertical line starting at one end; center vs periphery suits only vague importance. [Visualizing Thought](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/faculty/bt2158/faculty-profile/files/2011_Tversky_VisualizingThoughts.pdf)
- Tversky on trees: evolutionary trees put the present at the top while family and linguistic trees start time at the top, so vertical direction in a hierarchy is convention dependent. [Visualizing Thought](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/faculty/bt2158/faculty-profile/files/2011_Tversky_VisualizingThoughts.pdf)
- Tversky on tables (Kessell and Tversky 2008): for people across place and time, plain dots beat connecting lines for most inferences; lines matched dots only for one individual's sequence and interfered with other inferences. [Visualizing Thought](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/faculty/bt2158/faculty-profile/files/2011_Tversky_VisualizingThoughts.pdf)
- Tversky on cycles (Kessell and Tversky 2009): cycles are drawn as circles with a box per stage; people put the conceptual start at 12 o'clock and read clockwise, and once they see the circle they prefer the general depiction to the concrete case. Water cycle diagrams drawn over realistic geography obscure the cycle because arrows go up and down everywhere. [Visualizing Thought](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/faculty/bt2158/faculty-profile/files/2011_Tversky_VisualizingThoughts.pdf)
- Tversky: forces, traits, counterfactuals and negations are hard to depict unambiguously. [Visualizing Thought](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/faculty/bt2158/faculty-profile/files/2011_Tversky_VisualizingThoughts.pdf)
- Schematic figures carry meaning from their form in context: Tversky, Zacks, Lee and Heiser, "Lines, blobs, crosses and arrows" (Diagrams 2000). [Springer](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/3-540-44590-0_21); [Columbia PDF](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/faculty/bt2158/faculty-profile/files/_Diagrammaticcommunicationwithschematicfigures.PDF)
- Concept maps (Novak and Cañas): concepts joined by labelled links form propositions; an unlabelled line is not a proposition. Most general concepts at the top; built around a focus question; cross links join separate segments. [Novak and Cañas, Theory Underlying Concept Maps](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~paritosh/papers/sketch-to-models/Novak-Canas-TheoryUnderlyingConceptMapsHQ.pdf); [Raspberry Pi pedagogy summary](https://static.raspberrypi.org/files/curriculum/quickreads/7-Pedagogy_Summary_Concept_Maps_V4c_2023.pdf)
- Causal loop diagrams: they show feedback structure but not accumulation; a CLD can carry many untested assumptions about causality; delays are often underestimated; conventions should be followed faithfully. [The Systems Thinker, Fine tuning your causal loop diagrams](https://thesystemsthinker.com/fine-tuning-your-causal-loop-diagrams-part-i/); [System Dynamics conference paper 2003](https://proceedings.systemdynamics.org/2003/proceed/PAPERS/382.pdf)
- State diagrams: Harel's statecharts (1987) add hierarchy, concurrency and communication to flat state diagrams, because flat ones explode in state combinations; UML state machines descend from them. [Recurse Center summary](https://www.recurse.com/blog/59-paper-of-the-week-statecharts-a-visual-formalism-for-complex-systems); [Harel paper, Edinburgh copy](https://www.inf.ed.ac.uk/teaching/courses/seoc/2006_2007/resources/statecharts.pdf)
- 2x2 matrix: Lowy and Hood frame "2x2 thinking" as working the tension between two competing forces, deliberately constructed as a dilemma. A review notes that renaming the axes changes the whole matrix's argument. The form compares only two variables. [Globe and Mail review](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/report-on-business/2x2-matrix-illuminates-strategies/article655084); [strategy+business review](https://www.strategy-business.com/article/05110); [Management Consulted](https://managementconsulted.com/2x2-matrix/)
- Venn and Euler: Venn diagrams must show every zone, with empty sets shaded; Euler diagrams simply omit empty zones and are typically less complex as contours grow. [Kent Euler diagrams overview](https://www.cs.kent.ac.uk/events/conf/2004/euler/eulerdiagrams.html)
- Rodgers, Zhang and Purchase (2012): Euler diagrams with concurrent curves or disconnected zones are understood less well; non simple curves are preferred less. [Kent publication record](https://www.cs.kent.ac.uk/pubs/2012/3252); [Monash record](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/wellformedness-properties-in-euler-diagrams-which-should-be-used/)

### Inferences
Genre fit for each beat (inference, applying the findings above):
- "A hierarchy with no way through it": nested containers (boxes contain) are the honest form for hierarchy, and the absence of any arrow is the message. A tree implies traversal along its lines, so it would undercut "no way through". Failure risk: the reader reads nesting as organisation, a good thing. Show one concrete item lost in it (a step down the ladder) to make the problem visible.
- "Structure versus flexibility": a side by side pair under Tufte's parallelism, same frame and same content, one rigid and one flexible. A 2x2 is tempting but invites a reader to find four quadrants when there are two poles; use it only if a real second axis exists.
- "A week holds a slice of a route": a horizontal line for the route (time is horizontal), a box over one segment for the week (boxes contain). Victor's step down: the box can be expanded into the week's contents in a second frame.
- "Capacity sets how much fits": container with items, and the next frame changes only the container size (parallelism plus causal closure). Avoid an arrow from capacity to items; arrow meaning is ambiguous and the size change already shows cause.
- "Two dates, one fixed and one moving": one horizontal timeline, two markers with visibly different glyphs (a solid anchor and a hollow or ghosted marker), and in a second frame the moving marker shifted while the fixed one holds. The smallest effective difference applies: only the moving date changes.
- "Falling behind without failing": a before and after on the same timeline, where the plan line and the actual line diverge but the end goal box is still reached or still reachable. Up means better (Tversky), so do not drop the line downward unless decline is meant; show lateness horizontally, not vertically, to avoid reading as failure.
- Swimlanes, causal loops and state diagrams are system formalisms. They suit an appendix or a single mechanism beat, not a narrative sequence, because they show the whole at once rather than one change at a time.

### Gaps
- Harel's "On visual formalisms" (1988, higraphs and blobs) was not fetched. No sourced findings on swimlane diagrams were gathered. No direct Euler vs Venn comprehension experiment was found.

## Running example, persona through a sequence and the progressive build up

### Takeaway
User tested explanation research converges on one diagram per step, one change per step, the same subject throughout and an overview that each step points back to. A running example is Victor's concrete instance and Case's compressed life; the build up is Mayer's segmenting and signaling applied to one diagram that gains one element per beat.

### Cited findings
- Tversky et al. (2007), assembly instructions designed and tested by users: use one diagram per step, segment one step per part, show action, show the perspective of action, use arrows and guidelines for attachment and action. These principles apply broadly to visual explanations of how things work, and diagrams built to them beat the manufacturer's. [Visualizing Thought](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/faculty/bt2158/faculty-profile/files/2011_Tversky_VisualizingThoughts.pdf)
- Concrete context can fight the abstraction: realistic geography hid the water cycle. [Visualizing Thought](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/faculty/bt2158/faculty-profile/files/2011_Tversky_VisualizingThoughts.pdf)
- Mayer's segmenting principle: people learn better from a lesson presented in learner paced segments than as a continuous unit. Pre training: learners do better when they already know the names and characteristics of the main concepts. Signaling: cues that highlight the organisation of essential material help. [Ohio State multimedia learning](https://u.osu.edu/multimedialearning/?p=11); [Mayer, research based principles (UNH PDF)](https://www.unh.edu/teaching-learning-resource-hub/sites/default/files/media/2023-06/itow-research-based-principles-for-designing-multimedia-instruction-mayer.pdf)
- Worked examples principle: learners gain deep understanding when given worked examples during initial skill acquisition. [Cambridge Handbook of Multimedia Learning, worked examples chapter](https://cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/worked-examples-principle-in-multimedia-learning/8753055D1FB47CF1E2BB897FD44FBEF8)
- Case: show a worked example, then a similar problem; examples first. [ncase.me FAQ](https://ncase.me/faq/)
- Victor: move between one concrete instance and the abstraction of all instances; neither level alone gives insight. [Ladder of Abstraction](https://worrydream.com/LadderOfAbstraction/)
- Shneiderman via Hohman et al.: overview first, then details on demand; StretchText and similar devices disclose detail progressively. [Hohman et al.](https://distill.pub/2020/communicating-with-interactive-articles/)
- Photographs of real people help interest and memory where person glyphs do not. [Hohman et al.](https://distill.pub/2020/communicating-with-interactive-articles/)

### Inferences
- Recommended spine for the case study (inference): one running subject (a specific person in a specific situation, written not drawn as a glyph) carried through every beat, and one base diagram (the route on a horizontal timeline) that gains exactly one element per beat: the hierarchy that failed, then the route, then the week window, then capacity, then the two dates, then the falling behind state. Each still is the previous still plus one change. That satisfies one diagram per step, segmenting, signaling (the new element is the only emphasised mark) and Tufte's parallelism at once, and it gives motion a clean job later: animate only the delta between consecutive stills.
- Pre training argues for naming the framework's parts (route, week, capacity, dates) in the first beat that shows them, before they are used in a later beat.
- Risk to manage: a cumulative diagram becomes crowded by the last beat. Use Tufte's smallest effective difference: earlier elements fade to a quiet tone as new ones arrive, so the latest addition is always the loudest mark.

### Gaps
- No primary source was found that studies the cumulative "build up" diagram specifically (as distinct from segmenting in general). Duarte style slide builds and scrollytelling step patterns were not researched in this pass.
