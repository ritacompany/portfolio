# Hiring manager expectations for results/impact in unshipped UX case studies

Context: Haven, a CareerFoundry bootcamp concept app (LGBTQIA+ teens and vetted mentors called "Confidantes"). 6 proxy-group interviews, 6 moderated usability tests, no launch, no business metrics.

Source quality note: most accessible material is practitioner and mentor-platform blogs (UX Playbook, MentorCruise, UXfolio, CareerFoundry, UX Companion). NN/g pages on this topic are video summaries with little extractable text. Two Medium pieces by hiring managers returned 403 and could not be read. Where a claim comes from a blog with a commercial interest (portfolio tools, mentoring platforms, bootcamps), that is noted.

## What do hiring managers want in a results/outcome section, and do they expect numbers from junior or bootcamp work?

### Takeaway
Reviewers want to see that something changed because of the designer's work and that the designer thinks about success. For junior and concept work, the consensus across sources is that numbers are not expected; process, decision reasoning and honest evidence carry the weight. Shipped work with metrics is still preferred by some hiring managers, so a concept project competes on rigor of reasoning, not on outcomes.

### Cited Findings
- Sarah Doody (2019, updated May 2023): "People don't just want to know what you did, they want to know the impact of what you did." Her structure lists "Outcomes and lessons" as a required component, and she advises reflecting on what you learned and what you would do differently. Source: [Career Strategy Lab, Sarah Doody](https://www.careerstrategylab.com/how-to-write-a-ux-case-study/)
- NN/g (Rachel Krause, Nov 2019) frames a case study as the design, the process that led to it and "the business impact of the design." Source: [NN/g video: Creating a UX Design Portfolio Case Study](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/ux-design-portfolio-case-study/)
- NN/g (Krause and Maria Rosala, Aug 2020) surveyed 200+ hiring managers and reports that expectations differ for junior vs. senior and designer vs. researcher roles; the public page does not break out the specifics. Source: [NN/g video: UX Portfolios, What Hiring Managers Look For](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/ux-portfolios-hiring/)
- Indeed Design (April 2020), six named Indeed UX leaders: Anna Rowe (Senior UX Director) wants "the impact you've had"; Rhonda Gilligan (UX Design Manager) says to prove success "with metrics" showing it solved the initial user problem, and to "show us the 'before' if there was one"; Annie Jarvis (Senior UX Director) looks for "iterative design based on feedback"; Matthew Nesbitt asks "What was the conflict? What was the resolution?" Source: [Indeed Design: UX Design Portfolio Advice from Hiring Managers](https://indeed.design/article/ux-design-portfolio-advice-from-hiring-managers/)
- Same source, Corey Chandler (UX Design Manager): "I would rather see a smaller product that actually shipped. Concept projects aren't subjected to the same trials and tribulations." Source: [Indeed Design](https://indeed.design/article/ux-design-portfolio-advice-from-hiring-managers/)
- UXfolio (Feb 2026): for junior designers, outcome "can also mean validated learning, improved usability, or clarified direction"; the test is showing how something changed because of your work. Source: [UXfolio: UX Case Study Structure](https://blog.uxfol.io/ux-case-study-structure/)
- MentorCruise (Dominic Monn, July 2026): "Business impact documentation is achievable without production metrics." Reviewers spend 3 to 5 minutes on a cold portfolio and scan for business problem, constraints, documented choices, outcome evidence. (Mentoring platform, commercial interest.) Source: [MentorCruise: Product design portfolio that shows business impact](https://mentorcruise.com/blog/product-design-portfolio-how-to-build-one-that-shows-business-impact/)
- UX Playbook (Christopher Nguyen, Jan 2025): "You don't need massive conversion numbers to prove your worth. What you need to show is that you think about business impact." Source: [UX Playbook: Showcase impact without clear metrics](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/how-to-showcase-impact-in-ux-case-studies-without-clear-metrics)
- Recruiter Hang Xu via NN/g (Jan 2026): hiring managers "are not looking for a lesson or a quick course on what design is. They're trying to solve their own problems." Source: [NN/g: UX Hiring, Insights from a Design Recruiter](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-hiring-insights/)

### Inferences
- The Indeed quotes show real tension: some managers explicitly discount concept work. Haven cannot fix that with a results section; it can only reduce the gap by showing the "trials" it did face (proxy recruitment, safety constraints, what testing broke).
- "Impact" for a concept project is best read as "what changed in the design and the designer's understanding because of evidence," not business outcome.

### Gaps
- NN/g's 200-manager survey data on junior vs. senior impact expectations is behind a video; no text breakdown was retrievable.
- No source quantified what share of hiring managers expect metrics from bootcamp graduates.

## What do reviewers accept instead of metrics for unshipped work?

### Takeaway
Accepted substitutes, in rough order of weight across sources: usability test evidence tied to design changes (before/after), a decision trail from finding to change, defined success metrics you would track, then learnings and next steps. Every source says to label the project as concept up front.

### Cited Findings
- CareerFoundry (Jonny Grass, updated Aug 2021): "If you don't have any way of showing the impact of your project, lay out how you would measure the impact." Also: "No project is perfect or final. Showing next steps is a great way to demonstrate your thinking iterative approach." Source: [CareerFoundry: How to craft a UX case study](https://careerfoundry.com/en/blog/ux-design/ux-case-study/)
- UXfolio (Feb 2026): beginners should document "usability findings, qualitative feedback, or clearly defined success metrics you would track next"; "Even speculative results are valuable if you explain your rationale." Source: [UXfolio: UX Case Studies for Beginners](https://blog.uxfol.io/ux-case-studies-for-beginners/)
- MentorCruise (July 2026): accepted proxies are task completion from moderated tests, success on specific flows, stakeholder sign-off, business logic. Example given: 8 participants, six completed the redesigned flow without error vs. three on the original. "Speculative projects are acceptable when the case study documents the decision trail as rigorously as a shipped project would." Source: [MentorCruise](https://mentorcruise.com/blog/product-design-portfolio-how-to-build-one-that-shows-business-impact/)
- MentorCruise (June 2026) on usability testing entries: reviewers judge method rationale, sample-size justification and what changed because of the study. "Process narration (I ran 5 moderated tests with 5 participants) is not evidence." Source: [MentorCruise: Usability testing portfolio entry](https://mentorcruise.com/blog/usability-testing-what-your-portfolio-entry-says-about-you-as-a-researcher/)
- UX Playbook (Jan 2025): usability evidence such as "8 out of 10 users completed a task in half the time" is "gold"; indirect signals include reduced error rates and satisfaction scores. Source: [UX Playbook](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/how-to-showcase-impact-in-ux-case-studies-without-clear-metrics)
- Search-surfaced guidance (UX Playbook/Medium, 2025): for projects not live, "reflections and the project's next steps are sufficient to round out the results section"; be honest upfront that it is conceptual, since transparency signals integrity. Source: [UX Playbook via Medium: Showcase impact without metrics](https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/how-to-showcase-impact-in-ux-case-studies-without-clear-success-metrics-5ff03d791582)
- Phylicia Flynn (Medium, undated in snippet) proposes success rate, task time and NPS as measurables for conceptual projects. Source: [Ways to measure success for conceptual UX projects](https://phyliciaflynn.medium.com/ways-to-measure-success-for-conceptual-ux-design-projects-7054cd5ae32)
- Indeed's Rhonda Gilligan: "Show us the 'before' if there was one." Source: [Indeed Design](https://indeed.design/article/ux-design-portfolio-advice-from-hiring-managers/)

### Inferences
- For Haven, the strongest substitute is round-to-round usability evidence mapped to specific changes (finding, severity, change, retest result), reported as counts out of 6, not percentages.
- NPS with n=6 (suggested by Flynn) would likely read as a misuse of a quantitative metric; it conflicts with the MentorCruise sample-size guidance. Treat Flynn's suggestion with caution.
- "Success metrics I would track" works best when tied to Haven's actual risk (teen safety and trust in vetted Confidantes), e.g. match acceptance, first-message rate, report/escalation rates, rather than generic engagement.

### Gaps
- No source weighed which substitute hiring managers value most; the ordering above is an inference from emphasis.

## What makes reviewers distrust metrics in bootcamp case studies?

### Takeaway
Distrust triggers: big round percentages with no baseline or method, numbers that cannot plausibly follow from the design change, percentages from tiny samples, "5 users" with no quotes or rationale, and research that did not actually happen. Honest qualitative evidence is repeatedly said to beat fabricated quantitative claims.

### Cited Findings
- UX Companion (Jamie Pow, 2026), Mistake #10: flags "Increased engagement 320% with no baseline, no methodology, no acknowledgement of attribution"; senior reviewers spot excessively round figures, implausibly high percentages and results uncorrelated with the design change; "honest qualitative evidence beats fabricated quantitative." Source: [UX Companion: 20 UX Portfolio Mistakes](https://uxcompanion.co.uk/ux-portfolio-mistakes)
- MentorCruise (June 2026) red flags: default sample sizes without justification ("the 5 participants rule" is valid only for qualitative moderated testing with a homogeneous population), reporting findings without consequences, "using numbers habitually rather than defensibly." Source: [MentorCruise](https://mentorcruise.com/blog/usability-testing-what-your-portfolio-entry-says-about-you-as-a-researcher/)
- Search-snippet summary of hiring-manager red flags (Indeed Design / UX Playbook results page): a major red flag is "obvious fibs with research," e.g. claiming usability testing with 5 users when the process was not followed; if you could not follow the process, admit it. The exact wording could not be located in the fetched Indeed page, so treat attribution as unverified. Source: [Indeed Design](https://indeed.design/article/ux-design-portfolio-advice-from-hiring-managers/); [UX Playbook: 11 portfolio red flags](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/11-common-ux-portfolio-mistakes-and-solutions)
- Search-snippet (2026): hiring managers spot fabricated research such as surveys with suspiciously round numbers and "5 interviews" with zero quotes or raw notes. Source page among the results not individually verified. Source: [UX Companion](https://uxcompanion.co.uk/ux-portfolio-mistakes)
- UXfolio (Feb 2026): speculative projects should clarify their status and avoid inflated impact claims; "Most beginner portfolios fail not because of lack of skill, but because of misaligned scope." Source: [UXfolio beginners](https://blog.uxfol.io/ux-case-studies-for-beginners/)

### Inferences
- For Haven: "83% of participants..." from 6 people is the exact pattern reviewers read as inflated. "5 of 6" is honest and reads stronger.
- Calling interview participants "proxy group" explicitly (they were not the target teens) is a credibility asset, not a weakness, given the "admit it" guidance.
- Any projected business number (retention, adoption) for Haven would be invented and should not appear.

### Gaps
- Found no direct quote from a named Figma, Google or Meta recruiter on bootcamp metrics. Searches for Julie Zhuo and Tobias van Schneider on this specific topic were not run to completion within budget.

## Examples of case studies that handle "no launch, no metrics" well

### Takeaway
Guides recommend a consistent ending structure but few provide inspectable, well-regarded concept-project endings. The recurring structure is: status label, what testing changed, how success would be measured, learnings, next steps.

### Cited Findings
- UXfolio's case study generator ends with Business Impact, Learning and Next Steps sections; a learnings section should cover takeaways, what you would do differently, at least 3 to 4 sentences. Source: [UXfolio: Case Study Structure](https://blog.uxfol.io/ux-case-study-structure/); [UXfolio template 2026](https://blog.uxfol.io/ux-case-study-template/)
- UXfolio names Lindsay Wescott, "Colorsmith: Amazon" as an example; its ending structure was not described. Source: [uxfol.io/p/lwescott/colorsmith-amazon](https://uxfol.io/p/lwescott/colorsmith-amazon)
- UXfolio names Sooa Lee's portfolio as a beginner example, no ending analysis given. Source: [UXfolio beginners](https://blog.uxfol.io/ux-case-studies-for-beginners/)
- A case study by "Jaclyn" (cited by UXfolio) closes with an embedded interactive Figma prototype plus next steps. Source: [UXfolio: 27 Best UX Portfolio Examples](https://blog.uxfol.io/ux-portfolio-examples/)
- Example galleries exist but were not analyzed for endings: [Uxcel: 14 UX case study examples](https://uxcel.com/blog/ux-case-studies-examples), [LogRocket: 25 examples](https://blog.logrocket.com/ux-design/ux-design-case-study-examples/), [UXfolio examples](https://uxfol.io/examples)

### Inferences
- The MentorCruise 8-participant before/after example is the most concrete published model of a credible unshipped result and maps directly onto Haven's round-based testing.

### Gaps
- Could not verify any specific concept-project case study praised by a named hiring manager for its ending. This needs manual review of the example galleries above.

## How should the ending/closing section be structured?

### Takeaway
Sources converge on: (1) honest status line, (2) outcome as evidence of change (test findings and what changed), (3) how success would be measured post-launch, (4) reflection including what you would do differently, (5) next steps. Stakeholder impact appears only for client work; no source recommends it for solo bootcamp projects.

### Cited Findings
- Doody: "Outcomes and lessons," what you learned, what you would do differently in hindsight. Source: [Career Strategy Lab](https://www.careerstrategylab.com/how-to-write-a-ux-case-study/)
- CareerFoundry: how you would measure impact, plus next steps and learnings for course projects. Source: [CareerFoundry](https://careerfoundry.com/en/blog/ux-design/ux-case-study/)
- UX Companion: credibility comes from acknowledging what worked, what remained uncertain and what you would revisit given more time. Source: [UX Companion](https://uxcompanion.co.uk/ux-portfolio-mistakes)
- MentorCruise: frame the context clearly (e.g. "speculative redesign of ...") and document problem, constraint, decision and outcome evidence as if real. Source: [MentorCruise](https://mentorcruise.com/blog/product-design-portfolio-how-to-build-one-that-shows-business-impact/)
- UXfolio: learnings demonstrate growth mindset, problem solving and willingness to learn from mistakes. Source: [UXfolio](https://blog.uxfol.io/ux-case-study-structure/)

### Inferences
- A Haven ending that fits the evidence: "Concept, not launched" line; round-over-round test results as counts of 6 tied to specific changes; 3 to 4 named metrics tied to safety and trust with why each; the open risk the design did not resolve (e.g. real teen validation, since interviews were proxies); what would be tested next.
- Generic "learnings" (e.g. "I learned the value of research") are the weak spot; specific reflection tied to a decision reads as senior.

### Gaps
- No source addresses handling sensitive populations (minors, LGBTQIA+ youth) in a results section; ethics of proxy recruitment as a stated limitation is unaddressed in portfolio literature found.
