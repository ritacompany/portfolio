# Problem space statistics for Haven: LGBTQIA+ teens, affirming environments and adults, and mental health

Legend for the verification column used throughout:
- VERIFIED PRIMARY: read on the publisher's own page or PDF during this research (Sept 2026).
- PARTIAL: number seen at a primary URL but the exact wording came through a summarizer, or only secondary reporting was read.
- UNVERIFIED: secondary source only. Do not ship without checking.

Framing rule: Trevor Project "X% lower odds" figures are adjusted odds ratios (aOR) from cross-sectional surveys. They are associations, not causal effects, and they are not percentage point drops. Write "had 40% lower odds of reporting a suicide attempt," never "cut suicide attempts by 40%." Trevor samples are nonprobability samples recruited through social media ads, ages 13 to 24 (not teens only), US residents.

## 1. Trevor Project national survey findings (suicide risk, affirming spaces, accepting communities, care access, fear of outing, accepting adults)

### Takeaway
The two anchor numbers hold up at the primary source: at least one LGBTQ-affirming space was associated with 35% reduced odds of a past-year suicide attempt (2020 survey data, aOR = 0.65) and at least one accepting adult with 40% lower odds (2018 data, OR = .60; replicated in 2022 data for parent/caregiver acceptance, aOR about 0.60). Unmet mental health care is large and persistent: 56% (2023 survey), 50% (2024) and 44% (2025) of those who wanted care could not get it, with fear of being outed a named barrier (27% in 2023, 24% in 2024).

### Cited Findings

**Accepting adult (the "40%" stat)**
- Exact wording: "LGBTQ youth who report having at least one accepting adult were 40% less likely to report a suicide attempt in the past year." Supporting figures: 27% of youth without an accepting adult attempted suicide in the past year vs 17% with at least one; OR = .60, p < .001, controlling for race/ethnicity, age and gender identity. Sample 25,896 LGBTQ youth ages 13 to 24; data Feb to Sept 2018; brief published June 27, 2019. Type: odds ratio (and raw percentages). VERIFIED PRIMARY (via page summary). Note the headline says "less likely" but the statistic is an odds ratio; safer copy is "40% lower odds." [Trevor Project Research Brief: Accepting Adults Reduce Suicide Attempts Among LGBTQ Youth, June 2019](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/research-briefs/accepting-adults-reduce-suicide-attempts-among-lgbtq-youth/)
- 2023 update using 2022 survey data (n = 28,524, ages 13 to 24; 18,378 or 64.4% trans or nonbinary): "almost 9 in 10 (88%) reported that at least one adult was accepting of their sexual orientation"; "nearly 8 in 10 (79%) reported that at least one adult was accepting of their gender identity." Past-year suicide attempts: 19% without vs 14% with an adult accepting their sexual orientation; 25% without vs 19% with an adult accepting their gender identity. Adjusted odds by who accepts: parent/caregiver aOR 0.59 to 0.60 (about 40% lower odds); doctor aOR 0.55 (45% lower odds); teacher aOR 0.57 to 0.65 (35 to 43% lower odds); other family member aOR 0.57 to 0.67 (33 to 43% lower odds). Published Sept 22, 2023. Type: adjusted odds ratios plus raw percentages. VERIFIED PRIMARY (via page summary; the aOR ranges span the sexual orientation and gender identity models). [Trevor Project Research Brief: Acceptance from Adults is Associated with Lower Rates of Suicide Attempts Among LGBTQ Young People, Sept 2023](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/research-briefs/acceptance-from-adults-is-associated-with-lower-rates-of-suicide-attempts-among-lgbtq-young-people-sep-2023/) | [PDF](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Sept-2023-Brief_Accepting-Adults_Final.pdf)
- Disclosure in the same 2023 brief: "71% reported disclosing their sexual orientation to a parent/caregiver, compared to 52% of TGNB young people who disclosed their gender identity." By inference about 29% had not told a parent/caregiver their sexual orientation and about 48% of trans and nonbinary youth had not told one their gender identity. Type: percentages. VERIFIED PRIMARY (the "not out" figures are my arithmetic, label them as derived). [Same Sept 2023 brief](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/research-briefs/acceptance-from-adults-is-associated-with-lower-rates-of-suicide-attempts-among-lgbtq-young-people-sep-2023/)

**Affirming spaces (the "35%" stat)**
- Exact wording: "LGBTQ youth who reported having at least one LGBTQ-affirming space had 35% reduced odds of reporting a suicide attempt" (aOR = 0.65). By setting: schools aOR 0.62 ("strongest association with reduced suicide attempts"), homes aOR 0.78, workplaces aOR 0.87, community events aOR 0.89. Access: 62% had an LGBTQ-affirming school, 43% an affirming home, 36% an affirming workplace (employed youth only), 29% affirming community events. Trans and nonbinary youth: "at least one gender-affirming space had 25% reduced odds of reporting a suicide attempt" (aOR = 0.75); gender-affirming homes 42%, schools 55%. Sample 40,001 LGBTQ youth; data Dec 2019 to March 2020 (the 2020 National Survey); brief published Dec 3, 2020. Type: adjusted odds ratios. VERIFIED PRIMARY (via page summary). [Trevor Project Research Brief: LGBTQ & Gender-Affirming Spaces, Dec 2020](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/research-briefs/lgbtq-gender-affirming-spaces/) | [PDF](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/LGBTQ-Affirming-Spaces_-December-2020.pdf)

**2023 U.S. National Survey (data Sept 1 to Dec 12, 2022; n = 28,524; ages 13 to 24; 61% ages 13 to 17)**
- "41% of LGBTQ young people seriously considered attempting suicide in the past year." "14% of LGBTQ young people attempted suicide in the past year." Trans and nonbinary: 56% considered, 23% attempted. Type: percentages. VERIFIED PRIMARY (via page summary). [Trevor Project 2023 Survey](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/survey-2023/)
- "56% of LGBTQ young people who wanted mental health care in the past year were not able to get it." 81% wanted care. Top barrier "I was afraid to talk about my mental health concerns" (47%). Barrier: "I was not out about my LGBTQ identity and was afraid of being outed" (27% of those who could not get care). Type: percentages. VERIFIED PRIMARY (via page summary). [Trevor Project 2023 Survey](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/survey-2023/)
- Affirming homes: 38% found home affirming; past-year suicide attempt 10% with an affirming home vs 15% without. Affirming schools: 54%; 12% vs 16%. Trans and nonbinary youth whose household respected their pronouns: 12% attempt rate vs 21% when no one did. Type: raw percentages (not odds). VERIFIED PRIMARY (via page summary). [Trevor Project 2023 Survey](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/survey-2023/)

**2024 U.S. National Survey (data Sept 13 to Dec 16, 2023; n = 18,663; ages 13 to 24)**
- "39% of LGBTQ+ young people seriously considered attempting suicide in the past year"; "12% of LGBTQ+ young people attempted suicide in the past year." Ages 13 to 17: 46% considered, 16% attempted. Trans and nonbinary: 46% considered, 14% attempted. Type: percentages. VERIFIED PRIMARY (via page summary). [Trevor Project 2024 Survey](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/survey-2024/)
- "50% of LGBTQ+ young people who wanted mental health care in the past year were not able to get it." 84% wanted care. Barriers among those who could not get care: "I was afraid to talk about my mental health concerns with someone else" 42%; "I could not afford it" 40%; "I did not want to have to get my parent's/caregiver's permission" 37%; "I was afraid I wouldn't be taken seriously" 34%; "I was scared someone would call the police or involuntarily hospitalize me" 31%; "I was not out about my LGBTQ+ identity and was afraid of being outed" 24%; "I did not feel they would understand my sexual orientation or gender identity" 22%. Type: percentages. VERIFIED PRIMARY (via page summary). [Trevor Project 2024 Survey](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/survey-2024/)
- Where youth find affirmation: 68% reported affirming online spaces vs 40% affirming homes (trans and nonbinary: 73% online, 38% home). Type: percentages. VERIFIED PRIMARY (via page summary). [Trevor Project 2024 Survey](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/survey-2024/)
- Past-year suicide attempt by environment: affirming home 9% vs non-affirming 13%; affirming school 10% vs 14%; very accepting community 8% vs very unaccepting 20%. Trans and nonbinary: all people they live with respected pronouns 11% vs none 20%. Type: raw percentages. VERIFIED PRIMARY (via page summary). [Trevor Project 2024 Survey](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/survey-2024/)

**2025 U.S. National Survey (over 16,000 LGBTQ+ youth ages 13 to 24)**
- 36% seriously considered suicide; 10% attempted (trans and nonbinary 11%, cisgender 8%). 84% wanted mental health care; 44% of those could not get it. Barriers: "I could not afford it" 46%; "I was afraid to talk about my mental health concerns" 39%; "I did not want to have to get my parent's/caregiver's permission" 33%. Youth in "very accepting" communities attempted suicide at 6% vs 18% in "very unaccepting" ones. 85% of students had at least one supportive adult at school. Type: percentages. PARTIAL: summarizer gave the publication date as May 6, 2026, which I could not confirm; check the page's date and data collection window before citing. [Trevor Project 2025 Survey](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/survey-2025/)

### Inferences
- The strongest problem-section pairing: roughly half of LGBTQ+ youth who want mental health care cannot get it (50% in 2024), and a meaningful slice of those name fear of being outed (24%) or needing parental permission (37%). That is the Haven problem stated in the source's own terms: the doors to help run through adults and homes that may not be safe.
- The strongest ending pairing: one accepting adult (40% lower odds) and one affirming space (35% lower odds). Both are about presence of at least one, which supports a "one is enough to matter" framing without overclaiming causation.
- Online spaces were reported as affirming far more often than homes (68% vs 40%, 2024). This is the single cleanest stat for why a digital product is the right vehicle.
- Year labels trip people up: the "2023 survey" is 2022 data, the "2024 survey" is 2023 data. Cite the survey name, not the data year, and do it consistently.

### Gaps
- Exact per-setting access percentages and attempt rates for the 2025 survey beyond those listed were not captured.
- All Trevor figures above came through a page summarizer on the primary URL. The numbers matched across multiple fetches and search snippets, but exact punctuation of quotes should be checked by eye before publishing.

## 2. Not out to parents, fear of discovery, unsafe at home, homelessness due to family rejection

### Takeaway
Roughly 3 in 10 LGBTQ young people have not told a parent/caregiver their sexual orientation and roughly half of trans and nonbinary youth have not told one their gender identity (2022 data). 28% have experienced homelessness or housing instability, and identity-related rejection drives a large share of it.

### Cited Findings
- "28% of LGBTQ youth reported experiencing homelessness or housing instability at some point in their lives." Type: percentage. VERIFIED PRIMARY (via page summary). [Trevor Project Research Brief/Report: Homelessness and Housing Instability Among LGBTQ Youth, Feb 3, 2022](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/research-briefs/homelessness-and-housing-instability-among-lgbtq-youth-feb-2022/)
- 16% slept away from parents/caregivers because they ran away, with "55% reporting that they ran away from home because of mistreatment or fear of mistreatment due to their LGBTQ identity." "14% of LGBTQ youth reported that they had slept away from parents or caregivers because they were kicked out or abandoned," and 40% of those attributed it to their LGBTQ identity. Type: percentages (55% and 40% are shares of the subgroup, not of all youth). VERIFIED PRIMARY (via page summary). Sample 34,759 LGBTQ youth; data Oct to Dec 2020 (2021 National Survey). [Same brief](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/research-briefs/homelessness-and-housing-instability-among-lgbtq-youth-feb-2022/)
- Past-year suicide attempt: 28% of youth with past housing instability vs 10% of stably housed peers; 35% among currently homeless youth, "three times the odds" (aOR = 3.70). Type: raw percentages plus adjusted odds ratio. VERIFIED PRIMARY (via page summary). [Same brief](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/research-briefs/homelessness-and-housing-instability-among-lgbtq-youth-feb-2022/)
- Earlier brief (2018 data, n = 25,506): "25% of LGBTQ youth in our sample reported experiencing unstable housing at some point in their life"; housing instability associated with odds ratios of 2.75 (suicidal thoughts), 3.26 (attempts) and 3.07 (multiple attempts). Published Aug 28, 2019. VERIFIED PRIMARY (via page summary). [Trevor Project Research Brief: Suicidality of LGBTQ+ Youth Experiencing Housing Instability](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/research-briefs/unstable-housing-and-lgbtq-youth-suicidality/)
- Disclosure to parent/caregiver: 71% (sexual orientation) and 52% of trans and nonbinary youth (gender identity), 2022 data. See section 1. [Trevor Project Sept 2023 brief](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/research-briefs/acceptance-from-adults-is-associated-with-lower-rates-of-suicide-attempts-among-lgbtq-young-people-sep-2023/)
- Fear of being outed as a care barrier: 27% (2023 survey), 24% (2024 survey). See section 1.
- Affirming home is the minority experience: 38% (2023 survey), 40% (2024 survey), 43% (2020 data). See section 1.

### Inferences
- "Not out at home" plus "afraid of being outed" plus "don't want to need parental permission" together make the case that a teen-facing support tool has to work without a parent in the loop and without leaving traces. Stated as inference, not as a sourced claim.

### Gaps
- Did not verify the widely cited Chapin Hall "Voices of Youth Count" figure (LGBTQ youth at 120% higher risk of homelessness, 2018). It is outside the Trevor set and predates 2019; if used, verify at chapinhall.org.
- HRC/UConn LGBTQ youth report figures on being out to parents were not checked.

## 3. Text-based vs voice support preference

### Takeaway
The only primary LGBTQ-specific data found is from 2018: 77% of LGBTQ youth said they were somewhat to extremely likely to use text, chat or instant message in a crisis vs 43% for phone. It is dated (2018 data, April 2019 brief) but inside the 2019 window by publication date.

### Cited Findings
- Exact wording: "More than three-quarters of youth reported being somewhat to extremely likely to reach out via text, chat, or instant message (i.e., digital services) if they were in a crisis. This rate is almost double the percentage of youth who indicated that they would be somewhat to extremely likely to reach out via phone (43%)." Chart value 77%. Trans and gender diverse youth 80% vs cisgender LGBQ 75%. Reasons given by TrevorChat/TrevorText users for choosing digital: more confidential than phone 68%, easier to be themselves 63%, reduced fears of being misgendered 45% (trans youth). Sample: 2018 National Survey (34,808 consented, ages 13 to 24, data Feb to Sept 2018) plus a Sept 2015 to April 2017 service evaluation of "several hundred" users. Brief dated April 2019. Type: percentages. VERIFIED PRIMARY (read in full from the PDF). [Trevor Project Research Brief: Digital Support for Youth in Crisis, April 2019](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Trevor-Project-Digital-Support-Research-Brief-April-2019.pdf)
- A search summary stated that close to twice as many crisis contacts reach Trevor by chat/text as by phone each year. UNVERIFIED: I could not locate the primary wording or year. Do not ship. (Search result pointed toward Trevor prospectus PDFs, e.g. [FY22 prospectus](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/The-Trevor-Project-Prospectus-FY22.pdf).)

### Inferences
- Confidentiality (68%) and "easier to be themselves" (63%) are the reasons that map directly to a teen who is not out at home. Strong support for a text-first design decision.

### Gaps
- Crisis Text Line LGBTQ demographic share was not researched to verification in this pass. Check crisistrends.org or crisistextline.org before citing any figure.
- No 2022 to 2026 Trevor figure on share of contacts by modality was verified.

## 4. Mentorship and trusted adult effects

### Takeaway
The verified trusted-adult evidence is the Trevor accepting-adult work (section 1), including the breakdown showing teachers (35 to 43% lower odds) and doctors (45% lower odds) matter alongside parents. School-based supportive adults are near universal in GLSEN data, yet most LGBTQ students still feel unsafe.

### Cited Findings
- Accepting adult by role (2022 data): parent/caregiver aOR 0.59 to 0.60; doctor aOR 0.55; teacher aOR 0.57 to 0.65; other family member aOR 0.57 to 0.67. VERIFIED PRIMARY (via page summary). [Trevor Project Sept 2023 brief](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/research-briefs/acceptance-from-adults-is-associated-with-lower-rates-of-suicide-attempts-among-lgbtq-young-people-sep-2023/)
- GLSEN 2021 National School Climate Survey: 82% of LGBTQ students felt unsafe at school; 96% could identify at least one supportive staff member; 58% could identify six or more; about 35% could identify 11 or more. Type: percentages. PARTIAL: taken from search summary; verify wording in the executive summary. [GLSEN 2021 NSCS Executive Summary](https://www.glsen.org/sites/default/files/2022-10/NSCS-2021-Executive_Summary-EN.pdf) | [Report page](https://www.glsen.org/research/2021-national-school-climate-survey)
- 2025 Trevor survey: 85% of students had at least one supportive adult at school. PARTIAL (see section 1 note on the 2025 page). [Trevor Project 2025 Survey](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/survey-2025/)

### Inferences
- Adults at school are widely present (85 to 96%) but homes are affirming for well under half. The gap between "a supportive adult exists somewhere" and "an affirming adult is reachable when needed" is the space a product can work in. Inference only.

### Gaps
- No verified US data found on formal mentoring programs for LGBTQ youth specifically (e.g. MENTOR, Big Brothers Big Sisters LGBTQ outcomes). Not researched to verification in this pass.
- GLSEN has a 2023 or later survey edition (a 2025 report page appeared in search). The 2021 figures may be superseded; check for the latest release.

## Supplementary: CDC YRBS 2023 (population benchmark, high school students)

### Cited Findings
- 2023 YRBS: 65% of LGBTQ+ students reported persistent feelings of sadness or hopelessness vs 31% of cisgender and heterosexual peers; 41% seriously considered suicide; 20% attempted suicide; 53% reported poor mental health in the past 30 days vs 21% of peers. UNVERIFIED at primary: the CDC PDF could not be parsed and the CDC results page did not list the numbers. Figures come from secondary reporting ([ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Health/40-lgbtq-youth-considered-suicide-past-year-cdc/story?id=112604907)) and a search summary. Primary to check: [CDC YRBS Data Summary & Trends Report 2013 to 2023](https://www.cdc.gov/yrbs/dstr/pdf/YRBS-2023-Data-Summary-Trend-Report.pdf) and [MMWR Supplement Vol. 73 No. 4, 2024](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/su/pdfs/su7304a9-H.pdf).
- Why it matters: YRBS is a nationally representative probability sample, which the Trevor surveys are not. If one stat has to survive a skeptical reader, a verified YRBS number is the safer choice.
