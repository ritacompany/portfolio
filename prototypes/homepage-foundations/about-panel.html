Why the about panel works this way

The concept: a side drawer that gives a curious reader the fuller person in one glance, then puts them back exactly where they were. The superpowers step already says who you are in a few seconds; the panel is the optional second layer for the reader who wants more before the work.

  • Slides in from the left, over the page. Your call from the notes. It also sits opposite the nav, so opening it never covers the links that opened it. The page stays visible behind a 60% scrim, which tells the reader they have not left (progressive disclosure, Nielsen's "user control and freedom").
  • Two ways in. "More about me" under the superpowers line, where the question comes up, and ABOUT in the nav from anywhere on the page. Both open the same panel. The URL becomes #about, so a link can open it directly.
  • Three ways out. Close, Escape or a click on the page behind it. Focus goes back to whichever control opened it, so a keyboard reader keeps their place.
  • No scrolling. About and Current role, in the frame's own type (20px labels, 16px body, 545px column), fit a 1280 by 720 laptop with room to spare. Below 760px of height the spacing tightens before anything scrolls. Phones are the one exception, where the panel goes full width and may scroll.
  • Visual language from the page, not new. The panel uses the superpower container: the #040405 fill, the #28282b edge and two of its corner squares on the open edge. It reads as part of the same system.
  • Motion. Out in 800ms on an expo curve, back in 420ms, faster because closing is a dismissal. The text arrives just after the panel, label then paragraph. Reduced motion swaps all of it for a short fade.
  • Accessible by default. A modal dialog with a label, focus held inside while open, page scroll locked behind it.

About

I studied psychology before I studied design, and the thing that stuck was that a cause and an effect are never a fixed pair. Whether two variables stay connected, flip or quietly stop mattering changes depending on what else is in the room.

No problem is the same, and neither is one person. I’m a verbal processor that thrives in environments that foster collaboration. Most of what I know about my own work came from someone else saying it out loud first. So I try to keep a room where no voice feels less than another.

Current role

I lead design at Mondai, a tech career tool that the industry forgot to map. Two years, one of four people deciding what it becomes. I met the founder when he was my mentor. Now I'm one of the voices he argues with.
