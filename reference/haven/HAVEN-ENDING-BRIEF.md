# Haven ending brief

A fresh-start summary of the 29 and 30 Sep 2026 working sessions, so the ending is decided
from the whole picture and not from the last round of feedback.

## The goal

Finish the Haven case study so a hiring manager wants to interview Chadwick. Haven is one of
very few case studies he has, so it has to work harder than a bootcamp project usually does.
The only missing piece is the ending section. A small simple footer sits after it.

## What Haven is (from the Figma frame `Haven CS Master`, 3300:12596)

- A CareerFoundry bootcamp project, solo, unshipped. Scope: research, strategy, UX. The brief was
  to build a product for a specific user group with a way to connect users to an expert.
- The user: LGBTQIA+ teens who lack affirming environments. The page opens on belonging being
  "a difficult and isolating experience".
- Research: two proxy groups (3 subject matter experts, 3 LGBTQIA+ college students), 6
  interviews, 5 interview insights. Minors need a guardian's consent to join a study, and asking
  would out the teens Haven is for.
- MVP framing: two phases, Identity then Community. The timeline marks "Case study end point" at
  the Identity launch.
- Usability testing: 6 participants, 4 testing insights.
- Solutions: the Confidante program, an identity quiz, Confidante selection moved after the intro
  chat (testing showed hesitation and undone picks), a redesigned Confidante profile, scheduling,
  text-only chats and a privacy setup (disguised icon, app switcher hiding, notifications off,
  PIN with Face ID).
- All 9 findings are tagged to the design changes they drove.

## Rules and decisions already made

- No invented metrics. Counts he recalls from the sessions are his to state. Nothing about the
  project taking eight months. LGBTQIA+ everywhere.
- Visual style is his: dark, Satoshi, Neutral tokens, Heading/64 available, Metalab-derived
  layout grammar. No monospace. Lists on the page are interactive, so static lists or rows read
  as broken.
- The Testing synthesis flow animation is built as a component (step 3 moves to position 5).

## What has been tried for the ending and why it failed

1. Figma options A to C (test plan table, "still open" finding cards, milestone track): messy,
   unclear, did not feel like an ending.
2. HTML round one (Display/212 header, numbered moves, paired rows, insight line): header too
   big, monospace notes, list rows that look interactive.
3. HTML round two (gray bookend band, repeated MVP timeline, two tone note): the band's slogan
   "a profile gives information, a conversation gives trust" oversimplified the case study, the
   teen study without parental permission is not realistic, repeating the timeline was overkill.
4. Standalone B and B plus C: still not an ending.
5. Round three (full circle, tally, closing note): focused on proxy groups and research
   logistics, which is a misdirection. It does not feel like an ending beat.

The common failure: each round patched the last one. The thread ran metrics, then measurement,
then next test, then proxy groups, so the ending kept being about research logistics when the
case study is about Haven and the teen it serves, and the reader is a hiring manager deciding
whether to interview.
