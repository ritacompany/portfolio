=== Hero
Designed as a way for LGBTQIA+ youth to gain access to affirming environments.
For many LGBTQIA+ teenagers, finding a sense of belonging and acceptance can be a difficult and isolating experience. The lack of affirming environments at home and in their communities is linked to a higher risk of suicide.
This project was developed as part of a UX bootcamp. The brief was to create a functional product for a specific user group and include a way to connect users with an expert, which Haven calls a Confidante. Just having Haven on their phone could put a user at risk, so I designed it to stay hidden and to keep every chat by text.

=== Demographic access
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Demographic access
[headline] Minors need a guardian’s consent to join a study. Asking would out the teens who need Haven most, so I interviewed two proxy groups instead.
[eyebrow or sub-heading] 3 Subject matter experts
[eyebrow or sub-heading] 3 LGBTQIA+ college students
[eyebrow or sub-heading] 6 Total interviews
[eyebrow or sub-heading] 5 key insights
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Insight 1, Humanizing experts
When choosing between experts, users look for what makes each one feel human. They want to know the expert will understand them.
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Insight 2, Sensitive conversations
Users were uncomfortable discussing sensitive identity topics aloud, especially when someone nearby could overhear.
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Insight 3, Unique identities
A one size fits all identity model could flatten the demographic differences that shape each user’s unique experience.
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Insight 4, Scheduling concerns
Users need to pick a time when they can be alone and feel safe talking to an expert.
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Insight 5, Personal security
Users are concerned with using an LGBTQIA+ product in case disapproving guardians or peers may find out.
=== Designing an MVP
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Designing an MVP
[headline] Because the subject is complicated and sensitive, I planned Haven as an MVP in two phases: identity first, then community.
Identity comes first because Haven matches each user with a Confidante through who they are. The two phases let me decide which features mattered most now and which could wait until after the MVP.

[eyebrow or sub-heading] Wireframes

=== The Confidante program
[eyebrow or sub-heading] The Confidante program
[headline] The Confidante program is built to adapt to each user’s needs and comfort.
Confidantes support the user in two capacities:
[eyebrow or sub-heading] 01 A community liaison that connects the user to different people and communities.
[eyebrow or sub-heading] 02 A source of support for users who need guidance or someone to talk to.
=== Primary identity quiz
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Primary identity quiz
[headline] One identity model for every user would flatten the differences that shape their experience, so the quiz matches users on the experiences they pick.
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Tags: Interview insight 1, Interview insight 3, Confidante role 1
The quiz groups prompts into five areas: attraction, gender identity, race and ethnicity, belonging and upbringing. Confidantes then see a summary of the shared experiences behind each match, so they can tailor support and recommend communities that fit.
=== Testing synthesis
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Testing synthesis
[headline] Haven’s early flow asked users to choose a Confidante before they could have an introductory chat.
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Tags: Interview insight 1, Testing insight 4
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Confidante selection journey flow
Testing showed users hesitating over which Confidante to pick. They went back and forth between profiles, and some undid their choice after making it. I moved selection after the introductory chat, which exists to help them decide. In the revised flow, users can chat with more than one Confidante before choosing one.
=== Confidante profile
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Confidante profile
[headline] Users need to understand who the Confidante is before making a decision. They need to feel understood, represented and safe.
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Tags: Interview insight 1, Interview insight 3, Testing insight 4
Shared experiences, differences and humanized bios help users compare Confidantes and decide whom to schedule a chat with. Testing showed that those details needed more prominence. I made shared experiences the focus of the page and replaced vertical swiping with suggested Confidantes along the bottom, so users could switch between profiles.
=== Scheduling a chat
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Scheduling a chat
[headline] Users needed to know what to expect before booking an introductory chat.
Testers were confused about how chats would work and how long they would last, so the scheduling screen now says they happen by text, last a set 30 minutes and can end early. That lets users pick a time when they can be alone.
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Tags: Interview insight 4, Testing insight 1, Testing insight 2
=== Chat-based interactions
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Chat-based interactions
[headline] When speaking out loud is not safe or private, the interaction itself has to lower the barrier to asking for support.
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Tags: Interview insight 2
Chats happen by text, not voice or video, so users don’t have to say anything sensitive out loud. Each chat has a set length and can end early, which leaves users in control of when and how much they share.
=== Security features
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Security features
[headline] Privacy is a condition for access. Users need safeguards that protect the app before, during and after a conversation.
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Tags: Interview insight 5, Testing insight 3
=== Where things stand
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Where things stand
[headline] Haven’s initial MVP phase was built around two needs:
To be understood and to be safe.
A quiz pairs users with Confidantes who share their experiences.
Profiles lead with the experiences a user and Confidante share.
Intro chats happen by text, at a time the user can be alone.
An intro chat comes first, so users can see if it feels right.
Haven can hide behind a different icon on the user’s phone.
Identity was the first phase.
Community is the next.
