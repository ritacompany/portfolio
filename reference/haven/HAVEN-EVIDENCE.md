# Haven evidence

Source material for the Haven case study. Haven was the CareerFoundry bootcamp case study
(see `docs/about-source/WORKING-PROFILE.html`), not a multi-year project. Every claim on the
Haven page traces to the Figma frame `Haven CS Master` (3300:12596) or to this file.

## Owner statements, 29 Sep 2026

PARAPHRASE of Chadwick's dictation in the Claude Code session of 29 Sep 2026. Get his exact
wording before quoting any of it.

### Why the research used proxy groups

- Anyone under 18 needs a guardian's consent to take part in a study.
- That skews the pool from the start. Teens whose guardians would consent already have some
  level of affirmation at home.
- The teens Haven is for cannot be recruited at all. Asking a guardian for consent would out a
  teen whose family does not know.
- The closest proxy is LGBTQIA+ college students, often living away from their parents' homes,
  who can explore that side of themselves and look for affirming environments elsewhere.
- Subject matter experts were the second proxy group.

### What usability testing showed about choosing a Confidante

- Users did not feel confident choosing a Confidante without getting to know them first.
- Even with profile information, they hesitated over the decision and took longer, going back
  and forth between Confidantes.
- Some users went back and undid their choice after making it. Chadwick described this as
  "a lot of users". No count was recorded in this session, so do not state a number.
- This is why selection moved after the introductory chat.

### What the testing cannot support

- Tests ran in a controlled session, so completion rates for the identity quiz would not
  reflect use at home or in public.
- Adoption of the passcode or disguise icon cannot be measured in a controlled session. How a
  user feels at home or in public is too strong an influence.
- Intro chat to booked session conversion was not testable. That step sits after the intro
  chat and was outside the tested MVP.

### Next research step: 18 and 19 year olds at home

- Owner, 30 Sep 2026: a study with minors that skips parental permission is not a realistic
  next step. The closest legal pool is 18 to 20 year olds. He used college students because
  they can be reached on campus and recently lived through the same conditions.
- Research check (research_notes/Case study results without metrics/success_metrics_unlaunched.md):
  45 CFR 46.408(c) does let an IRB waive parental permission, but it needs institutional
  review. Mustanski spent 10 of 24 grant months getting one approved for 16 and 17 year olds,
  and IRBs at different sites decided the same request inconsistently (Klitzman et al. 2023).
  Not a step an independent designer can take.
- Chosen step: recruit 18 and 19 year olds who still live with family. They can consent on
  their own and are in the at-home conditions Haven is designed for, which college students
  living away may no longer be. The college student proxy still holds for recalling the
  experience; the at-home group adds current conditions.
