# CS2 ending, filled in with example results (12 Nov)

His ask: show what the ending could look like if the tests in `ending-test-plan.md` come back well, so he
can fill in the real numbers later. Every number below is an EXAMPLE, invented to show the shape. None of
it ships. The preview is `ending-filled-preview.html` in this folder, with an Example and Template switch.

One version, not a round of three, because this is a fill in template for the shape of the result, not the
copy round. It follows the research: the result first, then a short reflection on what broke and what
changed. Numbers sit in sentences and one chart, never a stat row (Haven owns the stat block).

Checks: humanizer full pass and no-ai-slop (detect) run as skills on the final text. Reader check not run.

## The version

Eyebrow: What eight people showed

Headline: Eight people typed the hours they could give each week, then logged what they really gave for two
weeks. Six of the eight stayed within a fifth of their number.

Chart: one row per person, sorted by hours typed. A ring for the hours they typed, a dot for the two week
average they gave, a line between them. The rows that missed are magenta, the Rollover colour, because
those are the people whose weeks would have kept rolling over. Caption: Hours a week, typed and given. One
row per person. This is the one memorable moment: every person is visible, so the small n is the point,
not something to hide.

Body 1: Before the first session I wrote down what would count as a fail: four or more of the eight giving
under 70 percent of their number in both weeks. Two did. The other two tests held as well. Shown the two
dates with no explanation, seven of eight predicted that an unfinished week moves the pace and leaves the
target where it is. Six of eight read the Rollover screen as the plan making room for them; the other two
read it as a mark against them.

Body 2: The two who missed were the two who typed the most hours, and both gave under half of them by week
two. Recalibration waits for three short weeks, so their plan would have stayed about twice as full as their
real week for one more Sunday. I changed it to step in after two.

Sources: Two week diary and moderated prototype sessions, eight people recruited for the study, December
2026. Fail criteria written before the first session.
