# Cold read: Framework case study

Reader: design hiring manager, no prior knowledge of Mondai. One pass, top to bottom.

## 1. Hero
1. A founding designer worked on Mondai, an AI career navigator for "the next generation of hustlers". The right-hand blurb is unfinished, so I do not know what the case study is about.
2. Rereads:
- "Mondai is an AI tech career navigator designed for the next generation of hustlers." Jargon: "navigator" and "hustlers" tell me nothing concrete. Is it a course, a coach, a job board?
- "This case study is focused on [placeholder text]" It is a placeholder, so the section gives me no thesis.
- "Founding designer | Affiliate: Mondai" Reads as contradictory. A founding designer is not an affiliate. No team size or timeline either.
3. Terms relied on but undefined: none yet. The product itself is undefined.
4. Nothing repeats yet. The title "Framework CS" is internal shorthand ("CS"), and "framework" is never explained for the whole piece.

## 2. Competitive analysis
1. The team found no direct competitor, so it borrowed ideas from CareerFoundry, Motion, Linear and learning apps. Each borrowed idea is backed by a psychology study. Mondai shows a target date and a pace date, treats the calendar as optional, keeps the home screen to next steps, and lets people fit work around their day.
2. Rereads:
- "Mondai shows two dates side by side: the target the plan sets and the pace date your progress is heading for." Target and pace date are introduced here with no setup. I do not know what "the plan" is, or what is being planned.
- "so a connected calendar suggests where the work fits, and the plan still runs without one." "The work" and "the plan" have no referent. What work? Whose plan?
- "so the home screen shows only the next steps and the full Growth Journey, every step to the goal, has its own page." Growth Journey appears capitalised with a gloss ("every step to the goal") but it is still not a defined thing. Also long, with three ideas in one sentence.
- "Research shows having a say raises motivation and that learners with clear goals and plans are more likely to reach them, so the plan keeps a target and a pace while each person fits the work into their own day." Too long, and the logic jump from "having a say" to "target and pace" is not clear.
- Card labels "Progress / CareerFoundry", "calendar integration / Motion" etc. use inconsistent capitalisation and read as notes.
- "Motion showed me AI could find free time" Is Motion the product? Not clear at first. Also this switches to "I" for the first time while the headline says "we".
3. Undefined terms: target, pace date, plan, Growth Journey, "the work". Target and pace become clear only in section 11. Growth Journey is first glossed in section 3, with a real explanation in section 4.
4. "we" in the headline versus "me" and "I" in the cards. The citation list is one dense line of seven names that I cannot map to claims.

## 3. The foundation we built on
1. The user's whole route to a new career is called the "Growth Journey". It has five Pathways (Discovery, Cognition, Networking, Branding, Opportunity). Each Pathway breaks into Focus areas, which break into Action items.
2. Rereads:
- "The user's route to their new career and the curriculum that existed to get them there was defined as the user's 'Growth Journey'" Grammar (subject/verb: "route and curriculum ... was") and it is a mouthful. "Curriculum that existed" is confusing. Did a curriculum exist before the product?
- "The growth journey was based on five pathways, each one covering a different aspect of the user's career journey" "Journey" is used twice and capitalisation switches to lowercase, so I wondered whether growth journey and career journey are different things.
- "Each pathway has two additional layers below it; focus areas and action items." Not wrong, but the hierarchy is only in a diagram label list, so I cannot tell which is the parent.
3. Terms: Growth Journey, Pathway, Focus area, Action item. All are defined here only by position in a hierarchy, with no description of what an Action item is. I get "Action item" properly only in section 6, and what one looks like in section 7. The five pathway names are never explained. "Cognition" and "Branding" mean little to me.
4. Repeats the Growth Journey gloss from section 2 and again in section 4.

## 4. Growth Journey
1. The Growth Journey is a page showing the whole route. People open it occasionally to see progress and what remains. Long-term numbers like target, pace and progress live there, deliberately hidden from the weekly screens because research says daydreaming about the goal makes people apply less.
2. Rereads:
- "Long term metrics like their journey target, pace and progress sit here out of the user's immediate view." Which metrics exactly? "Journey target" is new, and I still have no definition of target or pace.
- "Research on recent graduates found that the ones who daydreamed most about getting a job applied to fewer jobs and got fewer offers." Interesting, but "the same effect" in the next sentence is a stretch. Showing a date is not daydreaming.
- "Putting a far off finish line in front of someone every week can have the same effect, so the weekly screens stick to the current Pathway and the full journey lives here." Long, and "can" is hedged.
3. Terms: target, pace, progress (still undefined). "Weekly screens" implies a weekly structure I was not told about.
4. Contradicts section 2 Card 03 slightly: there the Growth Journey page is justified by "near goals build confidence", here by "daydreaming". Two different research justifications for the same decision. Also the Growth Journey is called a "page" here but a "route" or "curriculum" in section 3. Section 2 already stated this decision.
   There is no screenshot or description of what is on the page, only why.

## 5. Pathway
1. Each Pathway also has its own page listing its Focus Areas and Action Items in order. Users look ahead there occasionally.
2. Rereads:
- "Each Pathway has its own page, with all of its Focus Areas and Action Items laid out in the order someone works through them." Fine, but the capitalisation of Pathway, Focus Areas and Action Items is inconsistent with the lowercase in the body of the same section.
- "They won't need it every week, but it's there whenever they want to look ahead." Mild; it says nothing about a design decision.
3. Terms: "focus areas, like user testing or visual design" is the first concrete example. This tells me the product is for design careers. Until now I thought it was generic "tech". This is a late and useful clarification that should come in the hero. "Weekly" again.
4. Repeats section 4's structure and its "they won't need it often" point. The section 3 hierarchy sentence is repeated as "Each pathway is split into focus areas". Roughly 80 percent redundant with sections 3 and 4.

## 6. Action hub
1. The Action Hub is the main weekly screen. It lists this week's Action Items and replaces a traditional home screen. Each week opens with an AI-written headline and paragraph. Items must be done in order, so only the top "Active" one can be ticked.
2. Rereads:
- "Most of a person's week happens on one screen, the list of Action Items they come back to nearly every day." The comma splice is confusing. Is the screen the list?
- "Each week opens with a short headline and a paragraph the AI writes about that week's work." First mention of AI writing content. The AI role is unexplained: what AI, trained on what, writing what?
- "so it talks about the work itself and not about how much they've finished." Why? No reason is given, unlike elsewhere.
- "Action Items are finished in order here, so only the Active one at the top can be checked off." "Active" is a new state with no definition of other states. Sudden constraint with no rationale.
3. Terms: Action Hub (new, capitalised, and first called a "home screen" substitute), Active (a state). Action Item finally becomes something people tick off, but I still do not know what one is in content terms (a task? a lesson? a reading?). This starts to be answered in section 7.
4. Repeats: "full curriculum stays on the Growth Journey and Pathway pages" repeats sections 4 and 5. Section 2 Card 03 said the same "home screen shows only the next steps". Contradiction candidate: section 2 says "home screen", section 6 says "instead of a traditional home screen", section 10 says "The list stays the home view". Three terms for the same thing.

## 7. Action item
1. The Action Item page shows content the AI writes. The AI was not finished when this was designed, so the designer made a loose set of plain containers. MVP is text only, no input from the user.
2. Rereads:
- "Someone has to read each piece of work the AI writes, and when I designed the page the AI was still being built." The headline is confusing. "Someone has to read" reads like a human reviewer. It is really "the user has to read".
- "So the page is a set of plain containers the AI can fill however it turns out to work, and I kept the structure loose in case it couldn't use all of it at first." Abstract. "Containers" are never shown or named, so I cannot judge the design. Run-on.
- "That keeps the page simple for the MVP and leaves me room to grow it into something richer once the AI is ready." Vague, and "richer" unspecified.
3. Terms: MVP (fine). "Containers" is undefined. I still do not know what an Action Item contains. This section tells me what is NOT in it but not what IS.
4. Contradiction: section 6 said the AI writes the weekly paragraph, and here "the AI writes each Action Item as text". Fine, but section 3 implied a fixed curriculum, while here the AI writes the content. Is the curriculum fixed or generated? This hits the credibility of sections 3 to 5.

## 8. Personalized timeline
1. The number of Action Items a user gets per week depends on their weekly hours (capacity) and how heavy the items are (cognitive load). Example: Suzie has 6 hours, gets 3 items this week and 4 last week because this week's items are heavier.
2. Rereads:
- "The amount of action items each user was assigned per week became based on two things:" Grammar ("amount" should be "number", "became based on"), and the headline ends in a colon.
- "Each user sets their own capacity by informing the user how many hours they can dedicate per week." Circular: "informing the user" should be "telling Mondai". Unclear referent.
- "The complexity of an action item and it's required effort" Typo ("it's"), and cognitive load is defined as complexity plus effort, which is not the usual meaning. Who rates it? The AI? Unclear.
- "This is because this week's action items require more cognitive load than last week's." Wait, more load with fewer items and the same 6 hours? The example reads backward: this week has 3 items, last week 4, and 3 is the heavier week. It is consistent but I had to work it out.
3. Terms: capacity (defined here, finally), cognitive load (defined thinly). Note sections 11 and 12 use capacity again. Section 2 never mentioned it.
4. Weak connection to section 6. If hours set the number of items, how is "hours" shown anywhere? Section 14 later has "recalibration" of capacity, which depends on this. Lowercase "action items" versus capitalised elsewhere. No design content: no screen, no decision, no tradeoff. Reads like a spec.

## 9. Calendar setup
1. Calendar sync is optional. If on, the user answers two questions: their available time range and their "sharpest hours". Mondai plans Action Items around those answers.
2. Rereads:
- "Setting a preferred time is essentially the user telling the product their own availability, meaning when it can and can't schedule Action Items." "Preferred time" appears out of nowhere; the headline said "two quick questions" and I do not see what they are. "Schedule" is now used for the first time.
- "The range needs at least eight hours, because Mondai can only use the time not already taken by the person's own events. A short range on a busy day leaves none." "The range" has no referent (which range?). Eight hours of availability seems odd for a "preferred" time. Why is that the minimum?
- "Sharpest hours are more of a suggestion." "Sharpest hours" is undefined (I guess peak focus hours). "More of a suggestion" than what?
3. Terms: range, sharpest hours, schedule. Section 10 adds "the hours they chose in setup", which clarifies the range a little. Sharpest hours is understood only by inference.
4. Contradicts section 2 Card 02 in tone. That card said research showed pushing people to schedule did not help, yet this section builds an elaborate scheduler. It is reconciled only by "optional", which should be said.

## 10. Schedule view
1. With the calendar synced, the Action Hub can show the week as a schedule. Mondai finds gaps between the person's events and places items there. Items can be moved by the user. Mondai never writes to the calendar, only reads it, and never moves items on its own. Items are due by week's end, not at a time.
2. Rereads:
- "With a calendar synced, the Action Hub can show the same week as a schedule, with Mondai finding a time for the Action Items that fit around the person's day." Very long, with a dangling "that fit".
- "The calendar starts at the first of those hours, 8am for instance, so the day opens on the time Mondai can plan in." Hard to parse.
- "Each block is as long as its Action Item takes, and this is the only place someone can see how big a piece of work is before they start it." Important design point buried. It also contradicts the idea that Action Items are loose text containers: they have a duration. Where does the duration come from?
- "Mondai only reads the calendar. It never writes Action Items into it, because that calendar holds commitments Mondai doesn't own." But "Mondai finds a time" and "offers open time" sound like scheduling into the calendar. Reread to check; it means inside Mondai's own view only.
- "Automatic moving was easy to build, and I left it out because a plan that rearranges itself gets confusing." A good sentence, but the "easy to build" implies engineering context I do not have.
- "An Action Item is due by the end of its week, not at its time, so one without a time is fine, and finishing it still counts toward the week's progress." Contradiction feel: time versus due. Three ideas in one sentence.
- Subhead "Reschedudling an action item" has a typo.
3. Terms: the scheduled time versus due date distinction, "week's progress" (what is progress, measured how?), "list" versus "schedule" views, "home view".
4. Repeats section 9's optional calendar point (third time, counting section 2). The "list is the home view" echoes sections 2 and 6.

## 11. Falling behind (part 1)
1. If a week ends with unfinished work, everything after it moves later. The right rail shows two dates for the current Pathway: target (when it should finish given weekly hours) and pace (when progress says it will finish). The gap in days is "Ahead of target". There is no streak.
2. Rereads:
- "When a week ends with work left over, every Action Item that follows moves later." Headline is a consequence, not a design point. "Moves later" in which sense?
- "Research shows that a small weekly set keeps people motivated. Keeping the user focused on their current Pathway target rather than a longer term goal enhances user motivation." This is a non sequitur to the headline and repeats section 4's logic. Also "Pathway target" is used before being defined three lines down.
- "The date this Pathway should be done by, set from the hours the person gives each week. It holds steady through an ordinary week and moves when those hours change." Good, finally a definition, but "ordinary week" is vague.
- "Where the person's progress says they will finish. It updates once a week, so nobody watches it tick with every finished item. In week one it matches the target." Clear enough.
- "Label Ahead of target: The gap between the two dates, in days." What is the label when they are behind? Not stated. Then "It is the only ahead or behind signal on the screen."
- "The Action Hub's right rail shows..." First time I hear of a right rail. There is no picture.
3. Terms: Pathway target, Pathway pace (defined here for the first time, after being used in sections 2 and 4), Ahead of target. Rail.
4. Contradiction: section 4 said metrics like target and pace are kept out of the weekly view on purpose. Section 11 puts them on the Action Hub's right rail, the weekly screen. At least for the Pathway versions, but the reader will trip. Also "Research shows" again for the fifth time. Section title "Falling behind" is split into two parts with the same headline, which reads as a mistake.

## 12. Falling behind (part 2)
1. Unfinished Action Items roll over to the front of next week so nobody has to catch up manually. Rolled-over items eat into next week's capacity, pushing others out, which causes a domino effect that makes pace dates drift later than target.
2. Rereads:
- Headline is identical to part 1. I thought it was a duplicate render.
- "Label Rollover: An Action Item still open when the week ends on Saturday night." Not a sentence, and it is a definition of the item, not of the term "rollover" as a process. "Saturday night" is the first mention that the week ends on Saturday.
- "A week that comes up short can easily feel like failing, and that feeling makes people less likely to keep going. So nobody has to make up lost time: an unfinished Action Item moves to the front of the next week." Good, but "nobody has to make up lost time" contradicts the next sentences, where lost time does cascade.
- "The rollover takes some of those hours, so some of that week's Action Items will get pushed to the next week which creates a domino effect to the rest of the user's weeks. Meaning their pace dates gets pushed out as well and pushing their pace dates later than their targets." Very long, grammar errors ("dates gets", "pushing ... pushing"), and it ends on a fragment. Hardest sentence in the piece.
3. Terms: Rollover (defined here, but already appeared in section 10's "week has no room left" and will appear in section 14), capacity (re-explained as "the hours the person set aside"), pace dates (plural), domino effect.
4. Contradiction: "nobody has to make up lost time" versus "pushes pace later". It is framed as kind but is a penalty that cascades. The designer's claim of avoiding failure feeling is undercut by showing a growing red gap. Also repeats the research and failure framing from 11.

## 13. Buffer flow
1. When a user finishes the week's items early, a screen celebrates and offers next week's items to pull forward. Pulling work forward is the only way to get ahead of the target or recover after a short week. The copy changes depending on whether the user is ahead or behind.
2. Rereads:
- "Finish the week's work early and the days that are left become room to catch up or to get further ahead." Fine, but "buffer" is never defined, and the title "Buffer flow" does not appear in the text.
- "A week only holds the hours a person gave, so finishing early leaves nothing left to do. Pulling work forward is the only way to move the pace ahead of the target, or back toward it after a short week." Hard. "Leaves nothing left to do" and "pull forward" are in tension; also "a short week" is undefined (is it a week of rollover?).
- "So the words follow the gap. Someone ahead is told they can stretch it. Someone still behind gets the finish celebrated too, then hears this is how they close the gap." "The words follow the gap" is cryptic. "Stretch it" has no referent.
- "It can't wait for the next week. How much someone can pull forward depends on the days left in this one." "It" is unclear (the pulling forward? the screen?). Out of order logically.
3. Terms: Buffer flow, pull forward, "the gap" (I learned it in 11), "stretch". Pull forward is understood here from context.
4. Reverses the logic of 11 and 12 (behind versus ahead). No contradiction, but the order feels odd because the user is introduced to catching up before rollover is fully explained.

## 14. Start of week
1. The first screen each week is a recap and plan, and it changes depending on the situation. Five templates: Clean slate, Catching up, Rollover, Recalibration, Reset. Each handles a different state of how last week went.
2. Rereads:
- "Whether someone finished last week early or fell short, the next week starts with a recap and a plan for the week." Fine.
- "If they're on track, it says less. If they're struggling to stay on target, it offers ways to catch up." "Struggling" is a loaded word and it is not aligned with the no-failure framing in 12.
- Template 1: "A clean slate only means something if no day was lost, so a week with a lost day goes to rollover." "Lost day" is undefined (a lost day of what?). Hard.
- Template 2: "Finishing every Action Item doesn't close the gap for someone behind, so this screen shows the days they won back and points to finishing early, until they catch up." Hard. "Won back" is not explained.
- Template 3: "under a magenta tag" The only visual detail in the whole piece, and it is a color.
- Template 4: "Rollover handles one unfinished week. When it keeps happening, the screen suggests a capacity that fits the hours someone has been giving. They can take it, set their own or skip it with a warning." "Skip it with a warning" is ambiguous. What warning?
- Template 5: "so the week rebuilds from today with progress kept and new target dates. It has one button, because a keep my plan option would win by default." Interesting design reasoning, but "win by default" is cryptic, and there is no definition of "long absence".
3. Terms: Recalibration, Reset, Clean slate, "lost day". Capacity is reused and now makes sense. This section is where most of the earlier terms finally converge.
4. Overlaps with 11, 12 and 13 (rollover, catching up, pace). Section 14 is the strongest design content but it repeats the model. The "Start of week" screen is also described as "the first screen someone sees", which contradicts section 6, where the Action Hub is the "starting point". Which is the first screen?

## Section order that felt wrong
- Section 2 (competitive analysis) comes before I know what Mondai does or what a Growth Journey, target or pace date is. It is a justification for decisions I have not seen yet. Move it after the main flow or fold each card into the relevant screen.
- Sections 3, 4 and 5 are three passes at the same hierarchy. Merge them.
- Section 8 (capacity and cognitive load) should come before 6 and 10, because "capacity" and "week's hours" are assumed in the Action Hub, target date and rollover. It currently sits in the middle with no screen attached.
- Section 11 (target and pace definitions) should come far earlier, right where those dates are first used in section 2 and 4.
- Section 12 (rollover) should come before 13 and 14 but the term "rollover" is already used in 10.
- Section 7 (Action item) comes after section 6 even though section 6 uses Action Items heavily. I never learn what an Action Item is until 7, and then only what it lacks.
- The hero should say what the case study is about and what the designer's role was, and it does not.

## Three biggest things stopping me understanding the designer's work
1. Vocabulary arrives out of order and is overloaded. Growth Journey, Pathway, Focus Area, Action Item, target, pace, capacity, rollover and "week's progress" are used for many sections before they are explained. Target and pace, the core of the whole system, are used in section 2, and not properly defined until section 11, nine sections later.
2. I rarely see the designer making a decision with a tradeoff. Most sections describe how the product works, as a spec, or cite a study. Section 10 (no automatic moving, never write to the calendar), section 14 template 5 (one button) and section 7 (loose containers) are the only places I see judgement. The rest lacks alternatives considered, constraints and results.
3. There is no visual or concrete anchor in the text, and the evidence stack is research citations rather than the designer's work. I cannot tell what the screens look like (right rail, magenta tag, containers), what Mondai is as a product, who the users are, or what changed because of the design. There is no outcome or reflection, and the hero and ending are placeholders.

## Could I say what this designer actually did?
Only partly. I can say the designer designed a weekly planning flow for an AI-generated career curriculum: a home hub, a long-term journey page, a pathway page, an item page, an optional calendar schedule, and a set of "start of week" states for falling behind. They obviously thought about motivation, and avoiding guilt mechanics (no streaks, no auto-rescheduling, a single-button reset). What I cannot say is their actual scope (the "I" appears only occasionally, with "we" elsewhere), who the users were, what research they did themselves versus borrowed from papers, how it was validated, what shipped, and what the outcome was. I would not advance them on this page alone: it reads as a well-thought-through spec with a good point of view on motivation, but the person's contribution and impact are not visible.
