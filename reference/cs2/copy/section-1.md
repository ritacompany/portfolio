## Block 1

Eyebrow: Before the framework

Headline: The product had useful pieces, but nothing joined them into a plan someone changing careers could follow.

There was a curriculum of career paths, an onboarding quiz, career pages and a plan to sync calendars.
Each piece had been designed on its own and then made to fit the rest. No part of it showed someone
changing careers what to do next, or how their work would add up to a new career.

## Block 2

Eyebrow: No direct reference

Headline: The product was meant to guide people, and no course or task app did that, so there was nothing to copy.

A bootcamp has a curriculum and holds you to its timetable. This was closer to a guide, which shows the way and lets
the person set the speed. Task apps fit the work, but a checklist doesn't lead anywhere.
Many people changing careers work full time, so whatever guided them had to fit around their lives, not
the other way round.

## Block 3

Eyebrow: Borrowed and left

Headline: Each of a few products taught me something, and I kept only the parts that fit a guide for career changers.

Card: CareerFoundry | Takeaway | Two dates side by side show if you are on track: the date the plan says you will finish and the date you will finish at your current pace.
Card: CareerFoundry | Why | Studies find that tracking real progress against a goal helps people reach it, most of all when that progress is written down. So the pace date is worked out from the tasks each person actually finishes.
Card: Learning apps | Takeaway | Every learning app I reviewed, even the most structured, let people work at their own pace.
Card: Learning apps | Why | Research shows that having a say in how you work raises motivation and effort, and lack of time is the most common thing stopping adults who want to learn. So each person decides when to do the work.
Card: Motion | Takeaway | Motion showed me that AI could look at someone's calendar and find time for their career tasks.
Card: Motion | Why | A study of more than 18,000 online learners found that pushing people to schedule their study time did not help them stick with it. So finding free time in a calendar is there for people who want it, and the plan never asks anyone to schedule.
Card: Linear | Takeaway | Linear opens straight onto the work in front of you, so the product's first screen shows what to do now instead of a home page.
Card: Linear | Why | In a field test with over 9,000 people, breaking a yearly goal into smaller goals closer at hand raised the effort they gave by about 7 to 8 percent. So the first screen shows only the next steps, and the full path to a new career sits on its own page.
Card: Duolingo | Takeaway | Duolingo showed me how a daily streak can make one missed day feel like failure.
Card: Duolingo | Why | Studies find a visibly broken streak makes people less likely to come back, and being kind to yourself after a slip makes you try harder. So falling behind only moves the pace date, and the main screen never shows a streak.

None of them did the whole job, so the framework was worked out from first principles. It started with
the smallest question, which unit of time a plan should run on.
