# Section 1: Hero

## Block 1

Eyebrow: Before the framework

Headline: The product had useful pieces, but no framework to join them into one plan a person could follow.

When I started there was a curriculum of career paths, an onboarding quiz, career pages and a plan to sync
calendars. Each piece had been designed on its own and then made to fit the rest. Nothing turned them into
one plan someone could follow from start to finish, so I had to build a framework that could hold all of them.

## Block 2

Eyebrow: What Mondai is

Headline: The product occupies a unique space, leaving no direct reference for us to draw from for the design.

Mondai is an AI career guide. It builds each person a route into a tech career from their goals and the time
they actually have. A bootcamp has a route but holds everyone to one timetable. A task app fits around your
life but doesn't lead anywhere. Mondai had to do both at once, and no product we could find did.

## Block 3

Eyebrow: Competitive analysis

Headline: Each of a few products taught me something, and I kept only the parts that fit our product's unique mission.

Card: 01. Progress | CareerFoundry | Studies find that tracking real progress against a goal helps people reach it. So, like CareerFoundry, Mondai shows two dates side by side: the target the plan sets and the pace date your progress is heading for.
Card: 02. Calendar integration | Motion | Motion showed me AI could find free time in someone's calendar. But a study of 18,000 learners found that pushing people to schedule didn't help them stick with it, so a connected calendar suggests where the work fits, and the plan still runs without one.
Card: 03. Home screen | Linear | Linear opens straight onto the work in front of you. Research on motivation finds near goals build confidence and progress while a distant goal does little, so the home screen shows only the next steps and the full Growth Journey, every step to the goal, has its own page.
Card: 04. Timing | Learning apps | Every learning app I reviewed, even the most structured, let people fit lessons around their day. Research shows having a say raises motivation and that learners with clear goals and plans are more likely to reach them, so the plan keeps a target and a pace while each person fits the work into their own day.


# Section 2: Background

## Block 1

Eyebrow: The unit of time

Headline: Not every life runs on a calendar, so the week became the unit.

Card: 01 | Per day | Tasks counted per day, worked back from a goal date.
Card: 02 | Calendar led | Too rigid for the people this is for.
Card: 03 | The week | Room inside it, pressure at its edge.

The people this is for don't have regular weeks. There was an opposite risk too. People need a little
pressure to get things done.

## Block 2

Eyebrow: Due Saturday night

Headline: Pressure at the edge of the week, freedom inside it.

Everything is due Saturday night. Inside the week, the work happens whenever life allows. Connect a
calendar and each Action Item, one piece of work, gets a suggested slot. Don't, and the week is a list. A
missed slot isn't a missed item.

Card: Research | Long goals | Nearer steps and seeing your own progress tend to help people keep going.

## Block 3

Eyebrow: Locks

Headline: The same line decides what gets locked.

Card: 01 | Past this week | Locked.
Card: 02 | Inside this week | Visible and can be moved.
Card: 03 | The order | Only controls what can be marked done.

The Pathways, the five stages of the journey, run in order, so the first proposal was to lock every
Action Item that wasn't available yet. I cut that back to the week.

## Block 4

Eyebrow: One level up

Headline: The product opens on the week, with one level above it: the current Pathway and whether
they're ahead or behind.

There was a case for showing people where they stood in the whole journey. A pace for the whole journey
would mostly show them how far they still had to go. Later I cut the journey level target and pace
altogether, because it made the picture more confusing.

## Block 5

Eyebrow: For looking

Headline: The whole journey has its own pages, and nothing can be checked off there.

That cost something. Pulling this week's items out on the Pathway page would have made it clearer, and I
hesitated. I left it, because the week is the one place work gets done and I wanted to keep it that way.

## Block 6

Eyebrow: Built for each person

Headline: The AI builds each person's Action Items from their onboarding answers.

So the week someone opens was put together for them. What it didn't know yet was how much time someone
actually had.


# Section 7: The Action Hub and the Growth Journey

## Block 1

Eyebrow: Before a career is chosen

Headline: An empty Action Hub invites the quiz.

Someone who has not taken the quiz opens an Action Hub with nothing in it yet, and an invitation to take the quiz and be matched with tech careers. A page that says it is still being built feels dishonest, as if that part of the product does not exist.

## Block 2

Eyebrow: Home base

Headline: The week is a list with times and a target.

The list view shows this week's Action Items with suggested times and weekly progress. The Pathway target and pace sit in the rail beside it, because they are the only anchor the weekly adjustments have. Each card shows its scheduled time, while the schedule view shows where the items sit among the week's events.

## Block 3

Eyebrow: No calendar

Headline: The list still works without a calendar.

Without a calendar, the same list shows a Schedule smarter prompt that offers to connect one. The view stays visible instead of disappearing. The idea for a week too packed to schedule was a plain list with no specific times, everything done by the end of the week.

## Block 4

Eyebrow: First time

Headline: A short tip explains the scheduled time.

An Action Item's time slot carries a tip that says Mondai picked the time from the person's availability, and that the icon reschedules it anytime. Tips appear only where they earn their place, and the first visit has to feel like a real start.

## Block 5

Eyebrow: The long view

Headline: The Growth Journey page holds the bigger goal.

The page shows a personalized summary, the journey target, journey pace, progress and the five Pathways. It holds the journey numbers because carrying both levels in one place was confusing. Capacity can be changed from here, since capacity moves the journey numbers.


# Section 8: Calendar integration

## Block 1

Eyebrow: First week

Headline: Calendar integration is offered, not forced.

In week one a person chooses to connect a calendar or manage their own schedule. The calendar step is written to represent a decision, with no forceful integration messaging.

## Block 2

Eyebrow: Boundaries

Headline: After sync, the person sets the window.

Once the calendar is synced, the person sets a preferred time frame and their sharpest hours, and Mondai suggests a range. The preferred times depend on the window they set, and the time bar version is the one that was picked.

## Block 3

Eyebrow: Moving things

Headline: A scheduled time is a suggestion that can be moved.

The schedule view lets a person move an Action Item to a new time. Scheduled times are recommendations and assistance, not due dates, because completion is decided at the end of the week. Mondai does not reschedule on its own, since that could get confusing fast.

## Block 4

Eyebrow: Edge cases

Headline: A packed calendar falls back to the plain list.

When the calendar is too full, a notice says it is packed this week and the scheduled times are removed. The idea was to switch that week to the non calendar approach, since a manual flow could be a lot of work for a fringe case. When a sync fails, a module over the list says the calendar lacks available times or could not sync.


# Section 11: Action Items

## Block 1

Eyebrow: Two pages

Headline: The Pathway page lists one Pathway at a time.

The Growth Journey stays the overview, and the Pathway page is where someone learns a single Pathway. It lists that Pathway's Focus Areas and Action Items, with breadcrumbs back to the journey page.

## Block 2

Eyebrow: Sequence

Headline: Focus Areas run in order, so Action Items do too.

Focus Areas turned out to be linear, so the Action Items progress through them in order instead of sitting under a sorting tag. They are numbered on the page, a rail marks each one Complete, Current or Not started, and the Action Items in later Focus Areas are locked.
