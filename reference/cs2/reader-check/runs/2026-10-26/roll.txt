=== Rollover, version 1
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Rollover
[headline] Unfinished Action Items move into next week on their own, and the week stays the size the person said they could give.
Because each week is filled to the person's capacity, leftovers take the place of new work instead of piling on top of it.
So a person used to four Action Items still opens a week that fits, not a week of six with two marked as leftovers.
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Nothing to sort
Nobody is asked what to do with each leftover. That choice would arrive at the moment they are already behind and turn the week into math.

=== Rollover, version 2
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Rollover
[headline] Plenty of weeks will not go to plan, so unfinished work moves into next week without the person having to do anything.
Missing a step toward a long goal raises the risk of giving up on it, so where leftovers go is a motivation problem.
Next week is built from the hours the person gave, with leftovers counted first, so the week never gets heavier.
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Carried, not late
Rolled over items carry a soft magenta tag rather than a warning color, so a leftover reads as carried forward rather than overdue.
Source: Rai, Sharif, Chang, Milkman and Duckworth 2022

=== Rollover, version 3
[eyebrow or sub-heading] Rollover
[headline] Leftover work had two bad options, asking the person to sort it or moving it silently, and sizing each week to capacity removed the need for either.
Asking turns a bad week into a list of decisions. Moving work silently would surprise someone used to four Action Items with a week of six.
Because every Action Item carries a time estimate and every week is filled to the person's capacity, leftovers take the place of new work.
[eyebrow or sub-heading] What sizing does not solve
Sizing keeps each week the right size, but a run of hard weeks can still build a backlog.
