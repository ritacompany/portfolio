VERSION V1: Falling behind, version A
  (label) Falling behind
V1.S1 Every plan built on weeks has to decide what happens when one of them doesn't get finished, and that answer shapes every week after it.
V1.S2 Say Sally had four Action Items this week and finished three.
V1.S3 Her Pathway target doesn't move, because it only changes when the hours she gives change.
V1.S4 A target that slid with every bad week would stop meaning anything.
V1.S5 When her next week starts, her pace moves to show the later finish.
V1.S6 The unfinished Action Item is already in the new week, and the week takes fewer new ones, so it still fits her hours.
V1.S7 Three mechanisms keep a short week from turning into a pile up.
  (label) Buffer flow
V1.S8 When someone finishes their week early, a screen opens that lets them catch up or get ahead.
  (label) Start of week
V1.S9 Every new week opens on a recap of where the person stands, after leftovers have moved.
  (label) Capacity
V1.S10 After a run of short weeks, the plan looks at their progress and suggests fewer hours.
V1.S11 People can change it in settings too.

VERSION V2: Falling behind, version B
  (label) Falling behind
V2.S1 When a week ends with work unfinished, the target, the pace and the next week all react, so each one got a rule for how far it can move.
V2.S2 The rule I set is that one short week changes as little as possible.
V2.S3 Take Sally, with four Action Items this week and three finished.
V2.S4 Her target stays where it was, since only a change in her hours moves it.
V2.S5 Her pace moves once, when the new week starts.
V2.S6 The unfinished Action Item carries over on its own, and the week takes fewer new ones so it still fits her hours.
V2.S7 Anything bigger than one week is handled at three moments.
  (label) When a week ends early
V2.S8 A screen called the buffer flow opens the moment every Action Item is done, so the extra time can go to catching up or getting ahead.
  (label) When a new week starts
V2.S9 Start of week opens on a recap of where the person stands.
  (label) When weeks keep slipping
V2.S10 The plan looks at their progress and suggests a lower capacity, which they can also change in settings.

VERSION V3: Falling behind, version C
  (label) Falling behind
V3.S1 Falling behind one week can't be allowed to grow into a pile of work, so I decided how every part of the plan responds when a week comes up short.
V3.S2 Sally had four Action Items this week and finished three.
V3.S3 Nothing about her goal changes.
V3.S4 Her target only moves when her hours do, so one short week can't push it.
V3.S5 What changes is small and expected.
V3.S6 When the new week starts, her pace shows the later finish, and the unfinished Action Item is already in the new week, which takes fewer new ones so it still fits.
V3.S7 Three mechanisms answer what she'd want to know next.
  (label) What if the week ends early?
V3.S8 A screen called the buffer flow opens with the chance to catch up or get ahead.
  (label) Where does she stand now?
V3.S9 Each new week opens on a recap called start of week, after leftovers have moved.
  (label) What if weeks keep slipping?
V3.S10 The plan looks at her progress and suggests fewer hours, and she can change them in settings too.
