## The schedule view A: as decisions, pairs with Rollover A
Covers all points: two views, how times are placed, block size, the time is theirs, two clocks, passed times, one way sync

Headline: People who sync their calendar also get the schedule view, which shows where each of their Action Items lands in their real week.

Image: Aisha's week as a calendar opening at 9AM, the start time she set in setup, her own events and Action Items side by side, the List and Schedule switch visible.
Image: A block clicked: the popover, then the reschedule menu with a new time picked.
Image: The card after its time passed: the time gone, the item still open.

I kept the list as the home of the Action Hub, because calendar sync is optional and the plan has to work without it. People who sync get the schedule view on top of it, one switch away.
Mondai places each Action Item using the two answers from setup. It looks for gaps between the person's events inside the hours they prefer and fills their focus hours first. Each block's length also shows how big that Action Item is, which nothing else on screen does.
The time belongs to the person, and they can move it from either view. Moving times automatically was easy to build. I left it out because a plan that rearranges itself gets confusing fast.
So each Action Item has two clocks, a time on Tuesday and the deadline on Saturday night. I made the week the one that counts. The times are there to help someone find room, and a due date on every item would only add pressure. When a time passes, it drops off the card and the item stays open. Nothing is marked missed, because Mondai can't see whether the work happened.
We kept the sync one way. Mondai reads the calendar and never writes into it, because the calendar holds commitments Mondai doesn't own.

## Rollover A: as decisions, pairs with Schedule view A
Covers all points: the stakes, three options weighed, the decision, the carried tag, target and pace, into the week flow

Headline: So what happens to the Action Items someone doesn't get to by Saturday night? They have to go somewhere.

Image: Next week's Action Hub list with a carried item at the top in its soft magenta tag.
Image: The Pathway target and pace before and after the week: the target the same, the pace a few days later.

Missing a step on a long goal is one of the moments people give up, and for Mondai that means losing a user. So where unfinished Action Items go decides whether someone keeps going.
I weighed three options. Moving everything into next week as extra work makes a heavier week right after a bad one. Asking the person to sort each item hands them decisions while they're behind. Dropping them lets the Pathway slip without anyone saying so.
I chose to carry leftovers into next week automatically without making the week bigger. A carried item gets a soft magenta tag instead of a warning color, because seeing a lapse makes people less likely to come back.
The Pathway target stays put and the pace moves once a week, so a carried item shows up as a later finish date. If weeks keep slipping, the plan looks at the person's progress and suggests changes. That is where the week flow starts.
Sources: Rai, Sharif, Chang, Milkman and Duckworth 2022; Silverman and Barasch 2023

## Rollover C: how the parts connect, pairs with Schedule view C
Covers all points: the stakes, three options weighed, the decision, the carried tag, target and pace, into the week flow

Headline: Every week ends with some Action Items still open, and where they go touches the person's next week, their pace and whether they keep going.

Image: Saturday evening: the Action Hub with two Action Items still open.
Image: Sunday morning: the new week with those two at the top in the magenta carried tag.
Image: The Pathway pace before and after the week, moved a few days.

Rollover is where the other weekly decisions meet. The week is the only deadline, so Saturday night is when unfinished work has to go somewhere. It is also the moment people are most likely to give up on a long goal.
Each week is sized to the hours a person gave, so leftovers can't sit on top of new work without making a heavier week after a bad one. Asking the person to sort them would land while they're behind, and dropping them would let the Pathway slip. So they carry forward on their own and the week stays its usual size.
The pace already tracks progress, so it absorbs the miss. The target holds and the pace moves once a week. Carried items get a soft magenta tag instead of a warning color, because seeing a lapse makes people less likely to come back. When weeks keep slipping, the plan looks at progress and suggests changes, which starts the week flow.
