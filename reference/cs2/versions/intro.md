## Intro B: one user's week

Angle: a worked example. One user with 6 hours, followed through a week, so the reader sees the whole
system before any of it is named.
Structure: example first, his sentence second, the week told as cause and effect, my role last; right column
as one sentence.

Eyebrow: Mondai MVP
Title: A career plan that still works in a week when the user only has 6 hours
Role line: Role: Founding designer / Affiliate: Mondai / Focus: Design, research and strategy.
Body: Say the user can give 6 hours a week. Mondai is an AI tech career navigator designed for the next
generation of hustlers, and its AI has already written their whole route into tech, far more than the user
could finish in a week. So each week they get only what fits in those 6 hours, due by Saturday night and
placed around their own calendar if they choose to connect it. If something is still open when Saturday
comes, it moves into the next week on its own, and that week takes on less so it still fits. If they finish
early, they can pull next week's work forward. I designed the framework behind all of this for Mondai's MVP.
Right column:
This case study is focused on how the curriculum is organized, how each week is sized to the user's hours and
how the plan adjusts when a week runs short or ends early.

## Intro C: the far off finish line

Angle: the human problem. A goal months away does little for motivation, and real weeks fall short.
Structure: problem title, his sentence, two problems with their reasons, the design answer; right column as
the three questions the page answers.

Eyebrow: Mondai MVP
Title: Keeping a user moving toward a career that is still months away
Role line: Role: Founding designer / Affiliate: Mondai / Focus: Design, research and strategy.
Body: Mondai is an AI tech career navigator designed for the next generation of hustlers. Its AI writes each
user a full curriculum, and the end of it is often months away, which research suggests does little to keep
the user going from week to week. The user also has a life the plan doesn't control, so some weeks they'll
fall short. I designed the framework for Mondai's MVP around both problems, so the user is always looking at
a week they can finish and a short week doesn't feel like failing.
Right column:
This case study is focused on three questions
How do you show a long route without putting the finish line in front of the user every week?
How much should one week hold, and who decides?
What happens when a week comes up short or ends early?
