# The map block and Falling behind, round 1 (3 Nov)

Two blocks together, written against his portfolio frame 4269:9433 after the page order was applied there
(Personalized timeline before the map; Growth Journey page and Action Item page placeholders added).
The map is his idea and he makes the visual, so each map version carries an image note describing it.
Falling behind is the short lead in: no cards, no Sally, in his bridge layout.

Points every map version carries, with the record behind them (reasoning/capacity.md,
reasoning/carry-over.md, reasoning/THROUGHLINES.md section 2):
1. Capacity is the one number a person sets; everything else comes from it.
2. Why hours and not a fixed weekly load: a fixed load suits neither someone fitting this around a job nor
   someone giving it forty hours (his correction of the cap model, 7 May).
3. The week is assembled to that size in order, by effort, so it never weighs more than the person said.
4. The hours set six dates, five Pathway targets and the journey target; only the hours move them.
5. The pace is the one date that comes from what the person did, so the gap is said against done.
6. Where hours change (journey target, start of week, settings) and why the control lives with the
   journey: a new number moves every date in it (his ownership argument, 19 May).
7. A change applies next week unless the person picks now; dates update on save, not while typing, because
   the screen should keep the plan's weekly rhythm (17 and 19 May).

Points every Falling behind version carries: the question every weekly plan meets; why that moment matters
(a lapse read as failure makes people less likely to keep going, Soman and Cheema 2004, via
reports/Schedule view how it works.md); the plan answers it with rules for leftover work and for finishing
early.

## Map B: said and done

Covers: 1 one number, 2 hours over a fixed load, 3 week built to size, 4 six dates, 5 pace from what was done, 6 where hours change and why, 7 next week default and update on save
Eyebrow: How it connects
Headline: Every date in the plan comes from one of two places, what a person says they can give each week and what they actually finish.
Text: What they say is capacity, the hours per week, and it is the only number anyone sets. I picked it over a fixed weekly load, which would suit neither someone squeezing this around a job nor someone giving it forty hours.
Text: The hours build the week, with Action Items added in order until their effort reaches the hours, so it never weighs more than the person promised.
Text: The hours also set six targets, one for each of the five Pathways and one for the journey, so a change in hours is the only thing that moves any of them.
Text: What they finish sets the pace, so the gap between pace and target is the difference between what they did and what they said.
Side: I put the hours on the journey's target, as well as at the start of a week and in settings, because a new number moves every date in the journey. It starts next week unless the person picks now, and nothing moves until they save, so the screen keeps the plan's weekly rhythm.
