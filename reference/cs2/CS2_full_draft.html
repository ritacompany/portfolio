The reframe

Mondai gives people a structured path into a tech career. The structure was never the hard part.

Its users chase that career around lives that are already full and rarely the same two weeks running. They do not need less structure. They need structure that fits into a life like that, not one they have to rebuild their life around.

The original direction was fixed scheduling and keep-pace accountability, the model traditional education runs on. I pushed for a different kind, because a fixed cadence assumes a student with open, regular time, and that is not who these users are.

The reframe

The evidence
This came from the field, not from taste.
Fixed-cohort programs

One timetable, one pace. Miss a week and you are behind the group with no way back in.

Falls off real lives
Open course libraries

Total flexibility, no spine. Nothing decides what comes next, so people drift and quit.

No direction
Structured but self-paced

A clear path you move through at your own speed. The model that actually holds this audience.

The survivors
The pattern held every time. What lasts gives structure you move through at your own pace, not a schedule you fall off of. That was the target.
One input
1 number
The entire system runs on one thing the user gives us: how many hours a week they actually have.

Capacity is the only scheduling input. The target date, the weekly plan, the pace, the way a bad week gets absorbed, all of it is derived from that one number. Declared by the user, not observed by the system, because observed capacity means two or three weeks with no real plan to offer. Declared means a real plan on day one.

Planning fallacy: people overestimate their own time, so the capacity screen uses honest framing and manual entry, never pre-filled buckets, and previews on a button press instead of recalculating on every keystroke, which had felt like a slot machine.
The system
One decision forces the next. That is the whole design.
01

Capacity is the only input so

Every other number in the system is downstream of it. Which means the system can commit to a plan on day one and revise it honestly later.

02

The Target locks so

A real commitment date. It moves only through deliberate recalibration. A locked target makes a bad week visible, and visible drift is what pulls people back. A rolling target would just hide the slip.

Loss aversion
03

The Pace moves so

It updates once a week, never per task. Per-task updates scatter the signal across the week. Weekly concentrates it into one moment the user can actually attend to.

04

Rollover stays silent so

Unfinished work carries into the new week automatically. No modal, no math, no decision on the worst possible day. The system carries the weight of what to bring forward, not the user.

05

The week needs states

Five of them, because being behind and being absent are different problems, and each needs a different response. That is the next section.

Move one piece and nothing crashes. It is the principle that breaks. Make rollover a decision and the product quietly becomes a gatekeeper again. The coupling is not fragility, it is coherence. Every part descends from the same answer.

Start of week
Every Sunday, the week opens in one of five states.
Clean
On track or ahead
No rollover, no flags. The plan is holding.
Catching Up
Behind but moving
Closed last week clean, still behind pace, trending the right way.
Rollover
Work carried in
Items followed you here. Not an error, so the headline stays neutral.
Recalibration
Capacity mismatch
Weeks of finishing under capacity. The plan offers to right-size, no penalty.
Reset
You were gone
Long absence. Re-bundles from today, progress intact. You disappeared and we are not mad.
One shared element across all five: a single pace-movement line, so the user always sees whether the distance is closing or slipping. Goal gradient and the progress principle.

The AI, on purpose
For this audience, how the product uses AI is a trust decision, not a feature.
Contextual AI

Adjustment knobs

Scoped to a single action item. Preset chips and a tag input, no chat box, no history.

  • Lives inside one task
  • Single transformation, then done
  • Never a conversation
Rita

The guide

Journey-level and persistent. A conversational surface for the whole path.

  • Full text and memory
  • Product-wide scope
  • Its own space, always
The two never share a container. The moment a user sees a text field, the model flips to “I am talking to someone.” Keeping them visually and spatially distinct is what protects that.

Onboarding explains the AI honestly. After that, recommendations are attributed to the reasoning, not to a label. Based on your interest in UX design and your graphic background, not AI-generated suggestion.

Research shows AI labels reduce trust, and for this audience, where most surveyed Black women believe AI is used to surveil them, concealment discovered would be worse. So the product discloses once and demonstrates always. And the AI only ever recommends. The person can always override. For these users that is the position the whole product takes.

How I worked
Process · design in the AI era

The wireframe used to be where I did my thinking. Now it is where I commit.

I explore and compare directions before committing a frame, so by the time something becomes a wireframe it has already been pressure-tested against its alternatives. That widened the option space. It did not just make me converge faster.

I do not use AI to decide what good looks like. Only to apply what was already decided, and I throw out the passes that miss.

How it holds up, and how it gets proven

The product has not launched, so I am not going to show you a usability report that does not exist. Here is the validation that is real, and the plan for the validation that is next.

What validated it so far
  • Founder and stakeholder review at every major decision
  • Structured design critique, which is where the kills came from
  • A behavioral-evidence base used to defend each call, not decorate it
  • Systems modeling of the dependencies before any change shipped
The validation plan, designed
  • Hypothesis: users understand rollover with zero explanation
  • Hypothesis: the locked target drives return after a slip
  • Measure: comprehension, re-entry after a missed week, perceived pressure
  • Protocol: moderated runs of the weekly flow across low and high capacity users
