## The draft

### The week [spends: the week, Action Hub, Action Item]

A Pathway is a long stretch of that route. What reaches the user is one week of it.

The Action Hub is that week. It holds the work that is live now, as Action Items, each one a discrete
piece of work with a page of its own. The Growth Journey page holds everything else, the whole route and
every item not started yet, for anyone who wants to see how far it goes.

Two surfaces instead of one. Seeing all of it is something a user goes looking for. The week is what
opens.

None of it is written in advance. The onboarding quiz feeds a model that builds the curriculum for that
person, so the week someone opens has been put together for them and for nobody else.

Finishing an item is one tap on its circle, and it can be taken back.

## Decisions taken in this rung, and why

**No count of items per week.** The evidence says three or four. Stating a number here would answer the
question rung 4 exists to answer, which is how the amount is worked out from the time someone actually
has. Rung 3 should leave the reader asking how much. Rung 4 answers it.

**The two-surface split is the design decision, not a psychological claim.** An earlier version of this
rung was going to say that showing a whole career at once makes people quit. That is exactly the move he
rejected at rung 0, diagnosing the user rather than describing the environment, and it is unsourced.
What is sourced is the split itself, in his words: "Where the Action Hub is where the weekly framework
exists, the Journey page is where the user can get the higher level view of their entire journey, view
future tasks." The split is the evidence. It does not need a claim about psychology attached.

**Generation lands here as one sentence.** Source, his words: "the curriculum (action items) are
specifically designed based on the user's input during the onboarding quiz. the ai creates the curriculum
by referencing those inputs." Rung 8 gets the full treatment. Placing one sentence here means the reader
is never surprised by it later, and it is doing real work in this rung: it is why the week can be sized
to a person at all.

**One line on completing an item.** His note: it would be odd not to mention what actions happen with the
Action Items. One sentence covers it and buys the B2 visual, which carries the interaction model far
better than prose would. The full state machine, the undo confirmation and the to-do case stay out of the
prose.

**"Locked items may change" stays out.** It is true, and it is the caveat he required on the Journey
page, but it opens recalibration five rungs early. It belongs at rung 7.
