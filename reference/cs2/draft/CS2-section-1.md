### A curriculum with no way through it

Mondai already had a curriculum. Each career became a Growth Journey: five Pathways in a fixed order
(Discovery, Cognition, Networking, Branding and Opportunity), each broken into Action Items. Around it sat
an onboarding quiz, career selection and a plan to connect people's calendars.

None of it said how a person moves through that curriculum inside the product. What does someone see
first? What happens next?

### What it had to hold

It had to carry a curriculum that runs in order. It had to act like a guide that adapts to someone's life,
rather than a course that holds them to its schedule. Michael wanted structure, the kind that gets people
to show up, with the calendar at the center of it. And the people using it don't have regular weeks. Some
have a predictable schedule, some don't, and the same person can move between the two.

So it needed structure and room to move in the same system.

### Nothing to copy

I looked at the products closest to it, and each solved one piece. CareerFoundry runs a fixed curriculum
at a fixed pace. Fall behind and the curriculum doesn't move for you. I kept its order of prerequisites and
left the pacing. Duolingo makes a missed day visible without punishing it. Your streak resets and you carry
on. I kept that. Notion and Todoist are flexible with no structure at all. I took the calendar as a planning
layer and left the blank page.

Mapped by how structured they are against how flexible, none of them sat where Mondai needed to be. The
framework had to be reasoned out piece by piece.
