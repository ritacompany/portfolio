### A week, not a day

The first version counted tasks per day, worked back from a goal date. We settled on a week instead, for
two reasons. A week works whether or not someone connects a calendar. And it gives people structure.
Michael wanted them to feel a little pressure to get things done, and a week is a container that
pressure can live in.

At one point the weekly structure was nearly cut. I argued to keep it, because the Hub, the calendar and
the rest of the product were built on it.

### One week, two ways to hold it

Some people connect a calendar, and each Action Item, one piece of work, gets placed in an open slot.
Others get a plain list for the week. It is the same week either way. A scheduled time is a suggestion.
The work is due when the week ends on Saturday night, so a missed slot is not a missed item.

### Where the week lives

The Action Hub is the week: what is live now, as Action Items. The Pathway pages show the whole route and
everything still to come, for anyone who wants to look ahead. Work happens in the Hub. I kept progress
controls off the Pathway pages, even where pulling this week's items out there would have read more
clearly, so the overview never became a second place to work.

None of it is written by hand. The AI builds each person's Action Items from their onboarding answers,
so the week someone opens was put together for them.

What the week did not say yet was how much it should hold.
