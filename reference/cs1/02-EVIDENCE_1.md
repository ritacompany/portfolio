## The part-time volunteer reality (core theme, underused)
- Team of about 5 to 6 designers, volunteers, 2 to 20 hours a week, very different career stages.
- Managing that like a staffed team does not work. This is the reason the systems had to exist. Weave through, do not isolate.

## The founder dialogue and the weekly framework
- Calendar integration was the founder's original idea. Feature-first design gave it no foundation to sit in.
- A series of ideation sessions, two real and conflicting perspectives: founder wanted structure and accountability, Chadwick worried rigid structure would hurt retention and add stress to a tool meant to reduce it. Resolution was a framework that provides structure while staying a guide. Tested, barely needed explaining, that was the version that stuck.
- Best-voiced version is Chadwick's own rewrite in 04. Needs condensing and a "wow" operating moment (his ask).
- The founder's recorded reply thanking him / crediting him with clarity exists in Notion (QN1). Pull verbatim if used.

## Building the systems (the operational heart)
- Review system, recorded, built on criteria not fixes, because a fix improves one screen and a criterion improves every screen after. Walks designers through changes so the why is never a mystery. Wants to understand their reasoning and research. (Chadwick's own draft in 04.)
- Approval pipeline change: design to Chadwick to founder to dev, changed so dev could start before the founder's final approval, because the founder trusted his calls and rarely changed anything. Cut handoff time roughly in half, product shipped faster, designs were half-built by the time they reached the founder. (Chadwick's own draft in 04. Strong, concrete, currently missing from the draft.)
- Decision / blocker tracker in Notion, carried a status for anything blocked on input from outside design, so stalls were visible and owned. (Chadwick's own draft in 04.)
- Terminology board, additions by approval, later handed to content. (Documented.)
- Documented layout standard living in the design file, exploration split from delivery, review states, an accessibility audit gate, named sign-off. (Documented, well-drafted in cs1-v5 section 6, salvageable.)
- OPEN: is the blocker tracker the same Notion board as the scoring model? Resolve with Chadwick so it does not read as two boards.

## Ceremonies
- Two weekly meetings: a full-team meeting of 25 to 30 across engineering, product, strategy where he is the primary design voice surfacing blockers and advocating decisions, and the design team's own weekly (10 to 15 min stand-up then a core topic, often a workshop). (Chadwick's own draft in 04.)
- Workshops on design systems, components, variables, auto layout, run when nobody felt confident and it was causing hours of rework. (Documented.)
- A real full-team meeting screenshot exists (Chadwick has it) and pairs with this material.

## Growing designers and the promotions
- Reviews teach criteria. Training built around how each designer says they learn. One matched to motion work for her strength and her own portfolio goals.
- Figma Make shared with the team the week it launched, with a note on free-tier limits so they would write thorough prompts. Two using it on live tasks within two weeks.
- 2026: three volunteers put forward for promotion, two to product designer two, one to senior accessibility designer grown from a standing start. All arrived with no formal role. Strongest emotional close available. (Documented.)

## AI as practice (rebuild to portfolio-tips Tip 06 and 08)
- Using AI for research and building compressed weeks into afternoons. Calendar-color cross-platform comparison brought as a recommendation not a hunch. Design handbooks written in an evening that replaced a week of clarifying questions and got three designers oriented before a meeting.
- The maintained Mondai design project fed with context and skills over time. This reset itself is an example of that practice.
- Boundary: does not use AI to decide what good looks like, only to apply what was already decided, throws out the passes that miss.
- Earlier documented uses: mapping task dependencies before roadmapping, structuring founder status updates, catching unanswered questions in threads.
