# CS1 causal chain

Built from a full chronological read of all 7,677 Discord messages, the 26 handwritten notes, Notion, the design system and 146 product screens, and the project files. Ordered by consequence rather than theme: each link caused the next.

Scope boundaries: CS2 owns the Growth Journey, scheduling, AI product strategy, hi-fi walkthrough and event KPIs. CS3 owns the design system. CS1 owns the operation.

---

## 1. Research operations, before there was a team (Nov 2023 to July 2024)

**Nov 2023, the first documented instinct.** On a bootcamp project, he pushes his partner past clustering stickies: "I worry that we won't actually be solving the problems for our users if we interpret everything on a super surface level." Process quality determines output quality. This is the whole case study in miniature, two years early.

**June 2024, he designs the research operation.** Interview groups reasoned out (industry insiders, newcomers at 6 months to 3 years "because they've had time to be reflective"). Interviews sequenced before surveys with five stated reasons. Recruiting via a LinkedIn post with TidyCal links split by availability. Inclusive screener language. Script QA from Allie.

**One of four stated research goals: "Understand how to integrate AI in the most human-centric way."**

## 2. The finding that set the constraint (July 2024)

Research surfaced two pain points that shaped everything after:
- **Lack of trust in AI**
- **Users prefer AI as a tool rather than the solution**

**July 25, 2024, he reframes it rather than accepting it:** "AI is still fresh, the perspective will change as time goes on and so will the AI itself. It's a great opportunity to build trust and loyalty with the user. Some kind of controlled transparency could be useful here... Honesty builds trust. I actually see this AI hesitation thing as a huge opportunity."

→ Consequence: the product could not be an opaque chatbot. Its logic had to be legible in the interface. That requirement is what made structure non-negotiable later.

## 3. Feature-first design, and the first ops artifacts (Aug to Sept 2024)

Team splits the product by feature (quiz, journey planner, career pages). Reasonable for an MVP.
- Aug 2024: a Figma frame titled "Michael's preferences," his first prototype of a stakeholder interface
- Sept 2024: builds the Design System page
- Sept 30, 2024: builds a Design Prioritization Board so the team can vote screens into the investor deck

## 4. The diagnosis (Oct 2024)

**Oct 9, to a teammate:** "The home page has been difficult for me to think about because of its relationship with the journey page, the fact that we worked so independently on our features and most importantly the underlying complexities of creating an **AI flexible product**. I think we could have more problems going forward if we just continue to design more features of the MVP and try to make things fit within the way we've designed things so far."

**Oct 10:** drafts the concern three times, worrying about overstepping.
**Oct 13:** sends to the full company. "It feels like we're sometimes adapting the architecture around the features, rather than having a structure that naturally supports them."

→ Consequence: structure goes on the roadmap. His job shifts from producing design to building what produces design. Note the causality: the AI requirement is *why* the architecture mattered.

## 5. Making decisions once (Jan to Mar 2025)

Each of these exists because something was being remade repeatedly.
- Component naming taxonomy worked out deliberately ("trying to save any potential confusion and create distinction between their roles")
- Cross-team tracker prototyped alone in Notion before anyone asked
- **Decision log (Mar 2025):** "tracking who made key decisions and why so that we can go back to those later before making any major changes"
- Question list with assignments after a meeting, so ambiguity becomes owned tasks
- A connective role designed around what that person actually wanted to do

## 6. The agency moment (Feb 2025)

**Feb 13:** "I'm struggling to figure out where I have agency to implement this in the way I believe will be most effective and instead often finding myself designing around it and getting stuck over and over... if we implement calendar integration in a way that is overly structured it feels like we are emulating the structured nature of traditional education. Aren't we the ones trying to give that type of shit a middle finger?"

Michael's answer, recorded in his notes: never feel like it's Michael's baby, challenge away.

**Mar 2025, he applies it immediately:** pushes back on the founder's task-pacing formula because "the formula doesn't take everything into account," then proposes weekly containers for two stated reasons: it works with or without calendar integration, and it creates structure for the user.

→ Consequence: flexible structure replaces rigid scheduling. (The mechanics belong to CS2. What belongs here is that a designer changed the product's operating logic by arguing for it.)

- May 21: uses ChatGPT to map task dependencies before sequencing. An early AI-assisted ops moment.
- May 23: roadmap and task list shipped org-wide, sub-teams directed to stand up weekly meetings
- June 12: terminology database launched as a collaborative board with an approval flow

## 8. The reorg and the pipeline (July 2025)

**July 16:** owns a timeline slip in a message to the founder.
**July 22, all caps:** "PLEASE IF POSSIBLE REVIEW THIS MESSAGE BELOW BEFORE TONIGHT... what I want to focus on here is creating a pipeline between design and dev if all dev is being moved to their own team. The pipeline I created for Web X takes design, strategy and dev all into consideration."

## 9. Capacity, and what it cost (Aug to Oct 2025)

- **Aug 28:** Dev Handoff file created. Same day, he asks every designer how many hours they actually have per week.
- **Aug 26:** "I basically told them I'm not the teacher handing out tasks."
- **Sept 2:** "I have an idea now of everyone's availability and it's clear to me we are working with a lot less hours than I expected... it means I'll need to reapproach how we accomplish the rest of the work. Which yes means more on my plate even than I expected but that's okay, I just want to get this thing out there."
- **Sept 17, the delegation crisis, stated:** "If it takes time to provide feedback, manage, mentor the same or more than it would take for me to do it then why don't I just do it?" His handwritten answer, the four points of delegation: direction-based versus assignment-based, and **"design infrastructure and have them fill in the rest."**
- **Sept 17, handling performance with dignity:** "Maybe I can go the route of asking her questions about her design decisions and figure out what aspects of her reasoning I can apply to what is ultimately used. I could say 'Sierra's input on this led me to make this change.'"
- **Sept 26:** the four-part status format to the founder: Pending on Your Feedback / Approvals Coming Soon / Thoughts on Structure and Personnel / Open Questions.
- **Oct 16:** the documented layout standard. "I hated having to write it out in this much detail, but for the sake of establishing a consistent and working system, it was necessary. Believe me, this approach will save us countless hours of rework."
- **Oct 22 to 30, the cost:** "A lot of my work right now is cleaning up the work of the other designers... I've been doing like 12-13 hour days. They're not fully solving problems, making their designs responsive or even fully using the design system in most cases so it has created a lot of work. **That's not on them.**"
- **Oct 23:** hardstop met.
- **Nov 11, the response:** "I think I'm going to start training and having Ayushi shadow me so she can get more involved and support more when my time becomes less available."
- **Nov 12:** training designed around the trainee. "I've asked her to start thinking about her own learning style, the best way for us to keep notes that will work with that."

## 10. Standards become shared infrastructure (Nov 2025 to Feb 2026)

- **Nov 28:** starts a design channel to keep the team synced on the system, immediately uses it to share Figma's built-in contrast checker and assign someone to research it
- **Nov 25:** runs the WCAG 3:1 check himself, then spots the system gap: "This reminds me, we need to define a hover state color for the icons"
- **Feb 16, 2026:** 2.5 hour end-to-end walkthrough with the dev lead, converted into a Notion Prioritization Dashboard with statuses (Needs Design, Awaiting Stakeholder, Ready for Dev) "plus how we're prioritizing using effort and value"
- **Feb 17:** the dashboard becomes the meeting agenda, and strategy answers the Awaiting Stakeholder items as a group

## 11. Prioritization becomes objective (Feb 2026)

The mechanism, from his own notes:
- MoSCoW combined with an impact matrix. **Product decides value, engineering defines effort.**
- Design value 1 to 10, dev effort 1 to 10, the pair determines the MoSCoW bucket
- High value + low effort = Must have. High value + high effort = Should have, plan carefully. Low value + low effort = Could have, fill-ins. Low value + high effort = Won't have, kill it.
- **Feb 24, blind rating:** "I went through and separated our Value/Effort ratings so we don't see the other's ratings as we rate based on our individual scales."
- **Feb 18, the intent to spread it:** "I'm hoping showing Sierra what we were doing will branch into her doing something similar for prioritization that'll allow us to improve cross functional tracking and collaboration."

→ Consequence: what design works on stops being a negotiation. Also the single best data-visualization opportunity in the study.

## 12. Judgment becomes executable (Mar 2026)

**The problem, Sept 30, 2025:** "I would get so frustrated with ChatGPT because it's not good at maintaining information from earlier in a conversation... the output will start missing things and it's impossible to keep track of." A tool that cannot hold a system is useless for system work.

**The solve, Mar 10, 2026:** "It's super smart on product design stuff, back and forth problem solving but like **informed** problem solving. Basically I fed it the entire rita design system by creating a PDF. Then we walked through different steps. It isn't PERFECT ofc and **still needs a designer's eye** but it's great at correcting and coming up with solutions."

**The boundary, in his words (QN3):** "Frankly I do not trust them to lead the design efforts by any means, the main purpose here is to come up with ideas for certain things like navigation in a way that I can explore how they feel."

**What AI actually did in the design work,** from his handwritten list: help solve the metric problem, define architecture and correct logic, find "up next" examples, work out shortcut logic, motion for selected and not-selected states, find career path examples.

**Earlier uses, all documented:** mapping task dependencies before roadmapping (May 2025), building the pathway design handbooks (Sept 2025), the calendar color comparison chart (Oct 2025), catching unanswered questions in threads (Sept 2025), structuring founder status updates (Sept 2025).

→ Consequence: two years of written standards turned out to be the input that made the tooling work. Documentation for humans became training data.

## 13. Setting direction (2026)

- Leadership group of four; he is one of three with prior leadership experience
- **Mar 16, 2026 leadership notes:** the org's now-default rule is "if we give designs early it needs to be an architecture first approach... a decision point from design where they say this wireframe structure is ready." The thing he flagged alone in year one is now the operating assumption.
- Renegotiates meeting architecture with the founder after 33 minutes of voice notes, on the record, resolved constructively
- Kills the traditional home screen for a "starting point" model, with Linear as the reference
- Proposes org structure changes twice, with reasoning attached
