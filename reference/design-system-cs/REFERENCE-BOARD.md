# Design system case study: reference board

Built 6 Nov 2026 for the writing session. For each section of the working outline: the two or three published pages to borrow from, exactly what to take (a structure, a visual or a line) and the tactic behind it, by id from `docs/about-source/case_study_tactics.html`. Quotes are copied from the live page text captured on 6 Nov 2026 and were checked against it by a separate reader. Every page in the board was opened in a browser and screenshotted, and its visuals were judged from the image, not from the text. Numbers are the authors' own claims; none could be checked independently.

How a page got in. It had to meet three or more of: recommended by a curator or reviewer (being picked by Wall of Portfolios or Case Study Club counts); author at lead level or above; the system shipped; a decision told with its cost; a number with a starting point; visuals that carry the story. The six section picks are the best fit among the 24 that passed, not the 24 in order. The board builds on an earlier ranked list of eight pages (Valerie Lin, Kaelig Deloumeau-Prigent, Ashu, James Coulter, Robert Ritacca, Dave Connis, Peter Marc and Evan Yudistira), which had been read as text only: this round looked at their images, and five of them earn a pick below. Per page scores against every tactic and anti tactic are in the private research notes, not here.

What the whole set says, in four lines:

1. The strongest pages head each section with a claim, not a foundation name (t36). CleverX's "Components are easy. Rules are the work." is the model; a page headed Colors, Typography, Components reads as a tour.
2. A before and after number is only as good as its starting point. Rakbank prints the "from" under every number; most pages print a bare percentage (t44).
3. Almost no page has a section on what is unfinished. The three that do (CleverX, Valerie Lin, the Usborne interview) read as the most senior.
4. No page proves its own checks on planted mistakes. Vercel and Kaelig count agent failures before and after, and Blade runs checks on the system itself; nobody shows that a check catches what it should. That is open ground for section 5.

The four pages flagged as strongest going in, and where each landed: Gudipati's TV UI infra is a pick in sections 1 and 2 (the executive summary and the scaling pains list; its "Building a Foundation for the Future" section is the three surfaces model, noted under section 5). Duffner's Netflix page is a pick in section 4 for its documentation. Behera's Blade is a pick in section 5 for checks run on the system itself; its "Design Systems Designer" framing is a heading, not an argument, so there is nothing more to take from it. Shaikh's Jio page has no number for the efficiency in its title, but its trace of one component into three products is the visual noted under section 3.

## 1. Opening: an image and before and after numbers

- **[Peter Marc, Rakbank](https://petermarc.com/portfolio/rakbank)**. Borrow: the Impact grid of four bordered cards, each a large number with a grey "from" line under it ("4 days, from 3 weeks"; "15+, from 0"; "200+, from 0"; "2,287, from 0"), and the result stated in the first paragraph ("Handoff time went from three weeks to four days"). Copy the card layout, not the numbers' pattern: only the handoff card is a real before and after. Three of the four read "from 0", which says only that the thing did not exist before; use a zero only where the zero is the point (none of anything, before the system). It is still the only page that prints a baseline under every number. Tactics: t11 precision with its derivation, t06 answer first. Avoid the hero, a Figma logo on grey tiles that carries nothing (t42).
- **[Meghan Rose, Twitter design system](https://meghanrose.design/project/twitter)**. Borrow: the opening image as a pair, "The Before" (old screens on white) against "The Big Picture" (the same product on the new system), one sentence under each: "Before launching the re-design of the product, Twitter felt dated and cluttered. It lacked type hierarchy and contrast." For a system case study the pair can be the same screen before and after the library, which makes the image the first piece of evidence rather than decoration. Tactic: t12 before and after asserts a cause. Avoid its uncounted claims ("Thousands of variants"); put real counts in their place.
- **[Siddhartha Gudipati, Design systems and TV UI infra](https://websiddu.com/work/tv-ui-infra)**. Borrow: the executive summary shape, two short paragraphs that give the problem, what was built and one hard number before any story ("delivered substantial performance improvements (including a 38% faster boot time), and achieved strong adoption"). The absolute figure beside the percentage ("38.83% improvement (4s)") is what makes it hold. Tactics: t06 answer first, t17 the nut graf. Avoid the "Target: 15%" placed among results and the hand drawn productivity graph labelled illustrative (t44, t42).

## 2. The problem: a system only its author could use well, counted

- **[Ashu, CleverX: Sixty pages, four engineers, one system.](https://ashukaur.netlify.app/cleverx-design-system)**. Borrow: the three tile problem count under Context, laid out as starting points with no after values: 60 to 70 (pages, the whole dashboard), "2 + 4" (designers and engineers, total) and 0 (Figma files on arrival). Three numbers, each a fact about the start, do the work of a paragraph. Tactics: t14 clues before the reveal (the starting counts come before any change), t03 qualify the scope.
- **[Buzz Usborne, Designing with AI (Help Scout)](https://buzzusborne.com/work/designing-with-ai/)**. Borrow: the shape of the problem, which is the nearest public match to this outline's section 2. A system that was "established in 2018 and had been extremely well used and maintained ever since", then "Yet whenever an LLM was asked to use the system, it failed. Hard." Then the failures, counted by kind: "tags for buttons, entirely invented components and alarming custom-coded replacements for things already in the system." And the cause in one sentence: "our Figma and React libraries had (obviously) been organised for human discovery… not machine comprehension." The reader here is anyone but the author, people as well as agents, so count what each got wrong the same way: invented, misused, rebuilt by hand. Tactics: t23 scene at the hardest moment, t29 the wrong turn and the signal.
- **[Siddhartha Gudipati, Design systems and TV UI infra](https://websiddu.com/work/tv-ui-infra)**. Borrow: the counted problem list under the heading "The Problem: Scaling Pains & Inconsistency", each item a bold label followed by its consequence, framed by the line "When Google TV launched, the focus was speed to market. While successful initially, as the platform matured and scaled, significant challenges emerged:". The label to adapt is "Lack of Centralization & Documentation", whose consequence reads "There was no single source of truth for UI components or design guidelines, leading to confusion, difficulty onboarding, and challenges enforcing best practices." This is the people side of the problem, which the Usborne pick covers for agents. Set label and consequence on two lines, not joined by a dash as the page does. Tactics: t36 headings that argue, t15 order by cause. Avoid giving six items equal weight (t48): keep three.

## 3. Foundations made readable: tokens, grid and spacing, components and states

- **[Ashu, CleverX](https://ashukaur.netlify.app/cleverx-design-system)**. Borrow: the Button state matrix (rows are the button types, columns Default, Hover, Focus, Pressed and Disabled) with a caption that is a rule, not a label: "Every combination drawn, not inferred. A state you didn't draw is a state an engineer invents." Every image on the page sits in a tinted mat with a one sentence rule as its caption; copy that system for the tokens, grid and states images. Tactics: t02 state the warrant, t36 headings that argue (the section is headed "Components are easy. Rules are the work.").
- **[Jasha Michele, Starting a design system at Learnosity](https://jashamichele.com/case-studies/design-system.html)**. Borrow: the colour merge image, rounds of grey swatches merging left to right, captioned "we started with over 50 greys and by the third round of merging had just 9." It shows a token decision as a process, not a palette. The same move works for spacing values or text styles: show the rounds, give the count at each. Tactics: t12 before and after, t14 clues before the reveal (the audit is shown before the decision).
- **[Evan Yudistira, Flex: Kredivo's design system](https://www.evanyudis.com/p/flex-design-system)**. Borrow: spacing tokens annotated on one real component (chips such as spacing-s and radius-xs appear on a product card, with a replay button), and a live component with Colour mode and Brand toggles beside a "Colors used" token list. Grid and spacing become readable on a thing the reader recognises, not on a specimen sheet. A still version works: one screen with its spacing tokens labelled in place. Tactics: t33 one instance flagged as typical, t21 name what it cost (its takeaway: "The hardest part of a design system is deciding what not to build."). Avoid the atomic design diagram (t46).

Grid has no strong model in the kept set. The cleanest grid image is on a page that did not pass the bar: Rishikesh's [Kissflow design system](https://rishikeshprem.framer.website/kissflow-works/design-system) draws one diagram per breakpoint (desktop, tablet, mobile) with column, gutter and margin values; borrow the form, not the page. For components, Ashraf Shaikh's [reusable components at Jio](https://iamashraf.framer.website/reusable-components) traces one component into three product screens with connector lines, a compact way to show that a component is used, not only drawn.

## 4. Library and usage rules: what, and how and when, for people and agents

- **[Daokai Lin, vivo design system](https://daokailin.com/vivo-design-system)**. Borrow: the split, in the author's words, as the frame for the whole section: "I split the system into two parts: a component library and a usage guideline. The library focuses on what to use. The guidelines explain how and when to use it." Then show one guideline page beside a "when to use other components" page with do and don't examples, as the page does with Popover. Extend it one step the page does not take: the same usage rules are what an agent reads. Tactic: t02 state the warrant (the split comes with its reason).
- **[Isabelle Duffner, Netflix: design system expansion packs](https://belleduffner.com/netflix)**. Borrow: the bar the documentation had to clear, set as a goal before the design ("Understandable across teams." then "Any team, such as product design or engineering should easily understand the documentation without adding any additional learning curve."), and the finished template described as three behaviours: "Modular Layout", "Swappable Components" and "Seamless Integration" ("When new changes are done to the component library, all components within a doc. can immediately update, even if nested within other components."). Its three pain cards ("Visual Inconsistency", "No Prior Guidance", "Manual Updates") are a compact problem visual if section 2 needs one. Add the reader the page does not name: an agent is one more team that must understand the docs with no learning curve. Tactic: t05 name the standard of review. Avoid its bare "100+" and "<2 weeks" (t44); it also sets goals and never scores the result against them.

CleverX's "one modal, every job" split into "four components, four intents" (Destructive, Confirmation, Informational dialog, Form), captioned "Modal or dialog? If the user has to do something, it's a modal. If they only need to know something, it's a dialog.", is the best single image of a usage rule if one is wanted here (t12, t21).
- **[John Phamous, Vercel: How our agents build on-brand pages with design.md](https://vercel.com/blog/how-our-agents-build-on-brand-pages-with-design-md)**. Borrow: the three layers for agents, a stylesheet as the what, a written design.md as the how and when, and checks for whatever can be checked, with the rule for where each correction goes: "Each correction a reviewer records gets landed in the narrowest place that can consistently enforce it." That sentence is the governance model for people and agents in one line. Tactic: t05 name the standard of review. Robert Ritacca's [Rift DS](https://robr0-ds.vercel.app/work/robr0-ds) splits the agent files the same way (design.md, content-design.md, CLAUDE.md) if a second example is wanted.

## 5. Making it scale: agents build from the library, checks, the harness tested

- **[Buzz Usborne, Designing with AI](https://buzzusborne.com/work/designing-with-ai/)**. Borrow: the test loop as the method, told before the fix. "I began with no context and asked Claude Design to produce a simple screen. Predictably, it was terrible (by our standards)." Then guidance added and the same task run again: "Each pass exposed another piece of judgement the system needed." And the cost: "against my better judgement I set aside two long weeks and rebuilt the entire design system to make it legible to LLMs". Visual: a component documentation page with the usage rules beside the variant grid, then the screens an agent built from it. Tactics: t14 clues before the reveal, t21 name what it cost.
- **[John Phamous, Vercel](https://vercel.com/blog/how-our-agents-build-on-brand-pages-with-design-md)**. Borrow: the controlled side by side (the same page without and with the guidance) captioned with its controls: "Same prompt, data, model, and viewport. Each version generated once, no rerolls." Then the result and its limits in back to back sentences: "The pages generated with design.md had 39 of those failures. The pages generated without it had 91, which works out to 57% fewer in this test." and "Six pages is also far too small a sample to make claims about quality or reliability". Tactics: t12 before and after (done with the controls stated), t11 precision with its derivation, t28 the limitations paragraph.
- **[Rama Krushna Behera, Blade design system (Razorpay)](https://www.ramakrushna.xyz/selected-work/blade-design-system)**. Borrow: checks on the system itself, before any agent: "Ran internal plugins on a periodic basis to ensure the foundations in design and dev are in sync." and "Conducted regular hygiene checks across the DS file.", with a result that has a scope: "Led to an overall 70% reduction in total number of variants across 40+ components without compromising the functionalities of the components." And a small measured usage figure, more credible than the page's large ones: "Easier discovery and adoption of UI patterns with being used by ~5 designers per week." This keeps section 5 about the system scaling, with agents as one consumer. Tactics: t03 qualify the scope, keep the commitment, t11 precision with its derivation. Avoid its "Improved designer efficiency by 80%" (t44) and its twelve equal contributions (t34).

For the agent half, Kaelig Deloumeau-Prigent's [agent teams build log](https://www.kaelig.fr/design-system-components-with-ai-agent-teams/) has the scene to borrow if a check failing at its own job is needed: a section headed "The 27 fabricated tokens", with "Agent systems can be confidently, systematically wrong in ways that pass their own validation." (t23, t29). Siddhartha Gudipati's TV UI infra adds the three surfaces model (sticker sheets for design, a documentation portal for developers, a catalog app on device) with "Established a Governance Model with a dedicated UI Infra team managing sprints and prioritizing requests."

## 6. Still imperfect: what is not done and how it is developing

- **[Ashu, CleverX](https://ashukaur.netlify.app/cleverx-design-system)**. Borrow: the closing heading "What changed, and what I never measured", and two lines under it: "We never instrumented adoption." with "I'd set up tracking on day one now." and the limit of scale, stated in one breath: "It held because four engineers cared. It wouldn't have held at twelve." Tactics: t28 the limitations paragraph, t03 qualify the scope, keep the commitment.
- **[Jan Haaland with Buzz Usborne, Peel the mushroom (Case Study Club interview)](https://www.casestudy.club/buzz-usborne-interview)**. Borrow: the agent's hit rate as one plain number with what still needs a person: "it's never producing anything right off its first pass. Never. I'd say it gets us 40 to 60% of the way there." And one thing built but not yet adopted, said without spin: an evaluation skill the author built, where "I feel like people aren't using it". Tactics: t28 the limitations paragraph, t25 steal the thunder.
- **[Valerie Lin, iStaging design system: from zero to AI-ready](https://valerielin-portfolio.pages.dev/case-studies/case-study-05-design-system)**. Borrow: the three label close as a ready skeleton for this section: The trade-off, What I'd improve, Next time. The trade-off names its cost ("Setup cost was high, but it bought automatic cascade and AI-readability"). "What I'd improve" names where drift will come from: documentation for the unmaintained 20% of patterns is principle based, so worked examples per product would reduce it. Tactics: t21 name what it cost, t25 steal the thunder. Avoid the impact row, where "60%" and "3×" carry no baseline (t44).

## Lines to keep off the page

Patterns the sample falls into most, to check a draft against by name:

- A bare percentage or "from 0" with no method (t44): Blade's "designer efficiency by 80%", Zoho Show's 46% and 55% performance figures that never reconcile, Copilot notifications' "1M WAU" header against "roughly 20,000" in its results.
- Galleries of components or platform screens after the argument is over (t41): Zoho Show's back half, Jio's five near identical anatomy blocks.
- Too many items at equal weight (t34): Blade's twelve contributions. Every section the same shape and confidence (t48): Valerie Lin's identical four step decision cards.
- The atomic design or double diamond diagram (t46): Flex, and the Case Study Club primer.
