Haven ending, round three

Three endings built from the research on how studio and senior designer case studies close. Each is one short beat under "What happens next", then a single handoff to the next case study, which is the only thing you can click. The boxed notes are rationale only.

Option 1 · Full circle

What happens next

Nine findings changed the design. None came from the teens Haven is for.

That was the constraint from the start. Studying minors needs a guardian’s consent, and asking would out the people Haven exists to protect. So the next test gets as close as the rules allow: 18 and 19 year olds who still live with family, using Haven at home in the moment instead of from memory.

Rationale

Concept: end where the story started. The page opens its research on the consent problem, so the ending returns to it and shows the plan answers it. Editors call this circling back, and it's the most common strong close in longform writing.

One statement at 40, then two short paragraphs. Matches what studios do: one beat, about 7 to 9 elements, prose no bigger than the page's section statements.

Status, next step, one specific observation. The three things the strongest concept case studies end on, in under 200 words.

One handoff right after. Hiring manager advice is to end on "read another case study". It's the only clickable thing, so nothing static reads as a link.

Rationale

Concept: the Metalab ending your layout comes from. A short title, three numbers, one line of next steps.

Counts of the work, not results. When results aren't public, studios count the work itself (Metalab's Ro study counts 654 components). These three numbers are all true and all on the page already.

Digits in your Satoshi Bold, no dividers. Same treatment as the "3 + 3 = 6" numbers in Demographic access, with no hairlines so they don't read as more of the page's rows.

Tradeoff. It's the most scannable ending. It also partly repeats numbers the reader has already seen, and editors warn against summary endings.

Rationale

Concept: your voice, cut down. The last round put about 80 words at 40. No studio sets more than about 30 words at display size, so the plan stays at 40 and the reasoning drops to 24.

Measure less, on purpose. Reviewers say to describe what success would look like and how you'd measure it. This line shows that judgment in one sentence instead of a metrics block.

Fewest elements of the three. Working memory holds about four chunks, and this ending has three before the handoff.
