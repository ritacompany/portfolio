# Wrong turns make design system case studies memorable

Across 42 design system case studies opened between 2 and 29 Oct 2026 (personal portfolios, solo and small team stories, studio pages and praised team write ups), the memorable ones share one move the rest skip: they tell a decision with the option not taken and what it cost, and they admit one wrong turn in the designer's own work. **About two in five do either and only six do both.** The common shape is a role card, then problem, process, solution, results and learnings, with the foundations list or atomic design as headings, a component gallery as the visuals, "we" as the voice and unbaselined percentages as the result. That shape is forgettable not because it is wrong but because it describes a library rather than an argument; one hiring voice puts the bar as portfolios that "feel less like galleries and more like arguments" ([Balint Bogdan](https://balintbogdan.substack.com/p/the-portfolio-question)). Adoption is the weakest beat in every pass: only one page reports a system coverage figure with a baseline, and none shows a screen built by someone who never met the designer. Three gaps sit open for a design lead who built a startup's system mostly alone: no case study found involves a volunteer or rotating team; none shows the system holding up for outside developers who were never in the room; and only one personal portfolio, which treats the system as test material, shows agents building from a system with the designer judging the output. The sample has real limits (20 more pages failed to load, images were judged from alt text and every number is the designer's own claim), so the patterns are strong tendencies, not laws.

## Two skeletons dominate, and the strong ones open on a person or a reversal

Two structures cover nearly every personal page. **Eight of the eleven personal portfolios in the 29 Oct pass use a role card followed by problem, process, solution, results and learnings**, and the other three are a system tour (Cheung, Peck, Simioni). Six put a header card of client, role, year and tools before any prose, and eight close on a learnings section; only Matze Lenz adds "What I would do more" ([Lenz](https://m423.super.site/cases/banking-design-system)). Studio pages are shorter (300 to 1,300 words) and treat the system as one beat inside a product or brand story: Metalab gives Ro's system one section of about eleven ([Metalab Ro](https://www.metalab.com/work/ro)), and frog gives Bupa's a single clause ([frog](https://www.frog.co/work/designing-the-future-of-health-insurance)). Small team stories differ from both. They are first person, chronological and confessional, and their evidence is a person; big company pieces are problem lists and plans, and their evidence is scale ([Kargaev](https://blog.deeflect.com/valk-02-design-process-from-zero/); [Vasenina](https://www.designsystemscollective.com/how-we-built-a-consistent-ux-for-10-products-in-3-months-892f489bda2e)).

The strongest pages organise by decision rather than by deliverable. Kargaev's headings are themselves the argument: "What I inherited; First instinct was wrong; What actually worked: pages first; Figma organization for one person; Getting the dev team to actually follow specifications; What I got wrong about scale; Six months in, honest assessment" ([Kargaev](https://blog.deeflect.com/valk-02-design-process-from-zero/)). Linear phrases its headings as the questions a reader would ask ("What tests did we run", "How did we prioritize") and gives each section to the person who made that call ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)). Connis pairs each heading with its finding, such as "Designing expectations | When more detail made output worse" ([Connis](https://daveconnis.com/case-study-3)). A reader who skims only these headings learns what was decided, which is what the foundations list as headings (Histen, Clay) cannot do.

**Openings split cleanly.** The weak ones start on context the reader already has: industry boilerplate ("In the rapidly evolving world of financial technology") ([Laschevoski](https://robnilas.design/projects/design-system-governance)), a definition ("A design system is not just a set of components, but a shared language that empowers collaboration") ([AG](https://agfolio.framer.ai/by-u-design-system-from-scattered-to-scalable)), a footnoted definition ([ustwo](https://ustwo.com/work/ishares/)) or client size ("Serving more than 2 billion people globally") ([Koto](https://koto.com/projects/whatsapp)). The strong ones start on a person stuck on an artefact, a feeling or a reversal. AG's page recovers by hinging on one developer's question, "Should I follow the button in Figma, or the one in Zeplin?" ([AG](https://agfolio.framer.ai/by-u-design-system-from-scattered-to-scalable)). Cheung opens on a wall of seven role tagged complaints, such as a new designer saying "I have no idea which components are ready to use" ([Cheung](https://www.dannyckm.com/works/credit-suisse-design-system)). Kargaev opens on an honest reaction: "Six months ago I opened Figma file for first time at VALK and felt something I did not expect. Not excitement. Just confusion." ([Kargaev](https://blog.deeflect.com/valk-02-design-process-from-zero/)). Nguyen reframes the ask in her second paragraph: "The ask wasn't 'make it prettier.' It was: give five engineers and one designer a single source of truth that survives contact with real deadlines." ([Nguyen](https://contra.com/p/ABsYONkR-vns-design-system)). Connis leads with a reversal: "I made AI prototypes more consistent. Stakeholders assumed they were final." ([Connis](https://daveconnis.com/case-study-3)). Results first openers work only when the first number carries its baseline in the same line, as Nasir's "5 weeks → 5 days per brand" does and his unbaselined "71% reduction in helpline and email cases" does not ([Nasir, Supercharged](https://kassamnasir.com/cases/supercharged/); [Nasir, DesignOps](https://kassamnasir.com/cases/designing-design/)).

One warning sits under all of this. The best written opening in the whole sample, "The first thing I did was nothing.", belongs to a Framer template about a template's sample company ([Zolt template](https://zolt-portfolio.framer.website/case-study/building-a-perfect-design-system-from-zero)). **Voice is now cheap.** What separates real work is specificity a reader can check: a named product, a real number with its baseline, a quoted colleague. Hiring voices say the same from the other side: one hiring partner warns that "When every portfolio looks the same after using AI, the AI becomes a sign of sameness rather than capability" (quoted secondhand) ([The Fountain Institute](https://www.thefountaininstitute.com/blog/senior-product-designers-hired)).

## The system shows best in use, not on a component sheet

Three moves keep a page out of the gallery. **The first is showing the system working on real product or a real task.** Swapnil ends not on components but on "Products Shipped with the Design System", eight real products with their screens ([Swapnil](https://www.swapnil.design/en/portfolio/375f4888-2ed7-4df7-95f2-83f85e4f3d82)). Nasir times one landing page step by step, from "Total time: 3-4 hours per landing page" before to "20-30 minutes" after, with a video of the workflow ([Nasir](https://kassamnasir.com/cases/supercharged/)); it is the most persuasive visual in the personal set and the cheapest to make. Peck validates components on "a key Contiki benchmark page that displays components in situ" with live Figma embeds ([Peck](https://chris-peck.design/portfolio/multi-brand-design-system-2025-and-beyond/)). Statsig shows building as GIFs captioned "Creating a new page is really easy" ([Lee](https://statsig.com/blog/design-system-important-early-startup)). Linear never separates its system from the app's own views, yet the reader leaves knowing the colour model, through a captioned "Before / Concept / After" triptych ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)). Instrument's Nike page shows the failure of the same balance: only product and nothing of the system, under a graphic reading "UNIFIED FLEXIBLE PREMIUM" ([Instrument](https://www.instrument.com/work/nike-digital-design-system)).

**The second move is giving each visual a decision and its reason.** Dannaway's sections are component and colour sheets, yet the page does not read as a gallery because each carries an opinion: "I decided to use transparent colours, rather than solid ones, to help ensure components look consistent on different coloured backgrounds" ([Dannaway](https://www.adhamdannaway.com/portfolio/figma-design-system)). Grixti and Walker lead with architecture and pipeline diagrams rather than component sheets ([Grixti](https://nessgrixti.com/portfolio/wise-multi-brand/); [Walker](https://stuartwalker.webflow.io/token-design-system-case-study)).

**The third move is showing the problem state before the parts.** Lenz lists symptoms a reader can picture, such as "Headlines jumping when navigating between pages" ([Lenz](https://m423.super.site/cases/banking-design-system)). Kargaev uses no images at all and still makes the mess concrete: "body text was 14px, in other places 13px, in other places 15px", and card padding of 16, 20 and 24px on one page ([Kargaev](https://blog.deeflect.com/valk-02-design-process-from-zero/)). Breault counts his before state (300+ text styles, 10+ fonts, 100+ colours) ([Breault](https://www.ryanbreaultux.com/work-jcp.html)).

The reverse predictor is equally clear. **Atomic design as section structure is the strongest sign of a gallery read** in the personal set: Simioni's "What we built" is atoms, molecules and organisms with three component sheets and no product screens ([Simioni](https://www.marcosimioni.net/portfolio-item/design_system/)), and Cheung's second half lists five library attributes ("Configurable", "Scalable", "Interactive", "Universal", "Accessible") then stops after token tiers ([Cheung](https://www.dannyckm.com/works/credit-suisse-design-system)). Studios contribute visual devices worth noting even though their voice is not: Calvin Klein's system appears as a "Motion System Animation" tied to navigation, and Ro adds a code module and a stat row ([Metalab Calvin Klein](https://www.metalab.com/work/calvin-klein); [Metalab Ro](https://www.metalab.com/work/ro)). Those claims rest on module names and alt text only.

## Decisions, failures and adoption: where the strong ones earn trust

### A decision only reads as one when something was given up

Most pages state choices without alternatives: atomic design, three tier tokens, Figma first. Few name what was rejected and what the choice cost. Swapnil is the only personal page with a true options table, rating "Build a Completely New Design System" as "Low velocity, High efforts" against building on an existing one at "Lower level of customization possible" ([Swapnil](https://www.swapnil.design/en/portfolio/375f4888-2ed7-4df7-95f2-83f85e4f3d82)). Statsig's first designer weighed whether to build at all, admitting "At an early stage startup, putting time and energy into establishing a design system could be costly and risky" before giving four reasons ([Lee](https://statsig.com/blog/design-system-important-early-startup)). Linear set navigation aside because "Any updates would require significant engineering work and change how users interacted with the product. This felt like an unnecessary risk and would expand the scope", and reduced theming from 98 variables per theme to three inputs ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)). Dan Donald took a stance he knew was disputed: "One early principle we had was that live code trumps the design. That's a controversial statement in some circles" ([Donald](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2022/04/artifacts-design-systems/)). The test that separates documentation from story is simple: **a foundations or governance beat that names nothing given up reads as documentation.**

Small teams make tooling decisions visible because one person makes them. Kargaev tells his Figma structure as three iterations (one big file, a file per area, then master plus product files) ([Kargaev](https://blog.deeflect.com/valk-02-design-process-from-zero/)), and De Wit built his own tool when Storybook did not fit his stack ([De Wit](https://uxdesign.cc/stop-doing-design-system-projects-b094369dcfdd)). Constraints read as strength, not complaint, when they become a method the reader could reuse: "extract, not prescribe" ([Kargaev](https://blog.deeflect.com/valk-02-design-process-from-zero/)), a "Minimum-Viable Design System" of colours, typefaces, buttons and text fields ([Jessica and Biancaputra](https://uxdesign.cc/first-hand-experience-in-designing-a-design-team-5db6d87285d6)), "The team is small so we required a workflow that would require as little ongoing input as possible" ([Walker](https://stuartwalker.webflow.io/token-design-system-case-study)). Demchenko shows the opposite: a list of challenges and an open question ("Should I follow Atomic design, should I group my components by purpose or find other structure?") never answered ([Demchenko](https://uxplanet.org/a-solo-designers-journey-in-a-fast-growing-startup-d4c18f40f743)).

### An owned wrong turn reads as seniority

Only two of the eleven personal portfolios in the 29 Oct pass tell a wrong turn in the designer's own work, and the strongest pages across all passes tell one. Kargaev spent "almost six weeks" building system first, then "threw away most of this work". He also admits a "button component with eleven variants, most of which we never used" ([Kargaev](https://blog.deeflect.com/valk-02-design-process-from-zero/)). Nguyen prices her mistake: "Retrofitting it cost a refactor week that a day of upfront modeling would have prevented." ([Nguyen](https://contra.com/p/ABsYONkR-vns-design-system)). Grixti turns on "We broke the library" ([Grixti](https://nessgrixti.com/portfolio/wise-multi-brand/)), and the Raise author gives a whole section to "What broke in Hermes V1.0?" ([Raise](https://naijatechguy.substack.com/p/building-a-design-system-as-a-solo)). Linear admits of its earlier theme generation system, "We never fully rolled out this system, though." ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)). Lenz names a lost argument: "due to internal regulations regarding Figma plugins, I couldn't convince the design team to adopt these token systems" ([Lenz](https://m423.super.site/cases/banking-design-system)). De Wit's one navigation component took eight months to merge ([De Wit](https://uxdesign.cc/stop-doing-design-system-projects-b094369dcfdd)).

How the failure is told matters as much as telling it. The good ones blame no one: Kargaev writes of developers who ignored specs, "I do not blame them for this. It is natural in early startup" and "What changed it was not confrontation." ([Kargaev](https://blog.deeflect.com/valk-02-design-process-from-zero/)); Abimbola opens by crediting the system he inherited ([Abimbola](https://www.designsystemscollective.com/from-ambiguity-to-adoption-evolving-kudas-design-system-583fddea948a)). A failure can also be wasted. Laschevoski tells a real one ("When the governance model was first introduced, feedback from designers and developers highlighted its complexity.") and then buries it under a results paragraph that admits there are no results ([Laschevoski](https://robnilas.design/projects/design-system-governance)).

Where agents enter the system story, the failure is the proof that the work was real. 1Password's team write up turns on "At first, it was a semi-hot mess." and "We hadn't saved developer time by making it easier to open a PR; instead, we'd moved the work downstream." ([1Password](https://1password.com/blog/agent-driven-design-system)). Connis's turn is that consistency itself misled people: "Until our process changed, the right answer turned out to be keeping the prototypes deliberately rough." ([Connis](https://daveconnis.com/case-study-3)). Outside portfolios, the strongest engineering logs share a pattern of the passing check that lied: Kaelig Deloumeau-Prigent's agent pipeline reported success while "All 27 tokens use a plausible" prefix that did not exist, because "Tests passed because tests check behavior, not visual appearance." ([Kaelig Deloumeau-Prigent](https://www.kaelig.fr/design-system-components-with-ai-agent-teams/)), and NordVPN's benchmark found "The audit score is always the optimistic one." ([Into Design Systems](https://intodesignsystems.substack.com/p/how-to-measure-if-ai-agents-actually)). Parundekar's piece shows what happens without that: agents build screens "other than a few hiccups", and nothing is judged ([Parundekar](https://medium.com/ai-hero/upgrading-my-design-system-with-claude-design-0051f2204cfa)).

### Adoption is the weakest beat in every pass

Most pages fill the adoption gap badly. Nasir's heading "Design System Adoption Rate" is followed by "The team embraced the new system for its apparent benefits and usability." and no number ([Nasir](https://kassamnasir.com/cases/designing-design/)). Simioni promises "The results were immediate and measurable" over blank slots ([Simioni](https://www.marcosimioni.net/portfolio-item/design_system/)). The Raise author lists company revenue and countries as "Final Metrics" ([Raise](https://naijatechguy.substack.com/p/building-a-design-system-as-a-solo)), and frog credits a 1.6 to 4.6 app store rating to a programme of which the system was one part ([frog](https://www.frog.co/work/designing-the-future-of-health-insurance)). Studios count inventory instead of change, such as "654+ Design system components and patterns" (read from Metalab's content API; a plain page fetch does not render it) ([Metalab Ro](https://www.metalab.com/work/ro)). ustwo's footnoted metrics are the best agency attempt and still pair a four hour to two hour flow with a claimed 75% gain ([ustwo](https://ustwo.com/work/ishares/)).

**One page in 42 reports system coverage with a baseline**: Swapnil's "Component Coverage Increased from 40% to 74%", method unstated ([Swapnil](https://www.swapnil.design/en/portfolio/375f4888-2ed7-4df7-95f2-83f85e4f3d82)). The honest moves that remain are qualitative and they work. A named user with a small, plausible gain: "Alex told me at some point that specific Figma annotations saved him maybe one hour per week" ([Kargaev](https://blog.deeflect.com/valk-02-design-process-from-zero/)). A quote from someone who builds with the system: "It's the first time I can just open Figma and know that's the version I should implement" ([AG](https://agfolio.framer.ai/by-u-design-system-from-scattered-to-scalable)). A behaviour change: "'Does it match the system?' replaced subjective debate" ([Nguyen](https://contra.com/p/ABsYONkR-vns-design-system)). Demand from outside: "Designers outside our team were also asking for new components" ([De Wit](https://uxdesign.cc/stop-doing-design-system-projects-b094369dcfdd)). Resistance named and met: "engineers hesitated to maintain shared libraries, and PMs questioned long-term ROI" ([Wemyss](https://www.wemyssdesign.com/design-system)). And a stated boundary, which reads as more senior than a vague percentage: "While we successfully implemented the system within the UX design team, I would push for deeper engagement with developers" ([Lenz](https://m423.super.site/cases/banking-design-system)); developer adoption "is our biggest challenge right now" ([Abimbola](https://www.designsystemscollective.com/from-ambiguity-to-adoption-evolving-kudas-design-system-583fddea948a)).

Engineering readers bring a harder yardstick. Sujeet Jaiswal writes that "Adoption is the only metric that matters at the program level" and "A beautiful system with 12% adoption has failed; an ugly one with 90% adoption is succeeding", and defines countable proxies a small system can report: component usage, share of UI surface on system components, detachment from the library (to be treated "as signal, not failure") and share of styles referencing semantic tokens rather than literal values ([Sujeet Jaiswal](https://sujeet.pro/articles/design-system-adoption-foundations)). None of the 42 case studies uses those proxies.

## Six habits make most of them forgettable

The forgettable core is consistent across personal, small team and studio pages. **First, the library as the table of contents:** foundations or atomic levels as headings (Histen, Clay, Simioni) ([Histen](https://michaelhisten.com/case-study-design-systems.html); [Clay](https://clay.global/work/corsair)). **Second, the stock thesis:** "shared language" and "single source of truth" as the point rather than the starting assumption ([Simioni](https://www.marcosimioni.net/portfolio-item/design_system/); [AG](https://agfolio.framer.ai/by-u-design-system-from-scattered-to-scalable)). **Third, adjectives in place of decisions:** "revolution", "unprecedented flexibility" ([Nasir](https://kassamnasir.com/cases/supercharged/)), "UNIFIED FLEXIBLE PREMIUM" ([Instrument](https://www.instrument.com/work/nike-digital-design-system)). **Fourth, numbers that describe the client, the inventory or the whole programme** rather than a change the system made. **Fifth, "we" with no own part**: both Nasir pages, Simioni and Peck never separate what the author did, while Swapnil's "My Contributions" section, Cheung's credit to a Design Ops colleague and Lenz's named teammates show how ([Swapnil](https://www.swapnil.design/en/portfolio/375f4888-2ed7-4df7-95f2-83f85e4f3d82)). A hiring manager says she skips gallery style work because she cannot tell "if they or someone else worked on the designs" ([Leslie Yang](https://leslieyang.substack.com/p/what-a-hiring-manager-looks-for-in)), and Tom Scott's rule is "Stop talking about "we" and focus on "I"." ([Tom Scott](https://verifiedinsider.substack.com/p/mistakes-designers-make-in-interviews)). **Sixth, platitude learnings:** "A system is never 'done'" ([AG](https://agfolio.framer.ai/by-u-design-system-from-scattered-to-scalable)), "Start with a Clear Vision" ([Laschevoski](https://robnilas.design/projects/design-system-governance)).

Two newer failures now end a read outright. **Template residue**: Peck's first paragraph is theme filler ("Far far away, behind the word mountains") under a sidebar still reading "Year 2017" ([Peck](https://chris-peck.design/portfolio/multi-brand-design-system-2025-and-beyond/)), and Simioni's benefit lines have double spaces where figures should be ([Simioni](https://www.marcosimioni.net/portfolio-item/design_system/)). **Machine written prose**: Laschevoski's page narrates itself in third person ("This case study explores the journey of a FinTech company") and offers "While specific metrics may vary, common indicators of success include" in place of results ([Laschevoski](https://robnilas.design/projects/design-system-governance)). Studio pages add one more: no people at all, which suits selling the next client and is the opposite of what a personal case study needs.

## A starting point for a lead who built the system mostly alone

This section gives material to work from, not a story. His story should come from his own material; what follows is what the 42 pages say to borrow, to avoid and where the open ground is.

### Patterns worth using

The page can open on a person and an artefact rather than on context: a specific person stuck on a specific thing (AG's developer and two buttons, Cheung's quote wall) is recognised by a reader in one sentence. If a number opens, its baseline goes in the same line. Headings can state decisions or answer a reader's question in the order the decisions were made (Kargaev, Linear, Connis), which also serves the skimmer; readers take in at most 28% of a page's words and 20% is more likely ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-little-do-users-read/)). The team and its size belong in the first screen with "I" kept for decisions and others credited by name for what they did (Grixti's "The Team", Walker's "My role", Quiec's "Lead product designer (myself) - 4 developers - 2 business owners") ([Quiec](https://kevin-quiec.fr/project/merchsquare-designsystem)). The before state works best counted and concrete (Kargaev's three font sizes, Lenz's jumping headlines). Each decision can carry the option not taken and its cost (Swapnil, Lee, Linear). One wrong turn of the designer's own, told without blame and with what it cost, is the single strongest seniority signal in the sample. The system is most convincing shown in use: real screens, a timed real task, a short recording of building with it. Adoption can be told through a named user, a stated boundary and, where his material allows, one of Jaiswal's countable proxies with a baseline. A close on "what I would do differently" (Lenz, 1Password) outlasts a learnings list.

On AI as one part of the designer's story, the sample offers two usable models and one useful frame. ustwo treats adoption as serving users of the system ("Design systems are products; they have users.") and names five kinds of them ([ustwo](https://ustwo.com/work/ishares/)); 1Password treats agents as contributors working through the same workflows people use, with a human gate before any work starts ([1Password](https://1password.com/blog/agent-driven-design-system)). Read together, agents can sit in the case study as one more kind of user of the system alongside designers, volunteers and developers, judged by the same adoption standard, rather than as the frame. Employers now write the job that way: Careem's 2026 design system role lists structuring tokens and components so agentic workflows "produce on-system output" and "AI-assisted audits and linting to catch off-system usage and token misuse before shipping" ([Careem](https://jobs.intodesignsystems.com/jobs/careem-senior-product-designer-design-systems-2ffpq7)).

### Patterns to avoid

Avoid the foundations list or atomic levels as headings, the "shared language" thesis, a definition or industry opener, adjectives where a decision should be, inventory counts presented as results, company or programme outcomes credited to the system, unbaselined percentages, "we" with no own part and platitude learnings. Check for template residue before anything ships. Avoid prose that reads generated; polished voice no longer signals real work, and the best written page found was fake.

On AI specifically, three traps show in the sample. AI as a coda: Makszy places it as step 5 of 5 and the closing line, with no AI metric or artefact ([Makszy](https://heydorka.rocks/case-studies/)). AI as a tool demo: Parundekar shows agents building screens and never judges what they built ([Parundekar](https://medium.com/ai-hero/upgrading-my-design-system-with-claude-design-0051f2204cfa)). AI as the headline hook: "agents as a second audience" is now table stakes, with 20 of 21 public systems in one census offering an MCP server and 19 an agent skill ([State of AI in Design Systems](https://state-of-ai-in-design-systems.netlify.app/)). There is also a measured cost to the label itself: across 13 experiments with more than 5,000 participants, "Clients placed 20% less trust in graphic designers after AI disclosure" ([University of Arizona](https://news.arizona.edu/news/disclosing-ai-use-can-backfire-research-shows)). Hiding it is not the answer either; a recruiter warns "If you hide the tools you used, you're not giving them anything to judge" ([Design Buddies](https://designbuddies.substack.com/p/design-hiring)). The evidence points to being specific about the human role: what the designer set, checked and overruled.

### Gaps his material could fill

Five gaps are open across every pass. **No case study involves a volunteer or rotating team**; searches for one returned job ads and civic tech background only. **No case study shows the system used well by people who never met its designer**, whether a newcomer to the team, an outside developer or an agent; every adoption proof in the sample comes from a colleague or client team the author worked with directly. **No page reports an adoption or coverage measure with a method**, and only Swapnil gives a baseline. **Only one personal portfolio (Connis) shows agents building from a design system with the designer judging the output**, and there the system is test material for a separate AI story; every published agent test of a system found in the landscape pass was run by engineers or design engineers on code packages, not by a designer on a Figma library ([Into Design Systems](https://intodesignsystems.substack.com/p/how-to-measure-if-ai-agents-actually); [Mateusz](https://dev.to/jablonowski/does-a-design-system-change-what-a-coding-agent-writes-280d)). And **no design system case study tells a check that passed but was wrong** from the designer's seat at startup scale; the examples come from engineering logs.

Which of these his material can fill is for that material to decide. The questions it has to answer before a story is chosen: what the system was at the start, counted; which decisions had a real alternative and what each cost; which turn was his own mistake; who built with the system without him in the room and what they got right and wrong; which number, if any, has a baseline and a method; and where adoption stopped. If the starting state was as small as the research brief recorded (a button, a palette, four brand images and two typefaces), it sits closest to goKampus's minimum viable system and Kargaev's "extract, not prescribe" method, and both are useful reference points for how small beginnings read.

## Conclusion

The field's weakness is the opening. Design system case studies are under represented in curated lists, most that exist describe a library rather than defend a decision, and adoption, the measure engineers and hiring panels say matters most, is almost never shown with evidence. That means the bar for standing out is not polish or scale but a few honest specifics: a decision with its cost, a wrong turn owned and someone outside the designer's reach building well with the system.

The newer finding is about where AI sits. Most 2026 pages that mention agents either tack them on as a closing step or demo them without judging the output, and both weaken the system story. The few strong pieces (1Password, Connis, the engineering logs) earn trust the same way the best non AI case studies do: by showing judgement, a failure and what changed. For a lead whose system was used by volunteers, outside developers and later agents, that suggests agents are most credible as one more user the system had to serve, held to the same evidence as the people, but that reading is a hypothesis for his material to confirm or reject.
