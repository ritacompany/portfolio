# The calendar suggests, the week decides

Mondai's schedule view is a Sunday to Saturday week grid that places the same Action Items a person already sees in their list as timed blocks in the gaps between their own synced calendar events. It exists only for people who connect a calendar, and every decision Chadwick made while building it follows one split of authority: **the calendar answers "when", the week answers "by when"**, so a scheduled time is a suggestion with no power over the person. Mondai picks the first time from open calendar time, the person's preferred hours and focus hours; the person can move an item to another open block in the same week whenever they want; Mondai never moves anything itself; and when a time passes it drops off the card while the item stays open until Saturday night with nothing marked missed. He rejected auto rescheduling after confirming it was easy to build, rejected missed and strikethrough states as demotivating and rejected per item due dates. Published research supports the core of this well: a specific day and time is what makes a plan work (Milkman 2011), people keep using an algorithm they can adjust (Dietvorst 2018) and coding a lapse as failure lowers later effort (Soman and Cheema 2004; Silverman and Barasch 2023). The evidence is thin in two places that matter for copy: no study tests a plan the system writes rather than the person, and no study compares automatic against user controlled rescheduling. Mondai has no user research, tests or analytics on any scheduling surface, so every claim in the block is design reasoning backed by secondary research, not observed behavior. Several source conflicts, most importantly whether a passed block dims, need Chadwick's answer before the block is final.

## A week grid where green blocks sit between the person's real life

The person reaches the schedule view from the Action Hub, which defaults to a List view. A **List / Schedule segmented control** sits top right of the item list, and Schedule shows the same week's items placed against real events ([ARCHIVE:154-158, 390-395](../../Mondai/mondai/reference/PRODUCT_KNOWLEDGE_ARCHIVE.md); shipped screen `Mondai/mondai/reference/desktop-screens/action-hub/list-view.png`). The grid has a weekday header with today highlighted and past days dimmed, an ALL DAY row and hour rows down the left, roughly 6AM to 8PM in the live Figma frame (Desktop 1 (MVP), node 2882:34507, viewed 28 Oct 2026; Mondai corpus C0810:152-153).

Three things share the grid, and **colour tells the person what they can do before they touch anything**: green Action Items, which carry a state and can be moved within the week; magenta Mondai events, which take an RSVP but cannot be moved; and external calendar events, shown read only ([ARCHIVE:162-168](../../Mondai/mondai/reference/PRODUCT_KNOWLEDGE_ARCHIVE.md)). A block of 30 minutes or more shows a small caps eyebrow for the focus area, a title, a time range such as "3pm → 4pm" and a status; anything shorter is too small to show text ([ARCHIVE:172](../../Mondai/mondai/reference/PRODUCT_KNOWLEDGE_ARCHIVE.md); C0810:121-123, 150). The Figma example week shows a person named Aisha working 8AM to 2:30PM on weekdays, with school pickup, a pottery class and dinners around it, and four Action Items in the gaps: a completed discussion guide on Sunday morning, an active usability testing session Tuesday 4 to 5PM, then synthesis on Wednesday evening and wireframe updates Friday afternoon, both not started (Figma node 2882:34507). That example is the clearest picture for a stranger: the work lands in the life, not on top of it.

Clicking or tapping any block, tall or short, opens **one anchored popover** with the item's pathway and week, title, status and time, then three actions: Go to action item, the state action (Mark as complete, Mark as active or Undo completion) and Reschedule ([ARCHIVE:170-176](../../Mondai/mondai/reference/PRODUCT_KNOWLEDGE_ARCHIVE.md); C0810:321-331). It is deliberately an action surface, not a detail viewer; full detail lives on the Action Item page. Chadwick held that the popover's actions mirror the list because there is no reason they would differ (C0810:183). Sequence still governs: every item in the current week is visible and movable, but only the system assigned Active item can be completed, and future weeks are not shown (ARCHIVE:196-200; C0504:116-138). Alternatives he considered and set aside were a card click straight to the detail page, a bottom sheet on tap and long press; hover preview is a desktop nice to have and drag to reschedule is marked for v2 (C0504:49, 283; C0520:69, 110-146; C0810:141).

## Mondai places from three inputs and never over an event

For MVP, Mondai picks times instead of asking the person to run a scheduling flow (C0430:12). The inputs are **open calendar time, the person's boundaries and focus hours plus each item's duration**, and the same function runs whenever it is called (C0503:356). Sync is read only, because reading availability is all placement needs ([ARCHIVE:405-411](../../Mondai/mondai/reference/PRODUCT_KNOWLEDGE_ARCHIVE.md)). Setup asks for two things: a preferred time frame on a range slider from 6AM to about midnight, with a "Mondai suggests" callout derived from the calendar, and the person's sharpest hours from five tiles (mornings, mid-day, evenings, late night, no preference) (`desktop-screens/onboarding/step-2-time-boundaries.png`). How many items a week gets comes from the capacity the person declared, not from the calendar ([RULES:29](../../Mondai/mondai/reference/PRODUCT_RULES.md)). Mondai will not place an item at 3am or over an event just to make it fit (C0503:366).

What is not written anywhere is the placement algorithm itself: item order, how strongly focus hours weigh, buffers around events or spreading across days (C0504:75 raised the question; no answer exists). The Figma week places items in sequence order, about one per day with free days between and never inside work hours, and it puts one on a Sunday, so weekends are placeable. The block should describe inputs and the never overlap rule and stay silent on ordering.

## Every rejection protects the person's ownership of the week

The governing tension is two clocks. A calendar user's Action Item has a time on Tuesday and a deadline on Saturday night, and Chadwick named that conflict himself on 3 May 2026: a scheduled time sits outside the weekly framework (`reference/cs2/reasoning/calendar-and-schedule.md:13-25`). He chose the week. On 30 May he put it plainly as schedules being recommendations and assistance tools, not due dates (`calendar-and-schedule.md:29-36`). Everything else follows from that choice.

**Auto rescheduling was feasible and declined.** He first checked that moving a missed task would not be hard to build, then decided it would get confusing fast inside Mondai (`calendar-and-schedule.md:59-70`; C0503:379). Comparators were Motion, which reshuffles missed tasks without asking, and Sunsama, which refuses (C0503:281, 580). Rescheduling is the person's move only: within the current week (next week is Rollover's job), into **open block pills** under a header that reads "SELECT A NEW TIME" rather than "RESCHEDULE", so an item can never land on a meeting ([ARCHIVE:180-186](../../Mondai/mondai/reference/PRODUCT_KNOWLEDGE_ARCHIVE.md); C0810:184). Reschedule lives on the schedule view because that is where the person has the most context; forcing a trip back to the list would be gatekeeping (C0520:110, 140).

**Missed, failed and strikethrough states were rejected** as demotivating, and so was inferring completion from a passed time, because whether the work happened is a person level check the system cannot make (`calendar-and-schedule.md:46-57, 72-74`; `reference/cs2/THROUGHLINES.md:141-153`). His resolution was a soft signal that adds no anxiety, since the item is still due Saturday night (`calendar-and-schedule.md:48-53`). Later in the same 3 May session he proposed the time leave the card entirely, with the reschedule icon kept, and the original time moving into the reschedule menu as "Originally scheduled" (C0503:622-695). His reason: a passed time, a time never given and a time that could not fit should all collapse into one card state, with no Saturday wall of dimmed cards, because the system is not keeping score (C0503:630-634). His approved copy carries this: anyone can move any item whenever they want, Mondai never moves one, the first time is a suggestion and a passed time drops off while the item stays open (`reference/cs2/reader-check/known-setup.md:9`). The approved merged block of 27 Oct adds that the block dims and that nothing is marked missed (`reference/cs2/reader-check/runs/2026-10-26/sched.txt:1-6`; commit 17690d7).

**Constraints on the person were loosened wherever they appeared.** Setup ranges guide placement and are not hard limits, which he corrected on 26 Oct 2026 (`reference/cs2/core/section-8.md:21`; approved "preferences, not limits" in `reader-check/known-schedule.md:9`). For a packed week he rejected a manual scheduling mode as a lot of work for a fringe case and chose to drop to the plain list experience, at first for the whole week and on 26 Oct only for the items that cannot be placed, with a neutral notice rather than orange "Unscheduled" badges, because a full calendar is not an error (C0428:373-413; `core/section-8.md:3, 32-36`). Disconnecting a calendar removes the times and returns the person to the list, and loses nothing that mattered because the schedule never held the commitment ([ARCHIVE:386-388](../../Mondai/mondai/reference/PRODUCT_KNOWLEDGE_ARCHIVE.md); `calendar-and-schedule.md:34-36`). Without a calendar the grid would be a blank page with only Mondai's items on it, worse than the list, so the Schedule tab is disabled and carries the connect prompt where the person feels its absence ([ARCHIVE:160, 397](../../Mondai/mondai/reference/PRODUCT_KNOWLEDGE_ARCHIVE.md)).

There is a background worth knowing but not writing: calendar integration was the founder's idea, Chadwick inherited it, and his Feb 2025 argument was that rigid scheduling copies traditional education and ignores how differently people live (`reference/cs2/reasoning/dossier-findings-22oct.md:173, 210`). Under the stranger rule and the no quotes rule, the block uses the reasoning and not the history.

## Research backs the suggestion and the soft passed time, not the automation claim

The strongest single support is a field experiment. Employees reminded about a flu shot vaccinated 1.5 points more when prompted to write a date, which was not significant, and **4.2 points more when prompted to write a date and a time**, which was ([Milkman et al. 2011, PNAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3127912)). A suggested slot with a day and a time does the step that separated the working arm from the failing one. Behind it sit two meta analyses of when and where plans: d = 0.65 across 94 tests ([Gollwitzer and Sheeran 2006](https://stafforini.com/works/gollwitzer-2006-implementation-intentions-and/)) and d of about .27 to .66 across 642 tests, stronger when the plan is specific and rehearsed ([Sheeran, Listrom and Gollwitzer 2025](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/handle/123456789/69905)). Two cautions travel with it. Planning helps with one goal and stops helping with many, unless the set is framed as manageable ([Dalton and Spiller 2012](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/doi10.1086-664500.html)), which supports a small weekly count sized to declared hours. And in a 61,000 person gym megastudy only 8% of programs kept an effect after four weeks ([Milkman et al. 2021](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/mys88-cc756)), so the honest claim is follow through within the week, not lasting habit. The biggest gap: **every implementation intention study has the person write the plan**, and nothing tests whether a plan the system suggests works as well.

Evidence that calendaring itself raises completion is weak. A randomized CHI 2023 study of automatically booked focus time found self reported gains for 89 new users ([Das Swain et al. 2023](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=926838)), and 13 studies found that fixing a set time made leisure feel like a chore while rough scheduling removed the penalty ([Tonietto and Malkoc 2016](https://www.business.rutgers.edu/sites/default/files/documents/tonietto-the-calendar-mindset.pdf)). That second finding fits the design: the week acts as the rough window while the slot supplies the specific time. Inside Mondai's own record, a scheduling nudge for about 18,000 online learners slightly lowered persistence (Baker, Evans and Dee 2016, summarised in `reports/Near term goal focus evidence.md:131`), which is the case for keeping the calendar optional.

The one weekly deadline rests on the planning fallacy. Thesis students predicted 33.9 days and took 55.5, and only about 30% finished by their own estimate ([Buehler, Griffin and Ross 1994](https://bear.warrington.ufl.edu/brenner/mar7588/Papers/buehler-et-al-1994.pdf)), so per item deadlines set from optimistic estimates would generate a stream of misses. The often cited finding that evenly spaced external deadlines beat self set ones **failed a 2026 replication** ([Hyndman and Bisin 2026](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/09567976261460772/)) and must not be cited. A field study found long deadlines can lower completion because people defer and forget ([Knowles et al. 2022](https://core.ac.uk/works/27680315)), a risk the suggested slot covers as a reminder inside the week. No study compares one window deadline plus suggested times against per task deadlines, so that comparison is reasoning.

The soft passed time has the cleanest support. Goal setters who violate a goal can perform worse than people who never set one, because the lapse is coded as failure ([Soman and Cheema 2004](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/31/1/52/1797567)). Showing a broken streak lowered later engagement even when actual behavior was identical, more so when people blamed themselves ([Silverman and Barasch 2023](https://udspace.udel.edu/handle/19716/34160)). A single missed opportunity did not materially slow habit formation ([Lally et al. 2010](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2009/aug/how-long-does-it-take-form-habit)). Because Mondai, not the person, chose the time, labelling it missed would turn the product's guess into the person's failure.

"Never move a time" rests on adjacent evidence. People were considerably more likely to use an imperfect algorithm, and did better, when they could modify its output even slightly ([Dietvorst, Simmons and Massey 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v64y2018i3p1155-1170.html)), and mixed initiative design holds that an agent should act on its own only when that clearly beats doing nothing ([Horvitz 1999](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/principles-mixed-initiative-user-interfaces/)). No experiment compares automatic against user controlled rescheduling for adherence or trust. Focus hours deserve modest wording: synchrony effects are real for strong morning or evening types on effortful work ([May, Hasher and Healey 2023](https://blogs.cofc.edu/mayc/files/2023/06/May-Hasher-Healey-2023.pdf)), but a 65 study review found no main effect of chronotype on cognition in over 80% of studies ([Kingston review](https://researchinnovation.kingston.ac.uk/en/publications/chronotype-and-synchrony-effects-in-human-cognitive-performance-a/)). Say it places demanding work where the person says they think best, not that it makes them perform better.

Two internal sources should not be used. The Precious app study on "missed session" language appears in the Mondai archive with no trace in 154 conversations (`calendar-and-schedule.md:204-208`), and the Google Calendar dimming precedent came from assistant turns, not Chadwick.

## Conclusion

For a stranger, the schedule view is easiest to understand as an assistant with no authority: it knows when the person is free, it never knows whether the work got done, and it never pretends otherwise. That framing is stronger than a feature list because each design choice (suggest, never move, drop the passed time, keep the item open) is the same decision seen from a different moment in the week, and because the research supports the parts a hiring manager would question, especially the specific time and the absence of failure states.

The evidence also sets a ceiling on what the block can claim. It can say a specific time turns an intention into a plan and that labelling a lapse as failure costs later effort. It cannot say Mondai's suggestions raise completion, that system written plans work like personal ones or that auto rescheduling would have hurt, because no study and no Mondai data show those things. Writing the block as reasoning grounded in published findings, with a sources line at the bottom and no inline citations per his block rule (`reference/cs2/HANDOFF.md:30`), keeps it honest and still lands the decision.
