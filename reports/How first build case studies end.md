# How first build case studies end, and how to get evidence fast

Research for CS2 (the weekly planning framework). Builds on `reports/Case study results without metrics.md` and `reports/How case studies end.md`; nothing from those is repeated here except where a link is needed. Researched 7 Nov 2026.

**A note on the quotes.** Every quote below came from a page fetched in this session. The fetch tool hands back extracted passages, not raw HTML, so before any line goes into Chadwick's copy, open the page and check the wording by eye. Medium posts (several founding designer MVP studies) returned 403 and were dropped, not cited.

**Sponsored or commercial sources** are marked [commercial]. That covers any page whose publisher sells a course, a critique, recruiting or studio services.

## Headline

Founding designers with no metrics end in five ways, and only two of them hold up. The strong ones (a) state the real status and say the first users are setting the baseline, with a target named, or (b) close on a short reflection that names what to validate next and the one observable test that would prove it. The weak ones close on an NDA plus "let's talk", on colleague testimonials or on funding raised with no line between the design and the money. Hiring guidance agrees: say what actually happened, then say what would need measuring next. For fast evidence the method that fits a weekly planning framework best is a 2 to 3 week diary study on the live build. A weekly plan only shows whether it works across real weeks, and no lab session can show rollover or a start of week state.

## 1. Fourteen opened examples

Ordered from most useful to least for Chadwick's situation.

### 1. Dimitra Chrysikou, RadPass (founding product designer)
- Link: https://dimitra.framer.website/projects/radpass_saas
- Stage: pilot. "In pilot at one radiotherapy department, with early feedback already in."
- Ending ("06 Results"), quoted: "Target: 95%+ compliance, from 70–93% today." / "Anyone can see who is responsible, at any moment." / "Any area's history, without spreadsheets." / "The pilot is setting the first baseline." The page then links to the next case study.
- In place of metrics: a status line, a target set against the current baseline, plain statements of what the system now makes possible and an honest admission that the baseline is still being measured.
- Why it matters for CS2: this is the closest match to his situation. It ends on results that have not happened yet and never pretends otherwise.

### 2. Dimitra Chrysikou, CatchMetrics (founding product designer)
- Link: https://dimitra.framer.website/projects/catchmetrics
- Stage: live ("Live · catchmetrics.io"); designed pre-launch.
- Ending ("Reflection"), quoted: "The hard part: keeping it simple without hiding the depth. Progressive disclosure did most of the work." / "What I'd validate next: where the handoff between non-technical users and developers should sit." / "How I'd measure it: whether a non-technical user can answer 'is my site slow, and where?' without help."
- In place of metrics: three lines. The hardest tension, the open question and one task-based success test anyone could run.
- Why it matters: it is the tightest "measurement plan" ending in the sample, three sentences long, and the success test is phrased as user behaviour, not a KPI.

### 3. Ivy Thong, Aidaly Medicaid onboarding (founding designer)
- Link: https://ivythong.framer.website/Designing-and-innovating-onboarding-for-complex-medicaid-paths
- Stage: shipped in one market. "This is live in our first market in Miami. This model will scale to Phoenix next, whatever their state requirements may be."
- Ending ("Takeaways"), quoted: "It just needs to work." / "As long as we can move folks through the pipeline, I've done my part." Then: "Thanks for reading. If you're interested more about my role and time at Aidaly, reach me at my email."
- In place of metrics: a launch status (first market live) and a scale signal (the model is built to carry over to the next state).
- Why it matters: "built to scale to the next case" is a fair claim for a framework when the framework really was designed to generalise. The takeaway voice is casual and short, which suits Chadwick's register.

### 4. Harshit Singh, Monk CI (founding designer, pre-seed)
- Link: https://madebyhx.framer.website/monk-ci
- Stage: pre-launch ("launch kept slipping").
- Ending ("What would I do differently?"), quoted: "A defensible decision is one whose reasoning is portable: a constraint, a reference, or arithmetic." / "The onboarding flow would have survived if I had defended it that way the first time."
- In place of metrics: reasoning that can be checked. Constraints, references to comparable products, arithmetic shown on the page ("Every figure reconciles, repo and runner spend both sum to $3,628").
- Why it matters: with no outcomes at all, this designer makes rigour the evidence. A weekly planning framework is arithmetic (hours of capacity against hours of curriculum against dates), so the same move is open to CS2: show that the numbers reconcile.

### 5. Ivy Thong, Foodable retro (founding designer)
- Link: https://ivythong.framer.website/A-retro:-Closing-the-gap-between-food-waste-and-food-insecurity
- Stage: sunset, revisited a year later.
- Ending ("Retro"), quoted: "MVP the MVP. Lift small but deliver big." / "Making something complicated is simple, and making something simple is complicated." / "After learning this, I was able to revisit and reimagine a more feasible scope and put my concept to designs."
- In place of metrics: lessons, plus a redesigned smaller scope.
- Why it matters: proof that lessons can carry an ending, but only because the product failed and the lesson changed the design. For a live MVP this framing would undersell the work.

### 6. Ivy Thong, Aidaly health plan partnerships (founding designer)
- Link: https://ivythong.framer.website/Building-solutions-for-health-plan-partnerships
- Stage: concept presented to leadership.
- Ending ("Results"), quoted: "Presented this concept to my CEO. A clear B2B2C model is being discussed by leadership." / "Bias for action. Trust intuition." / "...we hope to see an increase in amount of family caregivers Aidaly can help."
- In place of metrics: leadership buy-in, plus hope.
- Why it matters: a caution. "Being discussed" and "we hope" read as soft. Leadership buy-in is a team signal that Chadwick could not use anyway without naming colleagues.

### 7. Carmen Rincon, Learn.xyz (founding designer 2022 to 2025)
- Link: https://carmen-portfolio-iota.vercel.app/work/learn-xyz
- Stage: funded and scaling ("From zero to Series Seed").
- Ending ("Outcomes" then "Behind the Scenes"), quoted: "$4M raised." / "Udemy noticed." / "The numbers that got investors excited, and the design that made them possible." / "There's more to this story than fits on a page." / "I'd rather walk you through the research, the pivots, and the messy middle over a real conversation."
- In place of metrics: funding, a notable acquirer's interest and anonymous user research quotes ("I forget to open the app after the first week").
- Why it matters: it is a learning product, so it is the nearest domain match. Note that on the live page the metric counters render as "0+, 0%, 0/week" placeholders, which shows how badly an animated stat row fails when it breaks. The user quotes are anonymous research participants, a form Chadwick can use.

### 8. Julian Kraan, Abacum product vision (founding designer)
- Link: https://juliankraan.framer.website/abacum
- Stage: company scaled past the work.
- Ending, quoted: "The vision's impact outlasted my time at Abacum." / "It aligned the organization on a shared direction," / "some of its slides went straight into the Series B investor deck," / "and I still see parts of it shipping today." Then: "My answer became a presentation, Design Visions at Product Companies: How and Why."
- In place of metrics: the work's afterlife. Reused in the fundraise and still shipping.
- Why it matters: "parts of it are shipping" is checkable and needs no named colleague. If parts of the framework end up in other Mondai surfaces, that is the same kind of signal.

### 9. Julian Kraan, Bia (brand and product)
- Link: https://juliankraan.framer.website/bia
- Stage: operating startup.
- Ending, quoted: "Bia has since raised multiple funding rounds and is operating today." / "They served as marketing assets and, more importantly, as research props in conversations with industry experts," / "sparking understanding and excitement."
- In place of metrics: company survival and funding, plus speculative interfaces used as research props.

### 10. Varun T R, Dodo Payments (founding designer)
- Link: https://varuntr.framer.ai/dodo-payments
- Stage: early, used by over 4,000 users in 100+ countries (per the page).
- Ending ("Final Thoughts"), quoted: "Seeing these changes improve conversions and make the product smoother for users has been especially rewarding." / "The pace of change especially with new AI tools has pushed me to keep up skilling and experimenting."
- In place of metrics: one usage scale figure plus unquantified "improve conversions".
- Why it matters: a caution. An unquantified "improved conversions" is the claim reviewers distrust most.

### 11. Karolis Kosas, Cinemaclub (built from zero inside YPlan)
- Link: http://karoliskosas.com/cinemaclub/
- Stage: shipped standalone app.
- Ending, quoted: "Increase of app's MAUs and retention rate" / "Featured on Apple Store and Google Play". The last body line: "Finally, to create a habit of coming back and check what's new this week".
- In place of metrics: store featuring (an external, checkable signal) and unquantified growth.
- Why it matters: store featuring is a real third party signal. "Increase of MAUs" with no figure is a caution, same as Dodo.

### 12. thoughtbot, SplitFit (studio, first version) [commercial]
- Link: https://thoughtbot.com/work/splitfit
- Stage: first version shipped, then grew.
- Ending ("Outcome"), quoted: "The first version of the product was shipped and in the hands of users just two months from the start date of the engagement." / "The initial release was piloted with the CIC" / "This allowed for real testers in a controlled environment." / "The team was able to gather feedback from customers as well as trainers." Outcome card: "Idea to launch in 8 weeks, 1000 monthly paying users in 6 months".
- In place of metrics (for the first version): time to ship, what the first version did and left out ("the buyouts feature came later") and a controlled pilot.
- Why it matters: a studio states the scope of the first version honestly. It says what was in v1 and what came later, which is an ending a framework case study can borrow.

### 13. Harsh Potphode, Bolo AI (founding product designer)
- Link: https://harshpotphode.framer.website/work/how-i-helped-bolo-ai-raise-8m-in-seed
- Ending, quoted: "I do not have the freedom to post anything related to the products as of yet (NDA)." / "To establish credibility for my work, I would appreciate the opportunity to connect with you over a call."
- Why it matters: the anti-pattern. The title claims the fundraise and the body cannot show the link.

### 14. Yaroslav Makovetskyi, CURE (0 to 1 med delivery app, launched in three months)
- Link: https://yaroslavmako.framer.website/cure
- Ending: "Testimonials From Coworkers", then "Hire me". Quoted: "Here are a few kind words people have to say about collaborating and solving problems with me."
- Why it matters: colleague testimonials are the most common stand-in for metrics in this sample, and they are ruled out for Chadwick, since he never names colleagues. Listed so the gap is visible.

### What the fourteen use in place of metrics

| Substitute | Examples | Fit for CS2 |
|---|---|---|
| Status line plus baseline being set, target named | RadPass | Strong, if the MVP is live with users |
| Validate next plus one observable success test | CatchMetrics | Strong |
| Checkable reasoning (constraints, arithmetic) | Monk CI | Strong, the framework is arithmetic |
| First market live, built to scale to the next | Aidaly onboarding | Good if true of the framework |
| What shipped in v1, what was deliberately left for later | SplitFit | Good |
| Work's afterlife (reused, still shipping) | Abacum | Only once it is true |
| Anonymous user research quotes | Learn.xyz | Allowed; participants are not colleagues |
| Funding, acquirer interest, store featuring | Learn.xyz, Bia, Cinemaclub | Weak unless design's part is shown |
| Lessons from failure | Foodable | Wrong frame for a live MVP |
| Leadership buy-in, colleague testimonials, NDA plus call | Aidaly partnerships, CURE, Bolo AI | Ruled out or weak |

Pattern: no example in the sample states a projected impact figure as an outcome. RadPass names a target and labels it a target. The weak endings lean on unquantified verbs ("increase", "improve") or hope ("we hope to see").

## 2. What hiring managers and design leads say

Little of this is specific to 0 to 1 work. The lines below are the ones that are, or that speak to work with no outcome data.

- The Crit (Nikki Kipple, product designer and instructor) [commercial]: "A case study should close the loop, but that does not mean every project needs a conversion lift." / "State what happened: shipped scope, test result, stakeholder decision, operational change, classroom critique" / "If no outcome is available, write what the team learned and what would need to be measured next." / "An honest limit is stronger than borrowed certainty." https://thecrit.co/resources/portfolio-mistakes-cost-interviews
- First Round Review, founding designers: Anthony Short (first designer at Segment) says "design is not held to strong metrics and may want to push for things that can't be measured", and the article notes "With limited metrics to evaluate design against, it's no surprise that senior leadership questions" what design contributes. Katie Witkop (founding designer at Faire): "Moving fast and iterating quickly allowed us to build a company successful enough to hire a team". https://review.firstround.com/the-ultimate-guide-to-the-founding-designer-role/
- Carl Wheatley, X2Talent (recruiter for founding designers) [commercial]: founders should look for "a 0-to-1 product launch story, a research-led pivot," and "at least one metric tied to a shipped decision." Also "Not a gallery of final screens." https://x2talent.substack.com/p/hiring-a-founding-designer-a-practical
- Fountain Institute (Jeff Humble, quoting others) [commercial]: "A beautiful redesign that shipped and moved no metrics is, from a hiring perspective, equivalent to a concept project." Harry Enaholo's screening questions: "are these products live and traceable, and if they are live, are they scaling?" Budi Tanrim's test: "Can you show the line of reasoning, or just the outcome?" https://www.thefountaininstitute.com/blog/senior-product-designers-hired
- UX Playbook (Talia Hartwell) [commercial]: "Senior designers own outcomes, not just outputs." Lists "Pre-launch success metrics you defined" as evidence. https://uxplaybook.org/articles/senior-ux-designer-portfolio-get-hired-2026
- Raika Sarkett (Lead UX and conversation designer, Amazon): "I don't need perfect metrics to show impact." / "My portfolio shouldn't prove I was involved; it should clarify what changed because of my involvement." https://www.greatux.co/p/i-did-this-to-make-my-design-portfolio
- Leslie Yang (design manager, former hiring manager): asks for "project impact and metrics if possible" and closes presentations on "What would you have done differently? What surprised you?" https://leslieyang.substack.com/p/what-a-hiring-manager-looks-for-in

Read together: "live and traceable" plus "pre-launch success metrics you defined" plus "what would need to be measured next" is the bar. Wheatley's "at least one metric tied to a shipped decision" is the one ask CS2 cannot meet today, and it is exactly what the fast evidence section below can supply within weeks. One honest gap: no non-commercial, named senior hiring manager quote about 0 to 1 work without metrics was found on an openable page. The closest (a head of design's Product Coalition post) is on Medium and returned 403.

## 3. Fast evidence: what he can get in weeks, and what each can honestly claim

Founder, team or colleague quotes are excluded by his rule. Anonymous participant quotes are allowed. Every method assumes the MVP build is usable by real people; if it is not live yet, methods A and B can run on a prototype with real curriculum data, but the claim gets weaker (say "prototype" in the copy).

**A. Diary study across 2 to 3 real weeks (best fit).**
NN/g: diary studies capture "user behaviors, activities, and experiences over time and in context", run "ranging from a few days, weeks, to a month or longer", and small discovery studies with a fairly homogeneous group need about 5 to 12 people. NN/g warns that "there is a higher chance that participants will drop out of the study", so recruit extra. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/diary-studies/
Why it fits: rollover, pace dates and start of week states only happen at week boundaries. A diary study is the only cheap method that sees a Monday after a missed week.
Can claim: "Over three weeks, 6 of 8 participants finished their first planned week; 3 rolled work forward and described the next Monday as [their words]." Counts and anonymous quotes. Cannot claim rates, retention or that the framework caused completion.

**B. Moderated sessions with 5 users on the live build.**
NN/g: five participants uncover "the majority of the most common problems" for one user group; qualitative testing gathers "insights, findings, and anecdotes", while quantitative testing "is best for collecting benchmarks." https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-testing-101/ (Reporting rules for small samples are in the earlier report.)
Can claim: problems found and fixed, as "x of 5". Good for setting capacity and reading the plan. Cannot speak to whether plans survive real weeks. Run one session at a simulated start of week, with rolled over work seeded, to cover that state.

**C. A small beta cohort with five or six instrumented events.**
Events that map to the framework: capacity set, week generated, task completed, week closed, work rolled over, pace date moved. Lenny Rachitsky: "A good activation metric is causal for your retention, not just correlative"; a reader with about 300 customers warned "we know there could be a lot of errors in the numbers." https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-determine-your-activation (paywalled after the preview; only the preview was read)
Can claim: what this cohort did, as counts with the cohort size stated ("of the first 40 people, 31 set a capacity"). Cannot claim an activation metric, retention benchmark or causal effect at this size. The RadPass line is the honest frame: the cohort "is setting the first baseline."

**D. A Sean Ellis style "how would you feel" survey, once a cohort exists.**
First Round on Superhuman: ask "How would you feel if you could no longer use Superhuman?"; "Ellis found that the magic number was 40%"; "you start to get directionally correct results around 40 respondents"; survey users who "used the product at least twice in the last two weeks." Superhuman's own first score was 22%. https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit
Can claim: a directional read on the product as a whole, once there are 40 or more qualifying users. It is a product measure, not a framework measure, so it belongs in CS2 only as context. Probably too slow for "within weeks".

**E. A target named against a baseline, before data arrives.**
The RadPass pattern above: name the measure and the target now, and state that the first users are setting the baseline. Can claim: that he defined success before launch, which UX Playbook lists as evidence in its own right ("Pre-launch success metrics you defined"). Cannot claim any result. It costs nothing and it is the hook a later update fills in.

**Order to run them.** B in week 1 (fix what breaks), A across weeks 2 to 4 overlapping C, then update the case study ending with counts from A and C. D only if the cohort grows past about 40 active users.

## 4. Recommended ending structures for CS2

All three keep to the earlier report's limits (one beat, four or fewer ideas, about 30 words at display size at most, one handoff card). Each is built to be updated: a dated status line that gets replaced when evidence lands.

### Pick 1 (recommended): Status, baseline, the one test
Modelled on RadPass, tightened with CatchMetrics. Three parts, about 70 words:
1. Status line, dated: what is live and who is using it (no colleague names).
2. What the framework is built to change, as a target with its measure. For example, the share of planned weeks closed without overload, and how often rolled over work clears the following week. Labelled as a target.
3. One sentence: the first cohort is setting the baseline, and the test that would prove the framework wrong.
Why: it is the only shape in the sample that is honest today and turns into a results ending with no rewrite once diary or cohort counts arrive (swap the target line for "x of n"). It meets "live and traceable" and "pre-launch success metrics you defined", and it hits Wheatley's "metric tied to a shipped decision" the moment counts exist. What would change this pick: if the MVP is not yet in real users' hands, the status line becomes "built, not yet live" and Pick 2 is stronger.

### Pick 2: Reflection in three lines (hard part, validate next, how I'd measure it)
The CatchMetrics shape, word for word in structure. Hard part: for example, making a long curriculum feel finishable without hiding its length. Validate next: whether rollover helps people recover or quietly piles up. How I'd measure it: one behaviour test anyone could run.
Why: the shortest credible ending in the sample, it needs no launch and it shows judgment rather than claiming results. It fits a framework study, where the open question is a real design question. Weaker than Pick 1 once there is a live cohort, because it leaves out the status.

### Pick 3: The numbers reconcile (checkable reasoning)
The Monk CI move. Close on one worked example where capacity in hours, curriculum hours, pace date and target date all add up across a real plan, with a line on the tradeoff he would defend again.
Why: when there is no outcome yet, rigour that a reader can check stands in for it, and a planning framework is arithmetic, so this is evidence of the work itself, not a stand-in. Use it if the body of CS2 has not already shown a full worked week. If it has, this ending repeats the body and should not ship.

Avoid: funding or company milestones as the ending (design's part is not shown), unquantified "improved" verbs, lessons-from-failure framing and any colleague or leadership signal.
