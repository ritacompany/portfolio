# Case study writing protocol

Read this before writing any case study copy for Chadwick.

This sits on top of the tactics banks. A bank tells you what moves exist. This tells you
when to make them, in what order, and what has to be true before you write a sentence.

Written to be read by Claude, not by him.

---

## 0. The premise

A case study is an argument, not a record. The reader is not there to learn what you did.
They are there to decide whether you can be trusted with a decision.

Everything below follows from that.

---

## 1. Settle the frame before drafting

Do not write until all four of these are answered in one line each. If you cannot answer
one, ask him. Do not guess and proceed.

1. **Who reads this and what are they deciding?** Default is a hiring manager deciding
   whether to spend ten more minutes. Not deciding whether you are thorough.
2. **What is this case study arguing?** One sentence, contestable. If a reasonable person
   could not disagree with it, it is a description, not an argument.
3. **What one thing should they be able to repeat afterward?** A person retells one thing.
   Pick it deliberately or the page picks it for you.
4. **What is at stake in the work itself?** If nothing could have gone wrong, there is no
   case study here, only a portfolio image.

Write these four at the top of the working file and leave them there. Every later cut gets
checked against them.

---

## 2. Gather before you draft

Never draft from memory or from a summary. Collect first.

**Ask him for, and wait for:**

- Real artifacts. Messages, comments, screenshots, rejected versions, dated notes.
  Anything with a timestamp or another person's handwriting on it.
- The three to five **decisions**, not the phases. Research, wireframes, testing and
  handoff are phases. "We shipped without the thing everyone asked for" is a decision.
- For each decision: what was at stake, what the real alternative was, why it went the way
  it did, and what it cost. The cost is the part usually missing.
- At least one thing he got wrong, changed his mind about, or would do differently.
- Any constraint that was genuinely binding. Time, headcount, an unpaid team, a fixed
  external date.

**Hard rule from him:** no fabricated metrics and no invented results. If a number does
not exist, the case is made on reasoning and artifacts instead. Do not soften this by
estimating.

If the material is thin, say so and ask for more. Do not compensate with better prose.
Thin material written beautifully is the exact failure he is trying to fix.

---

## 3. Order of work

Argument first, prose last. In this order:

1. The four frame answers
2. The decision list, ordered. Decide the order deliberately, it is a real choice
3. For each decision, the evidence that proves it happened as described
4. Only now, sentences

Skipping to step four is what produces even, competent, forgettable writing.

---

## 4. The passes

Draft once, then pass over it separately. One concern per pass. Do not merge them.

**Pass 1, argument.** Get the reasoning down. Ignore craft entirely. Ugly is fine.

**Pass 2, evidence.** Find every sentence that claims a quality: rigorous, collaborative,
thoughtful, systematic. Delete the adjective and put the object in its place. A real
message, a real rejected version, a real date. If there is no object, the claim does not
survive the pass.

**Pass 3, tactics.** Now open the bank. Place moves deliberately and write down which one
you used and why. Ceiling of roughly one deliberate move per section. More than that and
the page becomes about its own technique.

**Pass 4, rhythm.** Measure, do not eyeball. Count words per sentence across the draft. If
almost everything sits in a narrow band, the page reads as a monotone no matter how good
the thinking is. Break it with short sentences at the points you want remembered. A short
sentence is an emphasis instrument, not a style preference.

**Pass 5, the boring test.** Section by section, ask what a person would repeat after
reading it. If the answer is nothing, that section is not finished. Do not move on because
it is accurate.

---

## 5. Failure modes specific to case studies

Check the draft against all four by name.

**Process recital.** Research, then wireframes, then testing, then final designs. No
decision visible, nothing at stake. The default failure.

**Rationale recital.** Subtler and more common in strong drafts. Every sentence is a claim
plus its justification, all evenly weighted, all equally confident. It reads as competent
and lands as flat, because nothing is allowed to be more important than anything else. The
fix is not better sentences. It is deciding which two moments matter and letting the rest
be quieter.

**Metric theatre.** Numbers that sound like results and prove nothing, or that the work
cannot actually claim. Worse than no numbers.

**The invisible designer.** The system is described, the judgment is not. The reader cannot
tell what he decided versus what was handed to him. Every decision needs a visible author.

---

## 6. Audience mechanics

- Assume a scan before a read. The page has to survive being skimmed at speed and still
  deliver the one repeatable thing.
- Two lengths, both live. The skim path and the full read should both be complete. Do not
  make the skimmer feel like they got the trailer.
- Never explain the significance of something the reader can see for themselves. Showing
  and then explaining cancels the show.

---

## 7. Voice rules, absolute

- No em dashes and no substitutes of any kind. Rewrite the sentence.
- No Oxford commas.
- Sentence case.
- These apply to your own prose as well as anything written for him.

---

## 8. How to work with him

- Lead with the answer. Short and scannable. He will tell you when he wants depth.
- Options are the deliverable when he asks for options, and they have to be genuinely
  different approaches, not one sentence rewritten several ways.
- Never agree reflexively. His words: "the intention is never to PLEASE me. The intention
  is optimal results over that always."
- Do not mistake his example for his subject. When he names one thing, he usually means
  things like it.
- Show him a small piece early so the shape can be corrected cheaply.
- Never state a fact about him you have not checked in the material he gave you.

---

## 9. Before you hand anything back

State plainly which passes you ran and what you could not verify. If the evidence pass left
claims standing with no object behind them, name them rather than quietly keeping them.
