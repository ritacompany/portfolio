## Fixed text in every version

> I studied psychology before I studied design, and the thing that stuck was that a cause and an effect are never a fixed pair. Whether two variables stay connected, flip or quietly stop mattering changes depending on what else is in the room.

> Most of what I know about my own work came from someone else saying it out loud first. So I try to keep a room where no voice feels less than another.

For each version, read its opener, then the locked psychology paragraph, then its people passage, then the locked collaboration paragraph. Version C starts with the psychology paragraph and has no separate opener.

## A. Keep the personal transition, recommended

Base opener:

> In my work, I'm drawn to the problem underneath the problem, the structural part that a surface request is usually pointing to.

Base people passage:

> I'm a verbal processor, so conversation is part of how I think. I try to understand how others work through ideas, then adjust how we work together.

Humanizer pass: Keep the opener. Its slightly unusual repetition is Chadwick's existing voice and carries a distinct idea. Replace the people passage with:

> I'm a verbal processor. Talking through an idea helps me understand it. I try to learn how other people work through ideas and adjust how we work together.

No-ai-slop pass: Keep the opener. It has a specific job after the superpowers and cutting it would remove that transition. Its minimum effective edit to the people passage reaches the same line as the Humanizer pass:

> I'm a verbal processor. Talking through an idea helps me understand it. I try to learn how other people work through ideas and adjust how we work together.

Assessment: Both passes converge because the base only needed a small clarity edit. Neither found a reason to rewrite Chadwick's existing opener.

## B. Make the design behavior explicit

Base opener:

> A request can name the symptom without getting at the problem. I want to know what is causing it before I decide what to design.

Base people passage:

> I think out loud. Other people arrive at ideas differently, so I try to adapt how I work with them.

Humanizer pass: The opener's symptom language sounds clinical and the people passage drops Chadwick's term, verbal processor. Replace them with:

> A request tells me where to start. I want to know what is causing the problem before I design an answer.

> I'm a verbal processor. Talking helps me think, and I try to understand how others reach an idea before deciding how we work together.

No-ai-slop pass: Cut the opener's abstract symptom framing and restore the specific verbal processing detail. Its result is the same as the Humanizer pass for the opener. For the people passage:

> I'm a verbal processor. Talking helps me think, and I try to understand how other people reach an idea so I can adjust how we work together.

Assessment: This direction is clearer about design behavior but sounds more like a general process claim. It loses some of the personality of the current opener.

## C. Start with psychology

Base opener: None.

Base people passage:

> That sensitivity to context is part of how I work with people. I'm a verbal processor, and I try to understand what helps other people work through an idea.

Humanizer pass: The first sentence explains the relationship instead of letting the two paragraphs show it. Cut it. The people passage becomes:

> I'm a verbal processor. I think through conversation, and I try to understand how the people around me work through ideas.

No-ai-slop pass: Cut the same explanatory sentence. Keep the adaptation that the base passage implies:

> I'm a verbal processor. Conversation helps me think, and I try to understand how others work through ideas so I can adjust how we work together.

Assessment: This is the shortest option but the psychology paragraph arrives abruptly after the superpowers. It also leaves the intended design behavior less visible.
