Companion to the reader-effect bank

Where the judgment shows

EXHIBIT 03

Tactics for making a reader believe a design decision was sound, and enjoy watching it get made. Forty-nine of them across eight categories, each with the mechanic, a worked example and the way it fails.

The failure every tactic answers

An About page fails by being generic. A case study fails by being a process recital: research, then wireframes, then testing, then final designs, with no decision visible and nothing at stake.

Every entry answers one of three questions. Where is the judgment? How is it made visible? Why should a stranger believe it happened the way you say?

How the evidence is labelled
  • researcha study I read at abstract level or better, with its sample size where the source gives one
  • secondhandreal research or a real book, but I only saw it through someone else’s summary
  • surveypractitioner data, self reported, not peer reviewed
  • contestedfailed or mixed replication, included as a correction
  • craft lorea practitioner rule with no empirical test behind it
Using it with the protocol

This is the bank for pass 3. One deliberate move per section, written down with the reason. Anti-tactics are for pass 2 and pass 5: read them against a finished draft, not before one.

Where a tactic also appears in the reader-effect bank, it has a different justification here.

What the research does and does not cover

Almost none of the research here was run on hiring managers reading portfolios. It was run on mock jurors, students, negotiators, readers of short stories and online panels. Where a finding is carried into case study writing, the entry says the transfer is an inference. Only one source (entry 35) measured design hiring directly, and it is a self report survey.

Tally for this build: 40 sources below. 21 I read directly at abstract level or better. 16 I saw only through someone else’s summary, quote or table of contents. Those entries are labelled secondhand or lore. 3 are search listings only. Pages that would not load: a portfolio newsletter and Poynter’s nut graf article (both rate limited), the Oppenheimer paper and three PubMed abstracts (security checks) and a Google Books search. For Baron and Hershey, Green and Brock and Jerez-Fernandez I found other copies. Oppenheimer rests on a press summary, and the nut graf entry is labelled lore for that reason. One claim often repeated in writing advice, that concrete wording makes statements seem truer, failed its preregistered replication and is entered as a correction (entry 13).

All examples in this version are checked against the brief: unpaid, part time, rotating volunteers and no outcome metrics yet are the only Mondai facts used.
  4. Greever was cited from two blog summaries only. I have now checked the book’s table of contents. It confirms chapter headings used in entries 22 and 47. The three questions in entry 07 still come from the summaries, and the entry says so.
  5. Fischhoff was attributed without a source read. Now read at summary level, which covers method and the Nixon trip study. The label stays secondhand.
