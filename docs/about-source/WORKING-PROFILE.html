How to read this, and what it cost to say anything

You gave one rule, in a file on your own machine: one instance of him doing something does not make him that thing. It has to establish something about him. That rule is the spine of this document. It is also the reason it is shorter than you might expect.

What earned a place

  • Three or more instances of the same observable behavior.
  • Across two or more independent sources, so a habit specific to one tool or one bad week does not become a trait.
  • Stated as something you did, never as an adjective. "He is rigorous" is not a finding. "He blocks output until the research exists" is.
  • Every quote copied verbatim, typos intact, then checked programmatically against its source line. One hundred and eight were tested. One failed on a typo I had silently repaired, so I restored your typo and it passed.

What was cut

  • Anything true once. There was a lot of it and most of it was flattering.
  • Anything I could say about most senior designers. If it survives a swap onto another person's profile it is not about you.
  • Anything sourced only to what you say about yourself. Self-description is in here, but it is quarantined in §06 and tested against behavior.
  • Interpretation dressed as observation. Where I am inferring rather than counting, it is labeled.

The uncomfortable structural fact. This corpus is you talking to AI assistants. It is a near-complete record of how you reason, argue, concede, specify and lose your temper, and an almost empty record of how you behave with people. Roughly 262 references to teammates by name exist in 302,000 words, nearly all of them about those people rather than to them. So: what follows is high-confidence on your thinking, your standards and your process, and low-confidence on your interpersonal conduct. Do not let the second one be inferred from the first.

04

Your design process, reconstructed from behavior

You said explaining your process would be useful. This is not what you say you do. It is what the record shows you doing, in order, over two years. It is a real methodology and it does not have a name yet.

Stage 1 · Load the state, refuse to start

You open by demanding a full context load and forbidding output. Not a summary of the problem, an audit of everything already decided: I need you to think hard on this. I need you to find any discussion revolving around the scheduled mode view of the action items list HUMAN_CORPUS L18506. In tooling contexts this becomes formal, an inspection-only pass with edits prohibited. The rule underneath is that re-litigating a settled decision is the expensive failure, so the first move is establishing what is already closed.

Stage 2 · Enumerate the space before touching the surface

You build the state space first. I need first to know ALL alt versions of the screen we will need. HUMAN_CORPUS L680. Then content and hierarchy, then layout, and you state the reason in cost terms rather than principle: It's way easier to design if you know WHAT needs to go on each page first... rather than just designing and throwing all the info at something and having to do a million extra iterations HUMAN_CORPUS L680. You are trading a slow start against iteration count, deliberately.

Stage 3 · Establish the evidence floor

Three named categories, requested in the same shape almost every time: what the research says, what comparable products do and what psychology or sociology tells us about the person in this position HUMAN_CORPUS L1105-1107. You do not ask open questions when you want rigor. You specify the shape of the answer in advance, which is why your research asks come back usable.

Stage 4 · Diverge wide, converge alone

Three to fifteen options, in a comparison surface you specify, explicitly not converged. Then a comparative critique rather than an accept or reject: you name what works in each, take a part from one and a part from another, and commission the next round from the pieces. hmm i like different things about each. i think the simplicity of B feels really nice but it isnt clear enough HUMAN_CORPUS L14584. Approval in your record is always a narrowed selection plus an open item. There is no instance of an unqualified yes.

Stage 5 · Stress the seams

P·07. You take the accepted direction and walk it into the rest of the system until something breaks, and when it breaks you go back to Stage 2 rather than patching. stop fixing this at element specific levels this is a large level problem solving conversation. HUMAN_CORPUS L19262.

Stage 6 · Reduce to what ships

MVP is a scope instrument for you, not a stage. You hold the full system and the shippable subset simultaneously and say so: even if we dont use it for the mvp s discussed in that document i am designing it as if they both exist HUMAN_CORPUS L6585. You then defer explicitly and by phase rather than quietly dropping things.

Stage 7 · Convert the decision into an artifact someone else can act on

The work is not finished when the design is finished. It is finished when it is a document a named person can execute from, at that person's depth. what i need is a document to give directly to dev with dev instructions HUMAN_CORPUS L3804. Then the reason goes into the durable record so nobody has to ask twice.

The honest description of this process. It is expensive, front-loaded and slow to converge on purpose. It is built to survive handoff and to be defensible to a stakeholder later, which is exactly the shape you would expect from someone who works with volunteers, part-timers and a CEO who approves everything. It has a known failure mode, named by your own boss and repeated back by you: michael has been pushing the opposite on me. that i dig into things too much and dont let errors just exist for the sake of moving forward. Do not describe the process without describing that tension, because anyone experienced will spot it and you will look like you have not.
